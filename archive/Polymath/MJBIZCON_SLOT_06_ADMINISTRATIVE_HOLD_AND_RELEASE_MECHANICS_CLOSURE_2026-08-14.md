# MJBizCon 2026 Canonical 14 — Slot 06 Administrative-Hold and Release-Mechanics Closure

**Story:** *When a Recall Has to Find the Product*  
**Date:** 14 August 2026 HST  
**Decision:** **PARTIAL CLOSURE, CORRECTED — enacted architecture and one post-enactment extension case established; initial issuance, pre-2025 authority, system mechanics and final implementing rule remain open**

## Bottom line

Nevada now has a statute specifically authorizing cannabis hold orders. Its text is conditional: a Board agent may issue a hold order **only in accordance with regulations adopted by the Cannabis Compliance Board**. Senate Bill 168 became effective upon approval on **6 June 2025** and is codified as NRS 678A.455.

As of 14 August 2026, the current codified NAC chapters do not contain the required hold-order procedure. The Nevada Register contains an **initial agency draft**, R103-26I, submitted 29 April 2026 and posted 4 May 2026, that would add an “Administrative Hold Order” section. The Register does not show a proposed LCB draft, adopted version or Legislative Commission approval for R103-26. The initial draft is not itself an adopted regulation.

That rulemaking status does **not** mean the statute sat unused. Official CCB minutes show the Board applied SB 168's extension procedure on **18 December 2025** in Greenway Medical LLC, Case No. 2025-028 (C018). The Senior Deputy Attorney General told the Board that SB 168 created a procedure allowing a Board agent to issue a product hold and required notice, a hearing for good cause and a Board order to extend it beyond 30 days. After an ALJ proceeding and a stipulated continuance, the Board approved an extension through 17 January 2026. The Board then approved another Greenway extension on its **15 January 2026 consent agenda**. The published minutes do not state the second extension's endpoint or final disposition.

This closes the enacted architecture, formal rulemaking status and one extension-stage application. It also exposes an unresolved legal/procedural tension between the statute's regulation-conditioned issuance language and the Board's documented use before a final implementing regulation appeared in the Register. It does **not** prove the initial Greenway issuance mechanics, the hold mechanism used in the 2023 ethephon matter, any link between Greenway and the 2025 Eagle 20 bulletin, which person changed a seed-to-sale status, or what happened to each affected unit.

## 1. What the enacted statute requires

NRS 678A.455 provides this framework:

1. A Board agent may prohibit transfer or movement only through a hold order issued under Board regulations.
2. The regulations may authorize a hold only when the cannabis or product both fails a Title 56 requirement and constitutes a substantial public-health hazard.
3. The establishment must receive notice, at or before issuance, stating the reasons.
4. A hold terminates automatically within 30 days unless the Board extends it after notice, a hearing and good cause.
5. The regulations must identify termination circumstances, including a passing retest when the hold arose from a failed laboratory test.
6. The Board must publish investigation-based hold-order guidelines.
7. Product subject to an active statutory hold may not be transferred or moved without Board or Board-agent approval.

SB 168 section 18 made the act effective upon passage and approval. The Governor approved it on 6 June 2025. The statute postdates the 2023 ethephon bulletin and cannot be projected backward as the governing procedure for that incident.

## 2. What the unfinished implementing text would add

R103-26I proposes NCCR 4.067, “Administrative Hold Order.” In initial-agency form, it would:

- prohibit movement, transfer or sale without prior written approval;
- require both noncompliance and a substantial public-health hazard;
- list grounds including failed or incomplete testing, inadequate traceability, unapproved additives or contaminants, diversion/inversion and misbranding or adulteration;
- expire after 30 calendar days unless a hearing officer extends it for documented good reason;
- allow removal after risk resolution, passing retest, an unsubstantiated investigation, approved extraction/remediation, or hearing-officer authorization with a seed-to-sale update;
- reserve removal of hold tags, labels or identification to a Board agent; and
- permit destruction of product not brought into compliance.

Before the Register filing, the CCB circulated substantially similar proposed NCCR 4.067 text in its December 2025 proposed changes to NCCRs 1, 4 and 5. That workshop-stage text shows implementing language under development when Greenway was heard. Neither it nor R103-26I is proof that the text had taken legal effect or that Greenway followed every proposed step.

## 3. First recovered operational case: Greenway Medical LLC

The official minutes for the CCB's 18 December 2025 meeting record Agenda Item III, “Consideration of Extending Hold Order for Cannabis and Cannabis Product,” for Greenway Medical LLC, Case No. 2025-028 (C018).

The minutes establish this bounded sequence:

1. A current hold already existed; the minutes do not reproduce its initial order or issuance date.
2. Greenway appeared before Administrative Law Judge Meyer on 9 December 2025.
3. The parties agreed to request a continuance until 17 January 2026 and stipulated to a 30-day extension while reserving rights and remedies.
4. Senior Deputy Attorney General Anthony Garasi advised that his reading of SB 168 required a Board order even with the stipulation.
5. The Board approved the ALJ's recommendation and extended the current hold through 17 January 2026.
6. The 15 January 2026 agenda again listed Greenway under “Consideration of Extending Hold Order,” and the approved minutes show the Board approved that item as part of the consent agenda.

This is strong evidence of an actual, repeatedly extended administrative control, not merely proposed rule text. Neither the January agenda nor the approved minutes state the new termination date. Greenway does not appear as a hold item on the published February 19 or March 19 agendas, but absence from those agendas does not prove release, expiration, remediation or destruction. The record remains insufficient to state what initiated the hold, which products or package IDs it covered, whether it was connected to Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2025-02, how Metrc represented it, or what physical disposition followed. No bulletin connection is asserted without an identifier-level or case-document match.

## 4. Current adoption status and unresolved tension

The current NAC pages are revised through April 2026 and contain no hold-order section implementing NRS 678A.455. The 2026 Nevada Register lists R103-26I as an initial agency draft; it does not list R103-26 as proposed, adopted or approved. R104-26 is a separate sampling/testing rulemaking.

The defensible status sentence is:

> Nevada enacted a cannabis hold-order statute in June 2025 and the CCB used its extension procedure in a December 2025 Greenway case; as of 14 August 2026, however, the detailed implementing procedure appears only in workshop and initial-agency draft text and is not present in the current codified NAC.

This is not a conclusion that the Greenway action was valid or invalid and not a legal opinion about every other power the Board may possess. The apparent tension is a reporting question requiring the initial order, briefing or hearing record and Nevada administrative-law review.

## 5. Technology and authority must remain separate

A status, flag, tag or transfer restriction in Metrc or another seed-to-sale interface is not automatically the same thing as a statutory hold order. The recovered record does not establish:

- the exact software object or status used;
- which role can create, edit, extend or remove it;
- whether a software restriction is automatic or manual;
- whether a software “hold” maps one-to-one to NRS 678A.455;
- what notice is generated, to whom and how receipt is recorded; or
- how release, remediation, return, destruction or unresolved inventory is reconciled.

The article must keep four layers distinct: legal authority, agency order, seed-to-sale implementation and physical inventory disposition.

## 5A. Pre-6 June 2025 authority scan — narrowed, not closed

The official 2023 ethephon materials do not establish a product hold. Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2023-01 identifies the source facility, affected harvest window, downstream item/production-run records, retailer endpoints and consumer warning, and its 9 February update expands that public scope. Neither the initial nor updated public bulletin states that the affected inventory was placed under an administrative hold, identifies an order, cites hold authority, names the person who imposed or released a transfer restriction, or records disposition. The bulletin therefore proves public warning and reverse-traceability outputs, not the legal or software mechanism used to immobilize unsold inventory.

The CCB's 24 January 2023 emergency-meeting minutes do identify an express pre-SB 168 emergency authority, but for a different control and a different licensee. In Helping Hands Wellness Center, Inc., the Board proceeded under NCCR 4.105 to summarily suspend cultivation and production licenses after evidence concerning concealment, untracked cannabis and security failures. The minutes describe destruction of unaccounted cannabis in agents' presence and a corrective-action route to reinstatement. They do not concern Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions, ethephon, Bulletin 2023-01 or a product-level administrative hold. A license summary suspension under NCCR 4.105 cannot be substituted for the missing authority for product holds.

The official 5 March 2025 Senate Committee on Commerce and Labor minutes now establish the pre-SB 168 operating description in primary legislative history. Senator Rochelle Nguyen described an administrative hold as the CCB freezing product in the seed-to-sale tracking system so it could not be sold or transferred, requiring product to be removed from shelves, online orders and delivery trucks. She distinguished a hold from a recall: a hold was a pause during investigation. She also testified that holds could last “many, many months,” that some were released after the investigation found no safety issue, and that product sometimes expired while immobilized. Will Adler supplied a concrete example: product placed on hold in Carson City in the first quarter of 2020 was not resolved until the first quarter of 2021; he asked to destroy it because much had expired and was physically taped to vault shelving. His testimony was that no response deadline then applied.

That official record materially closes the existence-and-practice question, but not the authority question. It proves that industry participants and the bill sponsor described a pre-2025 CCB practice involving seed-to-sale freezing, physical segregation, investigative release and possible destruction after long delay. The minutes do not identify the product, licensee, hold instrument, initiating official, statute, regulation, directive, audit history, release order or final disposition for the 2020–2021 example. Legislative testimony is evidence of the described practice and the problem SB 168 sought to correct; it is not a substitute for the missing underlying agency record or legal authority.

The same official minutes identify Adler as appearing for **Green Thumb Industries**, and his first-person account says he asked the CCB to allow “us” to destroy the expired product. This strengthens the operator-level attribution behind the Carson City example and makes a GTI-controlled Carson City facility the most specific public-record lead recovered so far. It still does not identify the Nevada license, retail location, product, package or batch; establish that GTI Nevada LLC dba Rise at 135 Clearview Drive held the inventory; or prove that the Cannex hold sequence was the same event. The safe formulation is therefore **GTI-affiliated witness describing a Carson City hold affecting his organization**, not **confirmed GTI/Rise Cannex inventory**.

Local reporting independently narrows the Carson City operator context without closing the incident match. A May 2020 report placed Adler at the operating Rise Cannabis location in Carson City, while 2023 reporting identified Rise and Sierra Well/Zen Leaf as Carson City's then-current GTI-controlled retailers and described Adler as appearing for GTI. This makes the retail footprint a rational records-search target, but it remains secondary-source corporate/location corroboration only. It does not establish which licensed premises stored the held product, whether the inventory was retail or upstream, or whether the Cannex products were the inventory Adler described.

The CCB's current official license search now supplies exact establishment identifiers for the GTI-controlled Carson City record universe: **D099** (GTI Nevada LLC dba RISE, cannabis dispensary), **C088** (GTI Nevada LLC dba Rise Dispensary, cannabis cultivation) and **P057** (GTI Nevada LLC dba Rise Dispensary, cannabis production). This is a material retrieval improvement because the Adler example need not be assumed to involve retail inventory; hold notices, Metrc audit events, correspondence, release instructions and destruction records can be searched across all three establishment classes. The indexed CCB results recovered in this pass do not connect D099, C088 or P057 to Cannex, Advisory 2020-05, Adler's taped-vault account, a 2021 release or a destruction decision. These IDs are search keys, not incident identifiers.

GTI's contemporaneous SEC filings add five historical Nevada license numbers that can be used as exact 2020-era retrieval keys: **18900369179730863251** (medicinal dispensary), **83887504703736981918** (medicinal cultivator), **17355802525954961447** (adult-use cultivator), **88939271215332828859** (medicinal processor) and **69272354565432352821** (adult-use processor). The filings confirm that GTI's Carson City licensed universe included retail plus upstream cultivation and processing during the relevant period. They do not identify which license held Adler's taped-vault inventory, establish a Cannex link or prove release or destruction. Any relationship between these legacy numbers and today's D099/C088/P057 identifiers remains a category-and-entity inference pending agency confirmation; it must not be represented as a verified one-to-one lineage.

One official incident is a plausible but unconfirmed match. Nevada Department of Taxation Public Health and Safety Advisory 2020-05, issued 21 February 2020 during the quarter Adler identified, expanded the Cannex Nevada microbial-testing investigation and listed GTI Nevada LLC dba Rise at 135 Clearview Drive in Carson City among 30 retail endpoints. The advisory identified affected batches, failed secondary microbial results, a sales window and six source cultivators. It did not state that GTI inventory was placed on administrative hold, identify taped vault inventory, describe a release in 2021, authorize destruction or name Adler. The temporal, geographic and operator overlap justifies targeted retrieval; it does not justify saying the hearing example was the Cannex incident.

The CCB's indexed disciplinary complaint in *Cannex Nevada, LLC, Case No. 2020-27* now supplies a stronger incident-level mechanics record. It alleges that Department inspectors compiled a “potential retest list” of samples that initially failed microbial testing but Cannex later reported as passing; on 19 December 2019 the Department initiated administrative holds for **all products on that list**; three independent laboratories received an initial divided sample list on 17 December; the Department assigned three laboratories to retest the remaining products on 25 January 2020; and results from that second round reached the Department on 19 February, preceding Advisory 2020-05 on 21 February. This directly documents list-based product immobilization coupled to staged secondary testing before SB 168. It also shifts the initiating date for this Cannex hold sequence to 19 December 2019, not the first quarter of 2020.

That pleading still does not close the Adler match. Searches of its text returned no GTI, Rise or Carson City identifier, and the complaint does not identify which listed product was physically segregated at GTI's facility, a first-quarter-2021 release, Adler's destruction request or the final disposition of the Carson City inventory. The complaint therefore strengthens Cannex as a candidate incident family and provides a real pre-2025 hold/retest sequence, while simultaneously preventing the February advisory date from being treated as the hold's start date. The specific Cannex/GTI/Adler linkage remains unproved.

The Board's 4 March 2024 final order closes the laboratory disciplinary case but **does not close the held-product disposition**. It finds that Cannex records contained 232 samples initially failing microbial testing and retested at least once, including 43 Aspergillus results alleged as falsely reported as passing, and identifies three clients examined in the record: THC Nevada, Integral Cultivation and Nevada Group Wellness. A footnote states that, because Cannex-reported passing product may actually have failed microbial testing, the Department had other laboratories retest products; when some secondary tests failed, it suspended Cannex testing and issued public advisories. Critically, the order says those other-laboratory results were not the basis of the complaint and were not at issue in the adjudication. The order contains no administrative-hold finding, release instruction, destruction authorization, held quantity or GTI/Rise/Carson City linkage. Therefore the 2024 final disposition date may be used for the lab discipline timeline only; it may not be represented as the release or destruction date for product placed on hold in December 2019.

The advisory-to-order entity reconciliation is now exact but still bounded. Advisory 2020-05 names **all three** clients examined in the final order—Integral Cultivation LLC, THC Nevada LLC and Nevada Group Wellness LLC—among its six source cultivators. The same advisory separately lists GTI Nevada LLC dba Rise at 135 Clearview Drive in Carson City as one of 30 retail endpoints and says the affected flower and pre-rolls were believed sold between 25 October 2019 and 16 January 2020. This establishes that the final order's three examined clients sit inside the advisory's affected cultivator set; it does **not** assign any specific product, batch or cultivator to the GTI Carson City store, prove that GTI held the product Adler described, or establish the hold's release or destruction. The bounded chain is therefore: Cannex investigation -> three examined clients also named as advisory source cultivators -> advisory includes GTI/Rise Carson City as a retail endpoint. The missing batch-to-retailer and hold-disposition links remain decisive.

The Department's 24 January 2020 *Stipulation and Order Regarding Summary Suspension* resolves a separate timeline ambiguity. It required Cannex to revise microbial method verifications and SOPs, train employees, document environmental controls, and cease unauthorized microbial retesting. On approval, the Division agreed to lift the laboratory's summary suspension for testing **other than microbials**, restore corresponding Metrc access, and lift the remaining suspension only after reinspection and confirmed compliance. This order governs Cannex's laboratory testing privileges; it contains no release, destruction or disposition instruction for cannabis products placed on administrative hold. The partial laboratory reinstatement therefore must not be represented as a product-hold release event.

**Bounded conclusion:** the public record now proves a pre-2025 CCB administrative-hold practice at the level of official legislative testimony: seed-to-sale freezing, no sale or transfer, physical segregation, investigation, release and possible destruction after delay. It still does not show which statute, regulation, directive or system permission authorized that practice generally or a product-level hold in the 2023 ethephon response. The pre-2025 existence-and-mechanics sub-gate is substantially narrowed; the authority, incident-record and disposition gates remain open.

## 6. Claims now supported

- Nevada enacted a cannabis-specific hold-order framework effective 6 June 2025.
- The statute conditions a hold on regulatory noncompliance plus a substantial public-health hazard.
- Notice, a 30-day endpoint, hearing/good-cause extension, retest-based termination and guidelines are mandatory subjects for implementing regulations.
- On 18 December 2025, the CCB approved an ALJ recommendation extending an existing Greenway hold through 17 January 2026 after agency counsel described SB 168 as requiring a Board order.
- On 15 January 2026, the CCB approved another Greenway hold extension on its consent agenda; the published minutes do not provide the new endpoint.
- Substantially similar implementing text was in a December 2025 CCB workshop proposal before the April 2026 Register filing.
- R103-26I proposes operational grounds, extension and release routes, tag control and possible destruction.
- As of 14 August 2026, R103-26 is not shown as proposed, adopted or approved and is absent from the current codified NAC.
- Official 5 March 2025 committee minutes document pre-SB 168 CCB hold practice as a seed-to-sale freeze barring sale or transfer, distinguish holds from recalls, and record testimony about a 2020–2021 hold that outlasted product shelf life.
- Those minutes identify the witness as Will Adler of Green Thumb Industries and record his first-person request that the CCB allow “us” to destroy the expired product; this strengthens operator attribution without identifying the licensed facility, product or incident family.
- Local reporting independently places Adler with GTI's Carson City retail operations and identifies Rise plus Sierra Well/Zen Leaf as the city's GTI-controlled retail footprint; this narrows records-search targets but does not identify the held premises or inventory.
- The CCB's official license search identifies GTI Nevada's Carson City establishment universe as D099 (dispensary), C088 (cultivation) and P057 (production); these are precise retrieval keys, not proof that any one establishment or the Cannex incident was Adler's example.
- GTI's contemporaneous SEC filings supply five exact historical license-number keys spanning medicinal dispensary, medicinal/adult-use cultivation and medicinal/adult-use processing; they improve records retrieval but do not identify the held license or verify a legacy-to-current ID mapping.
- The indexed Cannex disciplinary complaint documents a pre-SB 168 administrative-hold sequence beginning 19 December 2019 for every product on a potential-retest list, followed by staged independent secondary testing through February 2020.
- The 4 March 2024 Cannex final order confirms the retesting investigation's scale (232 initially failing microbial samples retested at least once), identifies three examined clients and expressly excludes the independent secondary-test results from the adjudicated issues; it does not resolve the held products' release, destruction, quantity or Carson City identity.
- Advisory 2020-05 independently names all three final-order clients among its six source cultivators and separately names GTI/Rise Carson City among 30 retail endpoints; this is an entity-level reconciliation, not a product/batch-to-store or hold-disposition linkage.
- The 24 January 2020 Cannex stipulation partially restored non-microbial laboratory testing and set conditions for full laboratory reinstatement; it did not release or dispose of held cannabis inventory.

## Editorial decision

**Retain Slot 06; keep canonical drafting blocked.** The public record now captures both law being translated into a proposed operational rule and one real extension-stage application. The accurate story is neither that Nevada already has a settled hold workflow nor that SB 168 was wholly non-operative pending final regulations. It is that the Legislature specified minimum safeguards, the Board used the extension architecture in Greenway while detailed rulemaking was unfinished, and the initial-order, software and physical-disposition chain remains unavailable.
