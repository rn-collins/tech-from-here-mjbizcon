## Secondhand / paraphrased evidence

- Nevada's current consumer page tells purchasers that licensed products are tested and that consumers may request COAs. This establishes the interface presented to the public, not whether people use or trust it. [Nevada CCB consumer page](https://ccb.nv.gov/consumers/), accessed 14 August 2026.
- Current Nevada regulation requires a sales facility, upon consumer request, to disclose the testing laboratory and corresponding COA. [NAC 678B.315](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-678B.html), accessed 14 August 2026.
- The CCB's public item catalog now exposes downloadable COAs for listed products. [Nevada Cannabis Item Catalog](https://ccb.nv.gov/cannabis-item-catalog/), accessed 14 August 2026.
- A 2021 peer-reviewed study found an unusual frequency of reported flower potency just above 20% in Nevada and Washington, concentrated among some laboratories; it explicitly described the result as suggestive rather than direct proof of manipulation. [Zoorob 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7958443/).
- Nevada reporting documents disciplinary matters involving elevated THC results and laboratory misconduct allegations or findings. Each case must retain its own procedural posture; allegations, settlement terms and adjudicated findings may not be blended. [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/tax-commission-approves-settlements-with-two-marijuana-companies-including-lab-accused-of-reporting-inflated-thc-levels); [Cannabis Business Times](https://www.cannabisbusinesstimes.com/business-issues-benchmarks/compliance/news/15688278/nevada-cannabis-lab-faces-10-year-ban-for-manipulating-test-results).
