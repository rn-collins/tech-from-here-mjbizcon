# MJBizCon Challenger — Child-Resistant-Package Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Candidate title:** *A Child-Resistant Package Is a Mechanical Claim About a Household*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS AN EXCEPTIONALLY TANGIBLE TECHNOLOGY, FAMILY-SAFETY, ACCESSIBILITY AND LABOR STORY. IT BECOMES A TOP SLOT 13 CHALLENGER, BUT PROMOTION IS BLOCKED UNTIL ONE ACTUAL NEVADA PACKAGE, ITS TEST REPORT, APPROVAL RECORD AND REPEATED-USE WORKFLOW CORRESPOND.**

## Controlling finding

Nevada requires cannabis sold at a sales facility to be placed into or directly packaged in opaque, child-resistant packaging that complies with 16 C.F.R. Part 1700. NAC 678B.596 further requires the child-resistant package to maintain its effectiveness for multiple openings before it leaves with the consumer. The CCB says packaging must be pre-approved and that only a licensed establishment or its designee may request approval.

The referenced federal standard makes “child-resistant” a measured human-use claim. Under 16 C.F.R. § 1700.20, the child protocol begins with 50 children aged 42–51 months and can expand sequentially to 200. Children work in pairs for five minutes; if they have not opened the package, an adult demonstrates normal opening without verbal instruction, after which they receive five more minutes and may be told they can use their teeth. The CPSC summarizes the performance thresholds as at least 85 percent not opening in the first five minutes and at least 80 percent not opening in the full ten minutes. Adult usability is tested with 100 adults aged 50–70; at least 90 percent must open within five minutes and, where applicable, open and properly resecure an identical package within one minute.

This is not proof that a package is “childproof.” It is not proof that a particular cannabis-filled retail unit matches the tested construction, was closed correctly, remained effective after real repeated use, accommodates every disabled or older user, prevents access by older children, or substitutes for locked and out-of-sight storage. Nevada separately requires retailers to offer locking storage containers, and CCB consumer guidance recommends both child-resistant packaging and a locked cabinet or box.

## Strong proposition

**A Child-Resistant Cannabis Package Is a Tested Negotiation Between a Young Child and an Older Hand.**

Sharper alternate: **Nevada Calls This Package Child-Resistant. The Claim Begins With a Stopwatch.**

The inherited title is attractive but “about a household” is interpretive. Use it only if the article distinguishes the standardized test room from uncontrolled household life.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The Nevada-specific object is the package in which a licensed Las Vegas sales facility hands cannabis to a consumer. It sits at the intersection of a visitor economy, edible products that may resemble ordinary food, state opacity and anti-child-appeal rules, federal package-performance protocols, retailer closure practice, adult dexterity and household storage.

The relevant place is not a generic packaging factory. It is the chain linking a Nevada production facility or packaging supplier, CCB pre-approval, a Las Vegas dispensary counter and the place where the consumer later opens, recloses and stores the package. The story must follow one approved package through that chain.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Investigate the complete package system:

- push-and-turn, squeeze-and-turn, align-and-lift, slider, zipper, locking-tab, blister or other closure geometry;
- cap, liner, threads, detents, torque, seal, pouch film, track and tactile/visual opening instructions;
- reclosable versus unit-dose architecture;
- package-conditioning, fill/product compatibility and repeated-opening assumptions;
- child-panel and senior-adult-panel protocol, stopwatch, demonstration package and failure recording;
- production-line closure application, torque or seal verification and change control;
- supplier protocol report, package drawing/specification and material/version identity;
- Nevada licensed-establishment submission, CCB pre-approval, receiving inspection and release/rejection records;
- retail insertion or direct packaging, correct closure, consumer instruction and optional locking storage container;
- accessibility conflicts for arthritis, low grip strength, vision, cognition or one-handed use.

The central invention is not merely a cap. It is a performance-certified closure plus manufacturing, approval and human-use system that must make access significantly difficult for young children while remaining usable by older adults.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- The federal Poison Prevention Packaging Act dates to 1970; the current protocol embodies the later senior-friendly test architecture.
- Nevada's medical and adult-use cannabis rules imported child-resistant packaging requirements before consolidation into the present cannabis code.
- Current NAC 678B.596 links Nevada retail packaging to 16 C.F.R. Part 1700 and explicitly requires effectiveness across multiple openings.
- Current CCB FAQ guidance says packaging is pre-approved and requests come from licensees or designees.
- Current Nevada law and regulation separately govern opacity, warnings, serving demarcation, resealable liquid caps, food-grade materials and child-directed imagery. These features may coexist with child resistance but are not interchangeable with it.

The package lifecycle to recover is: select design → protocol-test defined package/version → obtain technical report → submit Nevada approval material → approve/reject → procure unchanged components → package/fill/seal → inspect/release → place product into compliant retail package if needed → worker closes correctly → consumer opens/recloses → package and product are stored in a locked, inaccessible place → complaint/failure/change triggers investigation or redesign.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The ecosystem includes children and caregivers; older and disabled adults; packaging designers, polymer/material suppliers, closure manufacturers and testing laboratories; cannabis producers, packaging-line operators and quality staff; licensed-establishment applicants; CCB packaging approvers and inspectors; dispensary workers; poison-control and public-health staff; waste/recycling workers; and consumers who must operate and resecure the closure.

The present consequences include accidental-ingestion risk, adult access, compliance cost, packaging waste, worker technique, product freshness, portability and storage behavior. None of those outcomes should be inferred from a compliant design alone.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** a child-resistant/senior-friendly package-performance system coupling closure mechanics, standardized human testing, manufacturing control, Nevada approval, retail closure, repeated consumer use and safe-storage instructions.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** one CCB-approved Nevada package identified to manufacturer, model, size, materials, closure and version, with its corresponding 16 C.F.R. § 1700.20 protocol report and a permissioned empty/surrogate-filled specimen. Never place cannabis in a child test or reenact the regulated protocol with children.
3. **People and labor:** child- and adult-panel participants and test administrators; designers, toolmakers, factory and quality workers; cannabis packaging operators; CCB staff; dispensary workers; caregivers and consumers—including people for whom the closure is difficult.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada incorporated Part 1700 into its cannabis retail package rule, added opacity and cannabis-specific presentation controls, requires multiple-opening effectiveness, pre-approves packaging and requires sales facilities to offer child-locking storage containers.
5. **Present-day consequence or continuation:** the mechanism is touched at every Nevada retail handoff, then succeeds or fails through correct closure, repeated operation and household storage; the package claim shapes both product access and accessibility.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** child-resistant does not mean childproof; opaque does not mean child-resistant; a warning or universal symbol does not create mechanical resistance; an approved drawing does not prove every production unit conforms; one protocol report does not prove a materially changed package; compliance does not prove fewer poisonings without outcome evidence; difficult adult opening is not proof of greater child resistance; packaging alone does not replace locked storage or supervision; cannabis packaging's resemblance rules do not prove a causal reduction in ingestion.
