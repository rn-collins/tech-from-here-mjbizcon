# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Slot 13 Alternate-Incident Test and Reopen Decision

**Decision date:** 13 August 2026  
**Incidents tested:** CCB Bulletins 2025-01 and 2023-03  
**Decision:** **MERGE SAMPLE-CORRESPONDENCE MATERIAL INTO SLOT 4; REOPEN SLOT 13**

## Executive decision

The September 2025 sample-correspondence incident cannot remain a standalone Canonical 14 seat on the public evidence now available. A second, unusually well-explained Nevada incident—Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2023-03—was tested as a possible replacement case. It proves a reagent-related false-result mechanism, a retest, a positive Aspergillus fumigatus result, laboratory notification, a named product/lot, a retailer and a sale window. But the public Laboratory Library still does not provide the source-package/lot lineage needed to match the named lot to exact initial and retest sample records, and its current Metrc snapshot does not preserve both historical result states.

That repeated structural failure triggers the automatic boundary rule. Preserve both incidents as concrete case studies inside Slot 4, **A “Pass” Is the End of a Measurement Chain, Not a Fact of Nature**. Vacate and reopen Slot 13 for a genuinely distinct proposition with a stronger public evidence chain.

## Alternate incident tested: Bulletin 2023-03

The Nevada CCB stated on 23 June 2023 that:

- `Phantom Farms - 1/2 oz S.B, Dancing Monkey`, lot `2105 6926 0793 4799`, initially passed testing;
- Certified Ag Lab learned that its testing reagents might have caused a false result;
- after retesting, the lot was positive for Aspergillus fumigatus;
- Certified Ag Lab notified the CCB;
- the lot had been sold at Silver State Relief in Fernley between 9 and 21 May 2023;
- the Board had no reason to believe the laboratory knew of the positive result when it reported the negative result, and no reason to believe the retailer knew; and
- there were no known reports of illness at the time of the bulletin.

Primary source: <https://ccb.nv.gov/ccb-issues-public-health-and-safety-bulletin-2023-03/>

This is a stronger mechanism record than Bulletin 2025-01 because it identifies the technological failure mode at a bounded level—reagents may have produced a false result—and the laboratory's corrective action. It does not identify the reagent, lot number, assay, validation defect, date of discovery, precise initial/retest sample identifiers, COAs or detailed corrective-action procedure.

## Historical-data test

The CCB's May and June 2023 Laboratory Library files were searched using the corrected schema established in the first reconstruction. Commercial and corporate records identify Phantom Farms as a brand used by the Silver State operation; that secondary mapping was used only to identify a candidate facility, not to prove test correspondence.

The archive contains Certified Ag Lab records for Silver State Cultivation LLC and contains failed Aspergillus results near the bulletin period, including June 2023 sample records. However:

1. the CSV identifies laboratory sample tags, not the named consumer lot;
2. it does not publish source-package tags, product/strain names or parent/child lineage;
3. the bulletin lot number is not a Metrc package identifier and does not appear in the dataset;
4. the current export is a snapshot, so a corrected/retested state can overwrite or supersede the earlier state; and
5. no paired initial and retest COAs were recovered publicly.

No recovered sample can therefore be labeled the Dancing Monkey retest without unsupported inference.

Both cases are culturally legible examples of why a laboratory “pass” is conditional. That is precisely Slot 4's proposition. Without exact correspondence records, a separate story would repeat Slot 4 while offering less demonstrable mechanism.

## Merge instructions for Slot 4

Preserve the incidents as two sharply separated examples:

1. **2023 — analytical-system correction:** a laboratory learned its reagents might have produced a false result, retested, found Aspergillus fumigatus and notified the regulator.
2. **2025 — pre-analytical correspondence failure:** the CCB said products were misrepresented during sampling; initial results passed, secondary tests failed for microbials, and some lots were unavailable.

Use the contrast to show that a result can fail at different parts of the measurement chain. Do not imply that the 2023 and 2025 mechanisms, actors or legal statuses were the same. Do not identify exact sample records until correspondence is independently proved.

The best real-image carousel pages from these records move with the case studies into Slot 4: bulletin excerpt, real sample tag/data row, assay/reagent documentation when recovered, selected 2025 package identifiers, chain-of-custody form, and a diagram based only on verified stages. Audio adaptations must preserve all qualifications and pronounce Aspergillus fumigatus accurately.

## Reopened Slot 13

Slot 13 is vacant. The first candidate advanced for the next broad technology-discovery audit is:

**The Extract Output Was Larger Than the Input**

The official 2 November 2023 CCB record states that, while investigating suspicious Metrc activity and insufficient camera coverage at The Hempire Company in Tonopah, agents found cannabis extract outputs exceeding the cannabis utilized; determined failed or untested cannabis may have been combined with passing lots for extraction; found approximately 180 cultivation packages and 10 production packages missing; found two bags of usable cannabis in a dumpster; and observed drying without environmental controls. The Board summarily suspended cultivation and production licenses, citing a present threat to public health and safety.

Primary source: <https://ccb.nv.gov/https-ccbnvstaging-wpengine-com-wp-content-uploads-2023-11-ccb-issues-summary-suspension-of-tonopah-cultivation-production-licenses-11-2-23for-realease-1-pdf/>

This is not yet promoted. It must receive the full four-lens discovery pass and explicitly identify:

- the mass-balance and inventory-reconciliation system;
- tangible inputs, extracts, package tags, scales, extraction equipment, cameras and environmental controls;
- cultivation, production, inventory, extraction, security and regulatory labor;
- Tonopah's specific facility and geographic connection;
- present-day consequence or continuation;
- what the summary-suspension record proves versus alleges or leaves unresolved; and
- overlap boundaries with Slot 2's general data double, Slot 5's adjudicated laboratory conduct and Slot 6's recall tracing.

Promotion requires the suspension order, complaint/disposition/current license status, recoverable Metrc or mass-balance exhibits, technical extraction context and a visually meaningful real-evidence package. If those records do not exist publicly, the candidate fails without outreach.

Nothing in this decision authorizes publication, scheduling, sending, purchasing, outreach, interviews, public-record requests, facility access or public uploading.
