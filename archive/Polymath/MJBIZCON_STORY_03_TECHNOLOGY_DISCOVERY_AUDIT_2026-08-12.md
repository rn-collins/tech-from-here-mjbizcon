# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Story 3 Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026 HST  
**Story examined:** `Nevada Used the Shape of the Data to Police Potency`  
**Governing method:** Canonical 14 v2.1 four-lens technology-discovery and causal-classification gate  
**Evidence examined:** Story 3 claim ledger, evidence-closure record, structured outline, Nevada CCB 2025 Biennial Report, current CCB laboratory/testing and public-record materials  
**Publication authority:** None. Keep unpublished, unscheduled, unsent, and unuploaded to public destinations.

## Decision

**Retain Story 3 provisionally as a distinct technology story, but keep drafting blocked until the regulator's data pipeline, review protocol, and enforcement correspondence are better documented.**

The broad pass confirms a concrete Nevada sociotechnical system rather than an abstract story about statistics:

> regulated sample → laboratory measurement → reported potency result → aggregate dataset → distribution visualization → anomaly or threshold review → inspector/investigator examination → case-level evidence and response → continued monitoring

The central technology is not the histogram alone. It is Nevada's potency-surveillance and enforcement-screening system: laboratory instruments and reporting produce standardized results; state staff aggregate and visualize those results; a distributional pattern and a result above a stated threshold can direct human review; inspectors and investigators then require evidence beyond the chart.

The existing title and methods-and-uncertainty proposition remain viable. The package must nevertheless be rebuilt around the complete human-and-technical chain. It cannot imply that a chart detected fraud autonomously, that 30 percent is a biological ceiling, or that the change between displayed distributions proves enforcement caused the change.

## Strong revised proposition

**Nevada Turned the Shape of Potency Data Into an Enforcement Lead—Not a Verdict.**

### Headline options for later testing

1. **Nevada Used the Shape of the Data to Police Potency**
2. **The Histogram That Told Nevada Where to Look**
3. **When Cannabis Potency Data Becomes an Enforcement Instrument**

### Unsafe framing

- **“Nevada Proved THC Inflation With a Histogram.”** The public chart does not establish case-level falsification, intent, or causation.
- **“Cannabis Above 30 Percent THC Is Fake.”** The CCB describes a review trigger, not a biological maximum or automatic violation.
- **“The Algorithm Caught the Labs.”** No public evidence presently establishes an algorithm, model, automated alert, codebase, or autonomous decision.
- **“Enforcement Fixed the Curve.”** The regulator reports that suspected inflation was curbed; the displayed before/after distributions do not independently establish that causal claim.
- **“Nevada Invented Potency Surveillance.”** Unsupported without historical and interstate comparison.

## Four-lens discovery record

### 1. Place + technology

Nevada-specific systems tested:

- the CCB's statewide oversight of Nevada independent testing laboratories;
- the regulator's reported investigation into THC-potency practices across all Nevada laboratories;
- paired 2020 and 2023 potency histograms;
- the stated review trigger for flower results above 30 percent;
- routine laboratory inspections covering methods, materials, sanitary practices, and reported data;
- the relationship among certificates of analysis, Metrc records, raw analytical data, retesting, remediation, inspection, and discipline;
- current public product/COA and historical laboratory-data interfaces as downstream visibility layers.

**Finding:** Nevada's distinctive subject is the conversion of statewide regulated laboratory results into an enforcement-screening instrument. The place connection is institutional and operational: Nevada's CCB describes using the state's own regulated-market dataset and laboratory-inspection workforce to decide where closer review was warranted.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Relevant institutions and technical roles:

- cultivators and production facilities create lots and production runs;
- authorized samplers collect regulated samples;
- independent testing laboratories prepare samples, operate analytical instruments, validate methods, review outputs, and report results;
- laboratory information and seed-to-sale systems carry sample, batch, result, and certificate records;
- CCB laboratory inspectors review methods, materials, sanitation, and potency data;
- data analysts or technically trained staff aggregate results and construct or interpret distributions;
- investigators examine suspected conduct and supporting records;
- agency lawyers and Board members translate evidence into complaints, settlements, sanctions, or other dispositions where warranted;
- consumers, patients, retailers, cultivators, and brands act on potency numbers and certificates.

**Finding:** the meaningful invention is an administrative measurement-and-screening workflow, not a self-executing chart. Its performance depends on human sampling, instrument operation, data entry, statistical judgment, inspection, investigation, and legal process.

### 3. Period + products/processes

Tangible technologies and processes identified:

- regulated sampling tools and chain-of-custody records;
- laboratory preparation methods and analytical instruments used for cannabinoid measurement;
- chromatograms and instrument data;
- laboratory information records and certificates of analysis;
- Metrc sample, batch, and result records;
- an aggregate potency dataset;
- paired histograms representing result distributions;
- a stated above-30-percent review threshold;
- inspection records, retest/remediation requests, statements of deficiency, complaints, and disciplinary records.

**Finding:** the histogram is a tangible visualization produced from a longer measurement and reporting chain. It can expose clustering, tails, heaping, shifts, or other patterns worth examining. It cannot reveal, by itself, sampling bias, moisture convention, calibration, method error, data manipulation, product mix, laboratory identity, intent, or legal responsibility.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

Present-day consequence or continuation includes:

- continued CCB laboratory inspection and potency monitoring;
- a regulated testing infrastructure tied to certificates of analysis and seed-to-sale records;
- the current public Nevada Item Catalog for recent products and COAs;
- the Nevada Cannabis Laboratory Library referenced for historical testing data;
- public-record and disciplinary processes that may expose portions of case-level enforcement;
- ongoing consumer and commercial reliance on reported potency.

**Finding:** the system continues as a regulatory monitoring function, while public-facing product and historical-data tools extend visibility downstream. Those interfaces are related infrastructure, not proof that the 2020/2023 histogram investigation caused their creation.

## Mechanism and failure-mode map

| Stage | Input | Human/technical transformation | Output | Failure or burden to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lot and sample | Cannabis lot/production run | Selection and collection under regulated sampling rules | Laboratory sample and custody record | Nonrepresentative sample, selection pressure, handling error, moisture change, custody gap |
| Measurement | Prepared sample, standards, instrument, method | Extraction, calibration, separation/detection, calculation, analyst review | Raw data, chromatogram, calculated cannabinoids | Calibration, matrix effects, method variation, integration choices, uncertainty, transcription |
| Reporting | Reviewed result plus identifiers | Laboratory information and compliance-system entry; COA generation | Reported potency tied to a batch/sample | Rounding, dry-weight convention, duplicate/retest handling, identity mismatch, delayed/corrected result |
| Aggregation | Many reported results | Query, inclusion/exclusion, product classification, binning, date window | Distribution or histogram | Missing denominator, opaque exclusions, product-mix shift, duplicates, inconsistent units or categories |
| Screening | Distribution and individual results | Human review of shape, tails, clustering, and stated threshold | Lead, flag, or request for review | Base-rate error, arbitrary threshold, confirmation bias, no documented alert rule |
| Investigation | Flag plus laboratory and compliance records | Inspectors/investigators examine methods, raw data, records, practices, retests, and explanations | Supported concern, no finding, correction, or referral | Confidential docket, incomplete records, weak causal link, inconsistent treatment |
| Disposition and monitoring | Investigative evidence and legal standards | Agency/legal review, complaint or other action, later monitoring | Case outcome and/or later distributions | Public chart not linked to named final outcomes; deterrence and causation unmeasured |

## Nonduplication decision

This proposition remains distinct if the following boundaries are enforced:

- **Story 3:** regulatory data analytics—how aggregate result shape becomes an enforcement lead and why screening evidence is not adjudicative proof.
- **Story 4:** plant/package identity and operational traceability across a lifecycle.
- **Story 6:** what the public can see and understand through the Item Catalog and COAs.
- **Story 12:** how a laboratory produces an individual measurement and why a “pass” compresses sampling, method, uncertainty, and decision thresholds.
- **Story 15:** reverse traceability and public warning after a product problem is identified.

Story 3 may explain measurement only far enough to show what enters the dataset. It must not absorb Story 12's full analytical-measurement chain or Story 6's interface audit.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep the histogram as the central technology.** Rejected as too narrow: the visual is one artifact in a larger surveillance and enforcement workflow.
2. **Center laboratory analytical chemistry.** Rejected as primary because that duplicates Story 12; retain only the upstream inputs and uncertainty necessary to understand the data.
3. **Center Metrc as the data source.** Not selected: exact data provenance and query path for the published histograms have not been established.
4. **Center a national “lab shopping” or potency-inflation market story.** Rejected for this slot unless Nevada-specific commercial and case evidence is obtained.
5. **Center Nevada's measurement-to-screening-to-investigation system.** Selected provisionally as the strongest distinct technology proposition.

## Package consequences

- Preserve the existing outline and title as provisional, not approved for drafting.
- Rebuild the claim ledger around data provenance, field definitions, inclusion/exclusion rules, date windows, product types, denominators, bin construction, review thresholds, escalation, and case correspondence.
- Make the labor visible: sampler, laboratory analyst, quality reviewer, data analyst, lab inspector, investigator, lawyer, and regulated business.
- Use a faithful reproduction or explicitly labeled reconstruction of the official paired histograms. Never infer exact bin values from pixels.
- The real-image carousel should use the actual report page, a cleared Nevada laboratory instrument/sample workflow, a real COA, a real chromatogram or method record with permission, a process diagram grounded in records, and a real inspection/enforcement document. No decorative text-card carousel.
- The video route should prioritize a Nevada-specific laboratory, CCB, hearing, inspection, or analytical-method source that can be watched, timecoded, captioned, and lawfully embedded. Generic cannabis-lab footage does not satisfy the route.
- RN's location-photography assignment should identify a genuinely connected accessible site—preferably a Nevada independent testing laboratory with advance permission or the CCB Las Vegas office as a documentary institutional fallback—and specify privacy/security limits before capture.
- Audio must convert chart references into spatial spoken descriptions, explain “THC,” “COA,” “Metrc,” histogram, chromatogram, denominator, and review trigger conversationally, and preserve every qualification. The written article remains the complete transcript.

## Evidence still required before drafting

1. Machine-readable source data or exact bin counts, denominators, dates, product categories, laboratory universe, and exclusions behind the 2020 and 2023 histograms.
2. The data-system provenance and workflow: source records, fields, query/export, cleaning, deduplication, unit conversion, dry-weight treatment, retests, remediation results, and version history.
3. The operational meaning of the above-30-percent trigger: who receives the flag, whether it is manual or automated, required review steps, escalation criteria, and documentation.
4. Final complaints, settlements, sanctions, dismissals, corrections, or exonerations that can be lawfully connected to the investigation without implying guilt from a chart.
5. Evidence evaluating alternative explanations, including product mix, genetics, sampling, moisture, method, rounding, duplicate tests, selective submission, and laboratory-market changes.
6. Interviews or review from a Nevada laboratory worker, former/current regulator where available, independent analytical chemist, and statistician or forensic-data specialist.
7. Item-level visual provenance, permissions, captions, credits, and alt text; current legal and scientific review; genuine cold-reader test; LinkedIn, carousel, audio, and final accessibility QA.

## Final gate result

**PASS PROVISIONALLY AS A DISTINCT TECHNOLOGY PROPOSITION; DRAFTING REMAINS BLOCKED.**

Story 3 survives because it has a concrete Nevada technical system, tangible artifacts, identifiable labor, a documented institutional use, and a present-day continuation. It may return to drafting only after the missing data provenance and screening-to-enforcement correspondence are either recovered or made an explicit, evidenced absence central to the article, and after analytical-chemistry and statistical review confirm the language and limits.
