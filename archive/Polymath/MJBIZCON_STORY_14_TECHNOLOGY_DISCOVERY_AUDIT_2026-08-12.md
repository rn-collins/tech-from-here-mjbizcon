**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Prior working title:** *The Lounge Is an Engineered Intoxication Environment*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS A STRONG, DISTINCT TECHNOLOGY PROPOSITION. RETAIN THE ENGINEERED-ENVIRONMENT FRAME, WITH AIRFLOW, DOSE/SERVICE, HUMAN INTERVENTION AND EXIT-RISK SYSTEMS TREATED AS ONE OPERATING ARCHITECTURE. STORY 8 REMAINS REMOVED. DRAFTING IS BLOCKED PENDING AS-BUILT AND OPERATING EVIDENCE FROM AT LEAST ONE ACTIVE NEVADA LOUNGE, WORKER/PATRON EVIDENCE, AND OCCUPATIONAL-HEALTH, HVAC, ACCESSIBILITY AND HARM-REDUCTION REVIEW.**

## Controlling correction

The lounge is not technologically important merely because cannabis may be consumed there. Nor does a regulation requiring equipment prove that the equipment achieves its public-health purpose in operation.

The broad pass nevertheless reveals an unusually concrete Nevada system. The state does not regulate a lounge as an ordinary hospitality room. It joins:

- a physically separated smoking room with self-closing sealed doors;
- negative pressure, direct exhaust, minimum air-change rates and MERV-14-or-higher filtration, or an approved alternative;
- carbon-dioxide and fine-particulate monitoring in the nonsmoking room with audible and visual failure alerts;
- product-form, single-sale and low-dose rules;
- individual dosing, dose marking and allergen information for prepared products;
- ID authentication, inventory entries and a prohibition on serving visibly overconsuming patrons;
- compensated employee training and written response plans;
- secondhand-smoke protocols and personal protective equipment;
- medical-incident reporting and an impaired-driving mitigation plan.

Together these make a designed, monitored and human-operated risk environment. The article must test the correspondence between plan, installed system, operation, exposure and outcome. “Engineered” describes an architecture of controls; it does not mean “safe,” “effective,” “accessible” or “self-correcting.”

## Retained proposition

**The Lounge Is an Engineered Intoxication Environment.**

Stronger explanatory deck: **Nevada regulates cannabis lounges through pressure zones, air monitoring, dose controls, trained workers and an exit plan—but design requirements are not proof of protection.**

Alternate headline: **Nevada Put the Cannabis Lounge on a Control Loop.** Use only if field evidence proves that alarms, logs and worker protocols actually produce documented corrective action; otherwise “control loop” overstates the system.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The place is the Las Vegas lounge itself, not generic Nevada nightlife. As of 1 July 2026, the CCB lists three active lounge facilities, all in Las Vegas: DAZED! at 2548 West Desert Inn Road, SOCIETY at 4640 Paradise Road, Suites 8–9, and SMOKE AND MIRRORS at 2975 Sammy Davis Jr. Drive.

The physical plan matters. Indoor smoking or inhalation must be confined to a designated room separated by solid partitions or glass. Doors and service windows must self-close and seal. The baseline ventilation plan calls for at least 20 complete air changes per hour in the smoking room and six in the rest of the lounge, negative pressure in the smoking room, direct exhaust and MERV-14-or-higher filtration. The nonsmoking room must have atmospheric monitoring focused on eight-hour averages and 15-minute peaks for carbon dioxide and PM2.5, with audible and visual failure notifications. Approved alternatives and waivers exist, so no lounge may be described from the baseline rule without its actual approved plan.

The operating technology continues through ID scanners, point-of-sale/inventory records, menus and dose information, individually dosed preparations, cleaning/sanitation equipment, PPE, incident logs, training records, surveillance/security, transportation information and staff decisions about access, service and intervention.

### 2. Institution + inventions

AB 341 (2021) authorized two lounge types and directed the CCB to regulate their operation. The CCB and Cannabis Advisory Commission developed the implementing framework through public meetings and workshops; the rules became effective in 2022. In 2023, the Board adopted more flexible ventilation alternatives and a professional-engineer waiver route. The first final lounge license was issued in February 2024; three active facilities appear in the July 2026 official list.

Nevada did not invent negative pressure, air changes per hour, MERV filtration, particulate sensors, ID scanners, dose labeling, PPE or incident-response plans. The Nevada-specific technological story is their mandatory assembly around on-site cannabis consumption, including a monitored boundary between smoking and nonsmoking space and a human response system extending from entry to departure.

This institutional architecture includes design review, professional engineering, local building/health/fire/business approval, CCB final inspection, ongoing records and possible plan revision. Plans are not performance data. Final licensure is not evidence that exposure, comprehension or impaired-driving outcomes remain controlled on a busy night.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- **2017:** Nevada adult-use retail begins without the later state lounge architecture; this is context, not Story 14's central technology.
- **2021:** AB 341 authorizes retail-attached and independent consumption lounges and requires implementing operational controls.
- **2022:** Nevada adopts the initial lounge regulations after a multi-meeting public process. Application and prospective-license processes begin.
- **2023:** the first conditional licenses are approved; ventilation rules gain alternative-device and professional-engineer waiver pathways.
- **February 2024:** the CCB records issuance of the first final lounge license.
- **July 2026:** the official active-license list shows three operating lounge facilities.
- **Current operating cycle:** verify age/authenticity and visible condition → communicate about use/needs → sell within product-specific limits and record the transaction → serve a marked single-use or ready-to-consume dose → maintain spatial/air-quality controls → observe patrons and respond under written plans → log/report qualifying incidents → discourage impaired driving and provide transportation information.

This cycle must be documented through one real operating shift, not inferred from legal text.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The ecosystem includes lounge owners and designers; architects and professional mechanical engineers; HVAC installers, controls technicians and filter/monitor vendors; local building, fire, health, air-quality and business officials; CCB licensing and inspection staff; product buyers and inventory workers; cooks and dose-preparation staff; hosts, servers, security workers and managers; cleaners and waste handlers; occupational-health professionals; rideshare/taxi/transit workers; emergency responders; patrons and nearby businesses.

The room operates through people. A pressure differential can be defeated by an open door. A monitor is useful only if located, calibrated, visible, understood and tied to an appropriate response. PPE availability does not prove fit, training, use or adequate protection. Dose information does not prove comprehension or predict individual response. A ride option does not prove an impaired person uses it.

The present consequence is therefore a regulated experiment in managing exposure and intoxication through coupled building, product, information and labor systems. Its actual effects remain empirical.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** the lounge's coupled environmental-and-behavioral control architecture: separated air zones and exhaust/filtration/monitoring; bounded products and individually communicated doses; authenticated entry and inventory records; trained observation/intervention; incident response; and departure-risk mitigation.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** actual approved/as-built ventilation plan; supply/exhaust and pressure diagram; MERV-14 filter; self-closing gasketed door or service window; differential-pressure indication where installed; CO2/PM2.5 monitor with audible/visual alarm; maintenance/malfunction log; low-dose product and dose-marked serving; ID scanner and transaction record; PPE station; training and response plan; incident report; transportation-information interface. Each depicted item must come from the audited lounge or be explicitly labeled as regulatory reference evidence.
3. **People and labor:** architects, professional engineers, HVAC/control technicians, installers, inspectors, CCB/local officials, product/menu designers, inventory staff, cooks, hosts, servers, security, managers, cleaners, workers entering the smoking room, emergency responders, transportation workers and patrons. Make workers' sensory and judgment labor visible.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada's visitor-heavy adult-use market existed before its state lounge system. AB 341 and the 2022–2023 rules created a Nevada-specific licensed environment; the first final license arrived in 2024 and three Las Vegas facilities are active in the July 2026 record.
5. **Present-day technological consequence or continuation:** active lounges now operate under spatial, ventilation, monitoring, dosing, training, incident and transportation requirements. Their product records also flow into Nevada's current Item Catalog, but Story 6 owns the public-interface audit.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** casino HVAC, nightclub security, bar responsible-service practice, commercial kitchens, tobacco smoking rooms, rideshare, air cleaners and smart-building systems are relevant comparators, not proven technical ancestors. Nevada's gaming identity did not cause the lounge ventilation design on present evidence. A lounge's location near tourism infrastructure does not establish technical lineage or impaired-driving prevention.

## Current story disposition

Retain Story 14 and the current title provisionally. It has absorbed the only defensible technical mechanism from Story 8; Story 8 remains a preserved law/tourism asset outside *Tech From Here*.

The canonical narrative should follow one breath and one serving through a real lounge. Begin at the sealed smoking-room threshold; move through pressure, exhaust and monitoring; follow the worker who serves and observes a dose; then follow the patron toward the door, where the engineered room loses physical control and relies on information, judgment and transportation behavior. End with the limit: the building can shape exposure and choices, but cannot convert intoxication into certainty.

## Mandatory evidence gate before drafting

1. Select at least one currently active lounge and verify its license, lounge type, local jurisdiction, opening date and current operating status immediately before reporting.
2. Obtain the facility's approved and as-built ventilation/odor plan, alternative/waiver if any, professional-engineer documents, commissioning/air-balancing evidence, monitor locations, alarm thresholds and applicable local conditions.
3. Document installed equipment and actual operation: room boundaries, door behavior, pressure relationship, air-change verification, exhaust route, filter specification/change records, sensor calibration and malfunction/alarm logs.
4. Recover the air-quality standard or approved facility thresholds used for eight-hour average and 15-minute peak CO2/PM2.5 monitoring; the NAC's monitoring requirement must not be converted into invented numerical limits.
5. Observe at least one permissioned operating period, including a busy interval, without photographing patrons or staff without consent. Record door openings, room occupancy, service practices, alarms, worker entry, PPE and patron movement.
6. Obtain privacy-safe training materials and records: initial/annual compensated hours, product/potency/absorption instruction, overconsumption, sexual-assault, health and impaired-driving plans.
7. Audit menus and real transactions across inhaled and edible routes: dose visibility, low-dose availability, allergen information, serving limits, staff communication and COA/product correspondence.
8. Obtain aggregate, deidentified medical-incident, overconsumption, air-alarm/malfunction, enforcement and transportation-mitigation records—or document their evidenced unavailability without treating silence as zero events.
9. Include workers across roles and shifts, especially those entering smoking rooms and cleaning devices; obtain occupational-health/HVAC review independent of the operator.
10. Conduct an accessibility audit covering physical access, seating, restroom/egress, menu readability, screen-reader/digital access, audible plus visual alarms, sensory burden, communication access and transportation. Regulatory low-dose availability is not an accessibility finding.
11. Obtain harm-reduction, disability/accessibility, Nevada legal/regulatory and genuine cold-reader review. No outreach or facility access is authorized yet.

## Final gate

**STORY 14 PASSES PROVISIONALLY AS A STRONG, DISTINCT TECHNOLOGY PROPOSITION.**

Nevada's lounge is a real place-specific technical system because air, smoke, dose, information, human observation, incident response and departure risk are deliberately joined. The story survives only if it reports the gap between required design and actual operation. A plan, license, filter, monitor, low-dose menu or rideshare option must never be represented as proof of protection.
