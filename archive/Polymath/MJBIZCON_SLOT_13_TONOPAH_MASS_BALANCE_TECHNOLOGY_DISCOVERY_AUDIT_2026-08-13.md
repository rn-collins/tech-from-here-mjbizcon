# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Slot 13 Tonopah Mass-Balance Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 13 August 2026  
**Candidate:** *The Extract Output Was Larger Than the Input*  
**Decision:** **PROMOTE PROVISIONALLY TO SLOT 13; DOSSIER AND DRAFTING BLOCKED**

## Controlling proposition

**The Extract Output Was Larger Than the Input.**

Expanded proposition: **A regulated cannabis extraction system must reconcile physical material with recorded material. In 2023, Nevada's Cannabis Compliance Board said suspicious Metrc activity led agents to a Tonopah-area facility where extract outputs exceeded the amount of cannabis recorded as utilized, packages were missing, camera coverage failed and material was being dried without environmental controls. The technology story is mass balance: scales, package identities, extraction records, cameras and human reconciliation must agree about what entered, changed and left a production system.**

The hook is immediate, tangible and publicly intelligible. It is stronger and more distinct than the vacated sample-correspondence candidate because its central mechanism is not the validity of a laboratory measurement. It is the conservation-and-accounting problem that arises when a physical production process and its digital inventory representation disagree.

Promotion is provisional. The one-page CCB summary establishes the Board's stated findings and summary-suspension action, but it does not publish the underlying Metrc records, mass-balance calculations, surveillance audit, inspection photographs, plan of correction, lifting order, ownership-change order or final disposition. Those gaps block drafting.

## Primary evidence currently recovered

### November 2023 CCB suspension record

The CCB's 2 November 2023 release states that it summarily suspended The Hempire Company LLC's medical and adult-use cultivation and production licenses, effective 1 November 2023 at 6:14 p.m., citing a present threat to public health and safety. The Board stated that, while investigating suspicious Metrc activity and insufficient camera coverage, agents:

- discovered cannabis extract outputs exceeding the amount of cannabis utilized;
- determined failed or untested cannabis may have been combined with passing lots and sent to extraction;
- found approximately 180 cultivation packages and 10 production packages missing;
- found two large bags of usable cannabis in a dumpster;
- found no working cameras in greenhouses, conex boxes or other growing/drying areas; and
- observed cannabis drying outside and in a conex box without environmental controls.

The release required a Board-approved plan of correction before operations could resume. It is a summary-suspension record, not the underlying complaint, evidentiary exhibit or final adjudication.

Primary source: <https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/CCB-Issues-Summary-Suspension-of-Tonopah-Cultivation-Production-Licenses-11-2-23for-realease-1.pdf>

### July 2026 current-status records

The CCB's July 2026 active-license workbook lists the same adult-use/retail license numbers as active under **Tonopah Farm LLC**:

- `67921128221276401437`, C184, Retail Cultivation, active; and
- `64364789010887954474`, P120, Retail Production, active.

The current CCB entity record identifies P120 as active through 31 March 2027, lists the establishment city as Tonopah and the county/local jurisdiction as Esmeralda, and shows current owners/officers/board members. These current individuals must not be associated with 2023 conduct unless a primary ownership/transfer chronology proves their role at the relevant time.

The changed entity name and active status prove that the 2023 suspension is not a safe description of present operating status. They do not by themselves establish when or why the suspension was lifted, whether a plan of correction was accepted, when ownership changed, or whether every issue was finally adjudicated.

## Four-lens technology discovery

### Place + technology

The precise public geographic statement is currently: a cultivation/production facility identified by the CCB as located in **Tonopah**, with current license records placing the establishment in **Esmeralda County**. Tonopah itself is ordinarily associated with Nye County; the apparent city/jurisdiction mismatch may reflect a postal designation or a facility outside the town boundary. Do not resolve it by assumption. Recover the licensed facility address and parcel/jurisdiction before place narration or RN photography.

The place matters technologically because an enclosed industrial process, remote facility, outdoor/conex drying spaces, state inventory network and regulator's off-site data review converged in one inspection. The story is not “rural Nevada is lax.” Remoteness is context only unless evidence connects it causally to staffing, communications, inspection, infrastructure or control failures.

### Institution + inventions

The candidate's system includes:

- tagged source and production packages;
- calibrated scales and quantity entries;
- production batches and extraction job records;
- recorded input mass, intermediate material, output mass, waste, samples and adjustments;
- expected process yields and unit conversions;
- Metrc package creation, transfer, finish and adjustment events;
- exception queries and human reconciliation;
- surveillance cameras, retention and coverage maps;
- environmental controls for drying and storage;
- physical inventory counts and missing-package investigation;
- inspection, summary suspension and plan-of-correction workflow; and
- license/entity continuity across later ownership or name changes.

Mass balance is the central technical idea: recorded outputs, losses, waste and remaining material must be reconcilable to recorded inputs within measured uncertainty and lawful process events. The audit does not yet possess the actual equation, quantities, tolerances, moisture corrections, extraction yields or records used by CCB agents. Never invent them.

### Period + products/processes

The bounded chronology is:

1. CCB agents investigated what the Board called suspicious Metrc activity and insufficient camera coverage.
2. Agents inspected/reviewed the facility and reported the mismatches and physical conditions described above.
3. The CCB summarily suspended four medical/adult-use cultivation and production licenses effective 1 November 2023.
4. The Board required an approved plan of correction and confirmation before resumed operations.
5. By July 2026, the two adult-use license numbers C184 and P120 appeared active under Tonopah Farm LLC.

The intervening correction, lifting, ownership, transfer and disposition chronology is unresolved.

### Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The system depends on cultivation workers, harvest/drying workers, production and extraction technicians, inventory-control staff, Metrc operators, quality/compliance personnel, security staff, camera installers/maintainers, waste handlers, managers and CCB data analysts/agents. A credible article must show that reconciliation is labor, not an automatic property of tags or software.

The present-day continuation is the use of the same license IDs under an active entity and the continuing regulatory need to reconcile physical product, production records and surveillance evidence. Do not imply that current operations repeat the 2023 conditions.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central system:** physical-to-digital mass balance and inventory reconciliation across cultivation, extraction, waste and package identity.
2. **Tangible technology/process:** a documented input/output reconciliation using real Metrc package events, scale records, extraction batch records, waste entries and camera-coverage evidence from the case.
3. **People and labor:** growers, dryers, extraction technicians, inventory staff, compliance personnel, security/camera workers, waste handlers and CCB agents.
4. **Place connection:** the specifically licensed Tonopah/Esmeralda County facility where CCB connected suspicious digital activity to physical inspection findings.
5. **Present consequence:** the same retail license identifiers are active under a differently named entity; the regulatory system must preserve accurate identity and correction history across operational change.
6. **Concurrent/noncausal temptations:** rural location does not prove causation; broken cameras do not by themselves explain mass imbalance; excess recorded output does not itself identify the source of material; missing packages do not prove diversion; discarded cannabis does not prove it entered commerce; later active status does not erase, affirm or finally adjudicate the 2023 record.
