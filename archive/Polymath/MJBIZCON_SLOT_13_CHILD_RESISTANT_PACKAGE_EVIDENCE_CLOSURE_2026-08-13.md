## What the public record proves

### Nevada performance rule

Current NAC 678B.596 requires cannabis sold at a cannabis sales facility to be placed into or directly packaged in opaque, child-resistant packaging in accordance with 16 C.F.R. Part 1700. It expressly says the packaging must maintain its effectiveness for multiple openings before leaving with the consumer. The CCB FAQ confirms that compliant single-product packaging or an exit bag can satisfy this retail requirement.

### Approval and submission system

The CCB FAQ states that packaging must be pre-approved by Board staff and only licensed establishments or their designees may request approval. Current amendment instructions identify a Packaging & Advertising Submittal Request and require “complete packaging” plus a sample compliance label. The CCB's 2021 Accela notice identifies Packaging Request as a license amendment connected in the back office to the license on which it is submitted.

These records prove an approval workflow exists. They do not reveal what technical report is required, what Board staff independently verifies, which model/version was approved, whether approvals are portable across licensees, or how changed components are controlled.

### Federal human-performance protocol

16 C.F.R. § 1700.20 and current CPSC guidance establish the timed child and senior-adult tests, age distributions, sequential panels, opening/resecuring procedures and effectiveness thresholds. This supports the central technological proposition and the essential qualification that child-resistant is not childproof.

### Separate household layer

CCB consumer guidance instructs consumers to retain child-resistant packaging and place it in a locked cabinet or box out of children's sight and reach. Packaging, correct closure, locked storage and supervision are separate controls.

### Terminology defect worth preserving

The CCB consumer webpage uses “child-proof packaging,” while the controlling regulation uses “child-resistant.” The article must follow the legal/technical term and may cautiously identify the public-language mismatch. It cannot imply that the webpage changes the regulatory performance claim.
