## Editorial determination

### Corrected working title

**The Cannabis Can Disappear. The Record Cannot.**

### Strong alternate

**Nevada Makes Cannabis Waste Vanish Twice**

### One-sentence revelation

In Nevada, regulated cannabis waste must disappear in two synchronized systems: workers physically render the material unusable, while the establishment creates a traceable digital and documentary event explaining what was destroyed, why, when, how, and by whom.

### Falsifiable version

Nevada's current rules require regulated establishments to notify the state through the seed-to-sale tracking system before rendering cannabis or cannabis products unusable and disposing of them; certain inventory records must document disposal, and the physical waste must be rendered unusable before it leaves the establishment.

### Unsafe versions — do not use

- “Nevada tracks every gram from seed to landfill.” The located evidence does not prove perfect end-to-end observation, nor that every waste stream ends in a landfill.
- “Metrc proves cannabis was destroyed.” A software entry can document a claimed event; it is not physical proof of correct destruction.
- “Cannabis waste is composted.” Nevada permits different disposal routes, and its rule imposes conditions on composting.
- “Grinding makes cannabis environmentally safe.” “Unusable” is an anti-diversion standard, not a complete environmental-safety finding.
- “Nevada invented digital cannabis-waste tracking.” No priority claim is supported.
- “The state watches destruction live.” The current rule and located public records do not establish universal live state observation.

## Why this is unmistakably a technology story

The central mechanism is a coupled physical–digital control system:

1. a regulated object is classified as waste;
2. its identity, amount, reason, and custody are recorded;
3. the state is notified through the seed-to-sale system before destruction and disposal;
4. the material is ground and mixed, or processed through another Board-approved method, until it is unusable;
5. the disposal method and responsible agent remain in the record;
6. audits compare the physical event, local logs, inventory adjustments, surveillance or other evidence when available, and the state tracking record;
7. discrepancies become compliance and enforcement problems.

The technology is not merely the grinder or the software. It is the attempt to keep matter, identity, custody, and account synchronized at the moment an economically valuable regulated product becomes trash.

## Nevada-specific legal and technical mechanism

### 1. Daily inventory includes disposal

NAC 678B.214 requires each establishment, except an independent testing laboratory, to operate an inventory-control system documenting beginning inventory, acquisitions, harvests, sales, disbursements, disposal of unusable cannabis, and ending inventory. The rule measures different materials differently, including plant counts, flower and trim weight in grams, THC in milligrams, and seed weight in grams.

### 2. The disposal record has named fields

For cannabis that is not usable, the rule calls for a description and reason; date; confirmation that it was rendered unusable; disposal method; and the name and agent-card number of the responsible establishment agent.

### 3. The material must become unusable before it leaves

NAC 678B.475 requires cannabis-containing waste to be rendered unusable before leaving the establishment. Unless another Board-approved method is used, the rule specifies grinding and incorporating the material with approved compostable or noncompostable waste so cannabis constitutes less than 50 percent of the resulting mixture by volume.

### 4. The digital notice precedes the physical event

The same section requires notice to the Board through the seed-to-sale tracking system before rendering cannabis or cannabis products unusable and disposing of them.

### 5. Nevada moved plant-waste reporting into Metrc

A Nevada Metrc support bulletin announced a workflow for recording plant waste by location directly in Metrc, replacing the prior practice of emailing plant-waste logs to the CCB each time waste was created. Its interface sequence is valuable technical evidence, but it must not be mistaken for evidence that the corresponding physical destruction occurred correctly.

### 6. Audits test reconciliation, not merely disposal technique

The CCB's public audit checklist asks whether the establishment rendered cannabis waste unusable, used an approved method, maintained complete disposal logs, identified responsible agents, and controlled significant inventory variances. Enforcement records show why the reconciliation layer matters: public matters include allegations involving large quantities entered as waste without adequate documentation, disposal methods that did not comply with the rule, and products recorded as destroyed or disposed of while other evidence indicated diversion or improper handling.

## The saveable framework: The Two-Death Test

A cannabis item is not fully removed from the regulated system merely because it is physically ruined or digitally zeroed out. Test both deaths.

1. **Identify** — What regulated plant, package, lot, run, or quantity is leaving inventory?
2. **Explain** — Why did it become waste?
3. **Notify** — Was the required seed-to-sale notice made before destruction and disposal?
4. **Render** — Was the physical material actually made unusable through an approved method?
5. **Dispose** — Where and how did the resulting waste leave or remain within the permitted disposal route?
6. **Attribute** — Which responsible agents performed and recorded the event?
7. **Reconcile** — Do weight, timing, logs, inventory changes, and available physical or surveillance evidence agree?

This framework distinguishes a software correction from a physical act and a physical act from an auditable regulated event.

## Law, labor, environment, and power

- **Law:** Nevada combines anti-diversion rules, inventory control, physical waste standards, state/local waste laws, and enforcement.
- **Labor:** Workers translate irregular biological material and manufacturing loss into standardized weights, reasons, timestamps, and system actions. Their measurement and judgment are invisible in a clean database record.
- **Environment:** The legal category “unusable” can coexist with landfill burden, plastic packaging, solvents, contaminated biomass, wastewater, or other environmental problems. This dossier does not claim the anti-diversion protocol is a sustainability system.
- **Power:** The licensee bears the duty to make physical and digital reality match; the regulator can audit and sanction mismatches. The platform structures which events become legible.
- **Security:** Detailed facility layouts, destruction schedules, waste-storage locations, credentials, tag numbers, and access practices must not be exposed.
