# MJBizCon Story 15 — Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Working title:** *When a Recall Has to Find the Product*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS A STRONG, DISTINCT TECHNOLOGY PROPOSITION. RETAIN THE REVERSE-TRACEABILITY AND INCIDENT-RESPONSE FRAME. DRAFTING REMAINS BLOCKED PENDING A RECORD-LEVEL RECONSTRUCTION OF ONE NEVADA EVENT, THE ADMINISTRATIVE-HOLD WORKFLOW, DISPOSITION AND CONSUMER-REACH EVIDENCE, AND SCIENTIFIC, REGULATORY AND OPERATOR REVIEW.**

## Controlling correction

A seed-to-sale system does not discover every hazard, and a public warning does not prove that every affected unit was found or recovered. Story 15 survives because it asks a narrower and more revealing technical question: once Nevada identifies a suspect source, how does the system find where its material went?

The answer is a human-operated incident-response chain. Investigators define a source event or affected interval; query linked harvest, package, production, transfer and sale records; distinguish usable cannabis from manufactured derivatives; place or request holds; identify retail endpoints; translate internal identifiers into label instructions and public lists; amend the scope as evidence changes; and document release, destruction, return or other disposition. The chain is revisable and limited by the data entered, the hazard known, the lineage preserved and the product still reachable.

The January–February 2023 ethephon bulletin is the strongest primary case. CCB agents reported nearly 8,000 pounds of cannabis and products treated at Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions. The warning defined affected usable cannabis by cultivator and harvest dates, instructed consumers of derivatives to match both item and production-run information, named sales facilities, and later added products. The same bulletin states that ethephon was not a required pesticide analyte and laboratories were not configured to detect it. That makes the crucial distinction visible: measurement failed to ask one question; traceability became useful only after investigators knew which material to follow.

A separate December 2025 Eagle 20 bulletin demonstrates revision. Its amended version says eight packages were removed after further review determined they were unaffected and permitted for sale. This is evidence of investigative correction, not evidence that the 2023 case contained the same error or that every amendment has the same cause.

## Retained proposition

**When a Recall Has to Find the Product.**

Stronger explanatory deck: **Nevada can follow a known source through packages, production runs and retailers—but tracking cannot detect an unmeasured hazard or guarantee recovery after sale.**

Do not call every public-health bulletin a “recall” unless the governing record supports that term and specifies the responsible party, scope and required action. The article may use “recall” in the headline as the reader-facing problem, then accurately distinguish administrative hold, public warning, licensee action, product disposition and consumer recovery.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The place is Nevada's licensed cannabis supply chain under incident conditions. In the 2023 case it begins at Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions, moves through harvests made between 23 July 2021 and 5 January 2023, continues into usable cannabis and manufactured derivatives, and ends at named Nevada sales facilities and packages consumers may already possess.

The technical geography is made of identifiers and custody events rather than a simple map. A cultivation facility and harvest date may identify usable cannabis. A derivative may require an item name plus a production-run or lot number. Between them sit source packages, splits, combinations, transformations, manifests, receiving records, laboratory records, inventory adjustments and sale events. Each transition is entered or reconciled by people. A missed, late, inaccurate or ambiguous event can alter the query result.

Nevada requires licensed cultivators, manufacturers, laboratories, distributors, lounges and retail stores to report establishment data through Metrc; the CCB says this has applied since 1 November 2017. Current NRS 678B.505 describes an inventory system intended, insofar as practicable, to monitor the chain of custody and current whereabouts of cannabis from acquisition by a cultivation facility through sale. Statutory intent and required reporting are not proof of complete, accurate or literally instantaneous records.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Nevada did not invent seed-to-sale databases, package identifiers, production-run records, administrative holds, recalls, spreadsheets or public-health warnings. Its place-specific story is the institutional assembly of those tools through the Department of Taxation-era Metrc deployment, the later Cannabis Compliance Board, Nevada statutes and regulations, licensed operators, laboratories, and public-facing label and bulletin practices.

The CCB's role is both investigative and translational. Internal records may identify long package numbers and branching lineage, while a consumer needs a cultivator, harvest date, item, lot or production-run instruction visible on a package. Retailers need an affected list and a direction about holding, transferring, selling, returning or destroying inventory. Laboratories contribute measurement and certificates, but only for analytes and methods actually in scope. Operators supply the event data that later make a reverse query possible.

Administrative-hold practice must be recovered from operative rules, CCB/Metrc instructions and a real case. A 31 January 2024 CCB solicitation-of-input record is a lead, not sufficient final proof. Before drafting, document who can initiate a hold, what object is held, whether descendants are included automatically or manually, what operations are blocked, how a licensee learns of it, how corrections are made, and who releases it.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- **1 November 2017:** statewide licensed medical and adult-use establishments are required by CCB guidance to report establishment data through Metrc.
- **2020:** the Cannabis Compliance Board assumes the modern regulatory role created through the 2019 legislation; do not imply that Metrc began with the CCB.
- **23 July 2021–5 January 2023:** harvest interval identified in the Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions ethephon case.
- **19 January 2023:** initial Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2023-01 warns about affected usable cannabis and derivatives, supplies label-matching instructions and identifies retail endpoints.
- **9 February 2023:** the updated bulletin and spreadsheet add affected products. The exact row additions and reasons must be reconstructed from the two files.
- **2024:** CCB public-meeting material provides a lead on administrative-hold practice; operative workflow remains an evidence gate.
- **12/19 December 2025:** Bulletin 2025-02 is issued and amended; eight Eagle 20 packages are removed after further review determines them unaffected.
- **2026 continuation:** the CCB's public Item Catalog displays current Metrc-derived product and hold status. Story 6 owns the interface audit; Story 15 may use it only to show how incident status can reach the public layer.

The operating sequence to verify is: signal or discovery → define suspect source/material and time boundary → query lineage and descendants → place or communicate holds → identify derivatives and retailer endpoints → issue facility and public instructions using label-visible keys → investigate discrepancies → amend scope → release, return, destroy or otherwise dispose → assess what remained sold, consumed or unreachable.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The system depends on cultivators and harvest workers; compliance and data-entry staff; processors who split, combine and transform packages; packagers and label designers; distributors and drivers; laboratory samplers, analysts and reviewers; CCB investigators and data analysts; Metrc administrators and support staff; retail inventory workers, managers and budtenders; public-information and accessibility staff; health experts; consumers; and workers who quarantine, return or destroy product.

The labor is not an incidental backdrop. Investigators decide what source boundary to query. Compliance workers reconcile physical inventory with records. Processors preserve or obscure lineage through transformations. Retail staff locate packages and stop transactions. Communications staff turn machine identifiers into usable warnings. Consumers must still possess, notice, understand and compare a label. People also correct the record when a package was linked too broadly or narrowly.

The present-day consequence is a revisable reverse-traceability capability. It can make a known problem more locatable across a regulated market. Its limits are equally technological: unmeasured analytes, bad or delayed entries, ambiguous transformations, product already sold or consumed, destroyed inventory, incomplete notices, inaccessible information and a lack of consumer-level identity or contact data.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** Nevada's reverse-traceability and incident-response system: source definition, linked inventory events, descendant-package queries, administrative holds, retailer endpoint identification, label-keyed public communication, investigation-driven amendments and disposition.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** real Metrc source/harvest/package/production/transfer/sale records; tag and package identifiers; transfer manifest; production-run record; administrative-hold status and notice; consumer package label; initial and updated affected-product spreadsheets; retailer warning; release, return or destruction record. No reconstructed diagram may substitute for the underlying evidence.
3. **People and labor:** farm and harvest workers; compliance/data staff; processors; packagers; distributors; laboratory samplers/analysts/reviewers; CCB investigators and communications staff; Metrc support; retail inventory staff and budtenders; health experts; waste/disposition workers; consumers.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada required licensed establishments to use Metrc beginning in 2017 and later used its regulated lineage, label and retail network in public-health investigations such as the 2023 Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions ethephon case.
5. **Present-day technological consequence or continuation:** current incident response can identify and hold known packages, traverse descendants, publish retailer/product lists, revise scope and expose hold state through newer public infrastructure. Whether it finds or recovers every unit remains an empirical question.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** laboratory testing is upstream evidence, not the same technology and not the cause of traceability; RFID, blockchain, QR codes, ordinary retail barcodes, food/pharmaceutical recalls and the 2026 Item Catalog are comparators or adjacent layers unless direct Nevada lineage is documented. Ethephon's absence from the analyte panel explains a detection blind spot, not a failure of package tracking. An amended 2025 bulletin does not prove the 2023 list was inaccurate for the same reason.

## Current story disposition

Retain Story 15 and its title provisionally. The canonical article should open with a real package or label in a consumer's hand, then move backward through the public list to the retailer, transfer, derivative production run, source package and harvest interval. At the source, reveal the detection limit: the record could say where known material went, but the original testing regime was not configured to ask whether ethephon was present. Then follow the response forward again through holds, warnings, amendments and disposition.

The ending must be an accounting, not a triumph. Show what was identified, what was stopped, what had already sold, what was recovered or destroyed, what was corrected, what remained unknown and who had to do the work.

## Mandatory evidence gate before drafting

1. Reconstruct Bulletin 2023-01 from the initial and updated PDFs and spreadsheets. Preserve originals, compare rows and fields, identify every addition/removal/change and establish the reason where documented.
2. Obtain or request the privacy-safe record chain for representative usable cannabis and at least one manufactured derivative: source harvest/package, split or combination, production run, transfers, retailer receipt, sale/remaining inventory, hold, correction and disposition.
3. Establish how the unapproved treatment was discovered and when CCB, source facility, processors, distributors, laboratories and retailers learned of it. Do not infer discovery from the public-issue date.
4. Recover the operative administrative-hold workflow from authoritative CCB/Metrc material and a real example: authority, trigger, object, descendant scope, timing, notice, blocked actions, correction and release.
5. Build a mass/quantity accounting where records permit: approximately 8,000 pounds at source; quantities transformed, transferred, on hand, held, returned, destroyed, sold, consumed and unresolved. Do not force unlike units into a false total.
6. Verify the pesticide/analyte distinction with the applicable 2021–2023 Nevada requirements, laboratory method scope and independent analytical-chemistry review. “Not a required analyte” is not a claim that no laboratory could detect it.
7. Audit real labels against warning instructions across usable cannabis and derivatives. Verify which consumer-visible field corresponds to each regulatory identifier and where names are insufficient.
8. Interview or otherwise evidence labor at cultivation, processing, compliance, laboratory, regulatory and retail stages, plus affected consumers where ethically and lawfully possible. No contact is authorized yet.
9. Recover store-warning practice and accessible routes: web, email, on-site signage, receipt/account notification if any, language access, screen-reader/mobile usability and bulletin persistence.
10. Obtain disposition and closure evidence. A public list without return/destruction/release/recovery records cannot establish recall effectiveness.
11. Use the 2025 Eagle 20 amendment only after comparing initial and amended versions and identifying the eight removed packages. Keep it a separate revision example.
12. Obtain Nevada regulatory/legal, traceability-system, analytical-chemistry, recall/public-health, accessibility and genuine cold-reader review.

## Final gate

**STORY 15 PASSES PROVISIONALLY AS A STRONG, DISTINCT TECHNOLOGY PROPOSITION.**

Its technology is not “Metrc solves recalls.” It is a bounded, revisable and labor-intensive system for traversing known product lineage and turning internal records into holds, retailer action and consumer instructions. The ethephon case is powerful precisely because it exposes both capabilities at once: Nevada could follow identified material through a regulated chain, yet the original analytical regime had not been configured to ask whether that chemical was present.

Nothing in this audit authorizes publication, scheduling, sending, purchasing, permission outreach, interviews, facility entry, photography, recording, public-record requests or public uploading.
