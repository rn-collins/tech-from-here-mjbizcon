# MJBizCon 2026 Canonical 14 — Slot 8 Cannabis-Waste Rendering Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026 HST  
**Proposition entering audit:** *Nevada Makes Cannabis Waste Unrecognizable Before It Can Leave*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS A DISTINCT MATERIAL-TRANSFORMATION, LABOR AND ENVIRONMENTAL-SYSTEM STORY; CORRECT “UNRECOGNIZABLE” TO “UNUSABLE”; DOSSIER AND DRAFTING REMAIN BLOCKED.**

## Controlling correction

Nevada's current rule does not use “unrecognizable” as the controlling standard recovered in this audit. NAC 678B.475 requires cannabis establishments to render cannabis-containing waste **unusable before it leaves the establishment**. Unless the Board approves another method, the rule requires grinding and incorporating it with specified compostable or noncompostable mixed waste so cannabis constitutes less than 50 percent of the resulting mixture by volume.

The original headline imported terminology commonly used in other states. It cannot be used for Nevada. Likewise, a mandatory independent witness or two-person destruction rule has not been established by the current primary record. Nevada's audit checklist requires disposal records with the responsible establishment agent or agents; that does not itself prove two people must witness every event.

The story still passes. Its central technology is the **controlled declassification of a regulated material**: a tracked plant, product, failed sample, solvent or recalled item is weighed and recorded; physically transformed so it cannot be used; mixed into a new waste stream; reconciled in Metrc and facility logs; and then transferred into ordinary, organic, composting, landfill, hazardous-waste or other legally appropriate infrastructure.

## Revised proposition

**Preferred working headline:** *Nevada Makes Cannabis Waste Unusable Before It Can Leave.*

**Preferred deck:** Before tracked cannabis can become ordinary waste, workers must record it, grind it, dilute it below half the mixture by volume and route it into another disposal system—turning destruction into a regulated production process of its own.

**Alternate:** *The Last Machine in Nevada's Cannabis Supply Chain Is a Grinder.* Use only if a reported facility actually uses a grinder; the rule allows Board-approved alternatives.

## Four-lens discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The meaningful place is the back-of-house destruction area of a Nevada cultivation, production, laboratory, retail or lounge facility and the waste infrastructure to which the transformed material travels. The process occurs before the waste leaves the licensed establishment.

Nevada's default physical pathways differ by endpoint:

- For an approved organic method, cannabis waste may be ground and incorporated with food waste, yard waste, soil or another Board-approved material.
- For landfill or another nonorganic route, it may be ground and incorporated with paper, cardboard, plastic or another approved material.
- Cannabis must be less than 50 percent of the final mixture by volume.
- The current rule contains a compost route tied to federal compost guidance and Board approval, while also stating that resulting compost must never be used outside the licensed establishment. The exact current operation and legal meaning require environmental/regulatory review.

The rule covers roots, stalks, leaves, stems, flower, trim, extracted plant material, processing/extraction solvents, laboratory testing waste and other Board-designated material. Those streams are chemically and physically different; one grinding/mixing story cannot safely stand for all of them.

### 2. Institution + inventions

The institutional chain includes cannabis establishments, facility QA/inventory/security staff, CCB auditors and inspectors, Metrc, local solid-waste and wastewater authorities, permitted haulers, landfills/transfer stations, composting or organic-processing operations, hazardous-waste specialists and environmental/worker-safety regulators.

Nevada did not invent grinding, dilution, composting, landfilling, hazardous-waste management or inventory accounting. Its state-specific system joins physical destruction with regulated inventory closure: advance notice through seed-to-sale tracking, a prescribed or approved rendering method, records naming the item/reason/date/method and responsible agent or agents, and a lawful downstream waste route.

“Disposal” is therefore not one action. It is a chain of custody and changes of legal/material status.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- Nevada's medical-cannabis regulatory system contained predecessor waste controls.
- Adult-use rules adopted in the 2017 period carried forward grinding/mixing and seed-to-sale notice requirements.
- The current consolidated framework appears at NAC 678B.475 and associated NCCR inventory/audit provisions.
- Current CCB inspection and desk-audit materials explicitly test whether waste is rendered unusable, approved methods are used and destruction/disposal logs are complete.

The operational cycle to verify is: identify reason for destruction → segregate and secure the material → reconcile identity/weight → notify/record in Metrc → remove packaging or otherwise prepare as required → grind or use an approved alternative → measure and add the appropriate mixing material until cannabis is below 50 percent by volume → document item, reason, date, method and responsible personnel → store the new waste securely → transfer through the lawful waste stream → retain receipt/weight/endpoint evidence where required.

Not every step above is proved mandatory for every waste class. Facility SOPs and applicable environmental law must establish the exact chain.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The labor includes growers and harvest crews; trimmers; production and extraction workers; laboratory staff; retail/lounge inventory workers; QA; Metrc/data personnel; destruction workers; security; cleaners; haulers; landfill/transfer/compost workers; environmental-health officials and auditors.

The work is materially and occupationally significant. Workers may handle dust, mold-contaminated plant material, failed products, sharps, glass, batteries/electronics, solvents, oils, chemicals and heavy equipment. The cannabis rule does not replace hazardous-waste, wastewater, fire, OSHA or local requirements.

The present-day consequence is environmental ambiguity. Grinding cannabis into plastic or cardboard may satisfy diversion control while increasing mixed landfill material; adding soil/yard/food waste may enable an organic route under conditions; contaminated solvents or devices may require entirely different treatment. The system's diversion-control purpose and its environmental outcome are not the same thing.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology/system:** controlled material declassification—the physical, documentary and logistical process that changes regulated cannabis inventory into unusable waste and transfers it into another lawful system.
2. **Tangible technology/object/process:** actual rejected/expired/trim/lab material; scale; Metrc destruction notice/adjustment; secure waste area; grinder or approved alternative; measured mixing material; container; destruction/waste log; PPE and dust controls; hauler manifest/receipt; endpoint weight ticket or processing record. Do not depict generic equipment as the audited facility's method.
3. **People and labor:** cannabis workers who segregate, weigh, grind, mix, record and secure waste; QA/inventory/security staff; CCB auditors; haulers; landfill/transfer/compost/hazardous-waste workers; environmental and occupational-health personnel.
4. **Place-specific history:** Nevada tied adult-use cannabis destruction to on-premises unusability, less-than-50-percent mixture and seed-to-sale notice, then made destruction logs and methods auditable.
5. **Present-day consequence/continuation:** every licensed sector produces regulated waste, but public evidence does not yet quantify Nevada's volumes, endpoints, diversion rate, environmental burden or compliance performance.
6. **Concurrent/noncausal temptations:** “unusable” is not “unrecognizable”; cannabis regulation does not make all waste nonhazardous; mixing does not prove environmental benefit; a Metrc notice does not prove physical destruction or final disposal; a disposal log does not prove the endpoint received it; a grinder is not required if another method is approved; multiple responsible agents in a log do not prove a universal witness mandate.

## Distinctness controls

- **Slot 2, data double:** owns identity and reconciliation through ordinary production. Slot 8 owns the terminal transition out of regulated inventory.
- **Slot 6, recall:** owns locating/holding affected product. Slot 8 may begin only when recalled material is designated for destruction.
- **Slot 7, kief-enhanced flower:** owns combining materials to create a saleable product. Slot 8 is the inverse: combining material to eliminate usability.
- **Slot 13, illicit market:** owns estimates outside Metrc. Slot 8 may discuss diversion risk only as the purpose of destruction controls, not estimate illicit supply.
- General municipal recycling or landfill history belongs only where it is the documented endpoint of the audited waste.
