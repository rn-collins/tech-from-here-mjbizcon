# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Story 4 Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026 HST  
**Story examined:** `Every Regulated Cannabis Plant Acquires a Data Double`  
**Governing method:** Canonical 14 v2.1 four-lens technology-discovery and causal-classification gate  
**Evidence examined:** reconstructed 1,928-word Story 4 package; claim ledger; evidence outline; current Nevada CCB guidance, audit checklists, Metrc materials, administrative-hold record, and Nevada regulations  
**Publication authority:** None. Keep unpublished, unscheduled, unsent, and unuploaded to public destinations.

## Decision

**Pass Story 4 provisionally as a strong, distinct technology proposition. Preserve the 1,928-word reconstructed article as the active source draft, but do not treat it as canonical or resume expansion until the lifecycle, correction, and operator-evidence gates below are closed.**

The broad pass confirms a concrete Nevada sociotechnical system:

> plant or plant-batch identity → physical tag → harvest and weight records → packages and parent–descendant lineage → transfers and manifests → samples, results, and certificates → sales and inventory changes → reconciliation, adjustment, hold, recall, release, or destruction

Nevada defines its seed-to-sale system as an electronic database used to monitor the current chain of custody of cannabis from acquisition or planting to the end consumer. CCB materials add tangible mechanisms: RFID tags, APIs or direct system use, unique batch/run identifiers, manifests, source and derived packages, laboratory attachments, physical-inventory reconciliation, surveillance, administrative holds, and adjustment reasons.

The central insight survives: Nevada does not regulate only the physical cannabis. It regulates the integrity of the relationship between physical material and a revisable chain of records. “Data double” is a strong explanatory metaphor precisely because it does **not** claim a continuous, complete, lossless digital twin.

## Strong proposition

**Nevada Governs Cannabis Through a Revisable Data Double.**

### Headline options for later testing

1. **Every Regulated Cannabis Plant Acquires a Data Double**
2. **The Second Body of a Nevada Cannabis Plant Is Made of Records**
3. **A Cannabis Tag Begins an Argument the Data Must Keep Alive**

The existing headline may remain only if the article precisely explains when Nevada represents immature material as a plant batch and when individual plants or later packages receive identifiers. “Every plant” cannot erase lifecycle distinctions.

### Unsafe framing

- **“Metrc Is a Digital Twin of Every Cannabis Plant.”** Unsupported. The system records regulated events and claims; it is not a continuously synchronized simulation of complete physical or biological reality.
- **“The Tag Tracks the Plant Automatically.”** A tag supplies an identifier. People and connected systems create, scan, select, weigh, enter, reconcile, and correct events.
- **“RFID Proves Where the Cannabis Was.”** A tag and recorded event are evidence, not independent proof of every physical fact.
- **“Seed-to-sale Is One Unbroken Line.”** The lifecycle branches through harvests, packages, production runs, combinations, divisions, samples, remediation, and derived products.
- **“The Database Prevents Diversion or Recalls Every Product.”** Required reporting and administrative controls do not guarantee complete, timely, or accurate records or consumer recovery.
- **“Nevada Invented Seed-to-Sale Tracking.”** Unsupported without an interstate and vendor-history comparison.

## Four-lens discovery record

### 1. Place + technology

Nevada-specific technologies and systems tested:

- Nevada's required Metrc seed-to-sale compliance system, mandatory for licensed medical and adult-use establishments since 1 November 2017;
- physical RFID plant/package tags and regulated identifiers;
- plant-batch, individual-plant, harvest, package, production-run, transfer, test, sale, waste, and destruction records;
- API connection from an establishment inventory system or designation of the state system as the primary inventory system;
- quarterly physical counts and reconciliation against the state record;
- Nevada audit and inspection checklists that compare manifests, inventory, identifiers, personnel, logs, and physical conditions;
- surveillance as a separate evidentiary layer;
- administrative holds and facility notification when specified product may not be sold or transferred;
- current public Item Catalog and laboratory library as bounded downstream views.

**Finding:** Story 4 is strongly place-specific because Nevada's rules, audit instruments, training materials, and public proceedings expose how the state makes regulated cannabis traceable and contestable. The story must show Nevada's implementation rather than rely on generic Metrc marketing or generic supply-chain language.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Institutions and technical roles include:

- the Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, which prescribes and audits the system;
- Metrc as the designated compliance-system provider;
- licensed cultivation, production, distribution, laboratory, lounge, dispensary, and retail establishments required to report data;
- establishment inventory-control managers and quality-control units;
- cultivation workers who create plant batches, attach tags, change statuses, record harvests, weights, waste, and locations;
- production workers who create packages, choose source material, record runs, and preserve lineage;
- distributors and receiving agents who create and reconcile manifests;
- laboratory samplers, analysts, and reviewers who connect bounded measurements and COAs to regulated material;
- retail and inventory workers who reconcile sales and remaining stock;
- inspectors, auditors, investigators, lawyers, and Board personnel who compare records with physical inventory, surveillance, documents, and testimony;
- software administrators, API vendors, support workers, and trainers who maintain the reporting infrastructure.

**Finding:** the data double is collectively manufactured. The database standardizes labor but does not replace it. Human judgment and correction are central technical components, not side notes.

### 3. Period + products/processes

Tangible objects, interfaces, and processes include:

- RFID tags and unique identifiers;
- scanners, printers, labels, scales, cameras, point-of-sale and inventory systems;
- APIs connecting establishment systems to the state system;
- plant-batch, plant, harvest, package, item, location, manifest, sample, test, COA, sale, adjustment, waste, and destruction records;
- source-package and derived-package relationships;
- physical-count and reconciliation procedures;
- administrative holds, correction/adjustment reasons, plans of correction, and audit trails;
- CCB audit/inspection forms and system training.

**Finding:** the technical breakthrough is not a single object. It is the linked identity-and-lineage architecture that allows a changing biological material to remain addressable after it is harvested, divided, transformed, transferred, tested, sold, held, or destroyed.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The present ecosystem includes:

- continuing required Metrc reporting across Nevada license types;
- current CCB audit, inspection, training, forms, guidance, and adjustment-reason infrastructure;
- operational systems connected through APIs;
- regulatory, licensee, laboratory, and public views with different access and purposes;
- the public Item Catalog for recent items and COAs;
- the Nevada Cannabis Laboratory Library for historical testing information;
- administrative holds and recall workflows that rely on identifiers and lineage under stress.

**Finding:** the data-double function is a documented continuation of Nevada's current regulatory infrastructure. Public catalog access and recall operations are later or downstream uses of related records, but Story 4 cannot claim that the plant-tagging system alone caused those interfaces or that every later record remains perfectly linked.

## Mechanism and failure-mode map

| Stage | Physical event | Recorded representation | Human/technical labor | Failure or limit to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Propagation | Immature material grouped or created | Plant-batch record and identifier | Create batch, count, label, update | Batch count, status, source, or timing can be wrong; title must not imply every immature plant starts as an individually tagged object |
| Individual plant | Plant reaches required tracked status | Individual identifier/tag, facility, location, status | Attach tag, scan/select record, update location/status | Tag-object mismatch, lost/damaged tag, late entry, wrong location |
| Harvest | Plant is cut, weighed, dried, and generates waste | Harvest record, weights, dates, waste and source relationships | Weigh, classify, record, reconcile changing mass | Moisture loss, scale error, timing, waste classification, missing denominator |
| Packaging/production | Material is divided, combined, remediated, or transformed | Package/item/run identifiers and parent–descendant lineage | Select sources, create packages/runs, enter yields | Wrong source link, permitted combination complexity, inherited error, incomplete yield explanation |
| Transfer | Material moves between licensees | Manifest, origin/destination, quantity, agents, receive event | Prepare, transport, receive, reconcile within required time | Manifest differs from physical load, timing delay, partial receipt, wrong identifiers |
| Testing | Sample is taken and analyzed | Sample record, result, laboratory, COA, status | Sample, analyze, review, upload, associate | Sampling/method limits, wrong attachment, retest/remediation complexity, analytes outside panel |
| Sale/consumer view | Package is sold or displayed | Sales/inventory record, label, receipt, bounded catalog/COA view | Scan/report sale, maintain inventory, publish/interpret fields | Regulator/operator/public see different records; consumer identity usually not part of product lineage |
| Reconciliation/correction | Record and physical reality diverge | Adjustment, reason, support, correction plan, audit history | Investigate variance, document, request/enter correction, approve/review | Authority, attribution, before/after visibility, invalid reasons, outage handling, unexplained rewrite |
| Hold/recall/destruction | Material becomes suspect or leaves commerce | Hold, affected identifiers, notices, release/destruction records | Identify descendants/endpoints, stop movement, notify, verify disposition | Missed descendants, sold/consumed units, amended scope, communication gaps |

## Nonduplication decision

Story 4 remains distinct only with firm boundaries:

- **Story 2:** person-level medical eligibility, credentialing, verification, and confidentiality.
- **Story 3:** aggregate laboratory-result analytics used as an enforcement lead.
- **Story 4:** object-level identity and lineage across the whole regulated lifecycle, including human maintenance and reconciliation.
- **Story 6:** public Item Catalog visibility, usability, coverage, and COA access.
- **Story 12:** how an individual laboratory measurement and regulatory “pass” are produced.
- **Story 15:** one end-to-end reverse-traceability and recall case, including notice and outcome limits.

Story 4 may bridge to those systems but must not retell their complete mechanisms.

## Alternatives considered

1. **Center RFID tags alone.** Rejected as too narrow; tags identify but do not create the lifecycle representation by themselves.
2. **Call the system a digital twin.** Rejected; the evidence supports a revisable record, not a continuously synchronized replica or simulation.
3. **Center Metrc as a vendor/product profile.** Rejected; the Nevada story is the state-specific sociotechnical implementation and labor, not vendor promotion.
4. **Center surveillance as the second data body.** Rejected as primary; surveillance is a separate corroborating layer with blind spots and security concerns.
5. **Center the relationship between physical cannabis and its revisable records.** Selected; this is the strongest, most generalizable, and most distinct proposition.
