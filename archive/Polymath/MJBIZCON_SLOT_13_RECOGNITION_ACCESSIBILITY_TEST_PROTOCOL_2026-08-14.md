# MJBizCon Slot 13 — Recognition and Accessibility Test Protocol

**Story:** Nevada Put the Warning on the Edible Itself  
**Date:** 14 August 2026  
**Status:** Ready-to-administer protocol; no participants recruited and no test run  
**Purpose:** Determine what an actual Nevada serving-level symbol makes visible, recognizable, understandable and actionable—and where the interface fails  
**Not authorized by this document:** Purchase, recruitment, contact, consumption, publication or effectiveness claims

## Governing distinction

This protocol separates five questions that must not be collapsed:

1. **Detection:** Does the participant notice that a mark exists?
2. **Discrimination:** Can the participant distinguish it from decoration, dosage text, a brand mark or a manufacturing artifact?
3. **Recognition:** Do they identify it as a cannabis/THC indicator without prompting?
4. **Comprehension:** What do they believe it communicates—and what do they incorrectly infer?
5. **Action:** Does it change how they would identify, separate, store, share or avoid the item?

A person noticing a mark does not prove they understand it. Correctly identifying cannabis does not prove they know the dose. Stating a safe intention in a test does not prove real-world behavior or public-health effectiveness.

## Official interface boundary

Nevada's official symbol guidance states that the symbol is intended to assist identification of THC-containing products; qualifying edible servings must carry the symbol; the mark may be stamped, molded or printed with color contrast; milligrams may appear beside the symbol but not inside it; and the symbol may not be modified. Current NAC 678B.599 preserves the serving-level requirement and form-specific exceptions. Products considered impractical to stamp or mold enter an exemption workflow supported by pictures and a retained approval record.

The clean official symbol is therefore only the **reference condition**. The story-relevant interface is the symbol as actually reproduced on a real serving.

## Claims this test can and cannot support

### It can support, if administered correctly

- a bounded description of how this purposive sample detected and interpreted the tested mark;
- differences between clean artwork, package presentation, macro photography and actual-size serving presentation;
- observed failure modes involving size, relief, contrast, lighting, glare, texture, breakage, placement, dosage adjacency or handling;
- accessibility-specific observations from participants who describe relevant lived experience;
- reporting questions for regulators, manufacturers, retailers and specialists.

### It cannot support

- statewide recognition rates;
- proof that the rule prevents ingestion, poisoning or dosing errors;
- clinical safety or behavioral efficacy;
- claims about children based on testing adults;
- claims about blind users unless the mark is independently tactile and tested through an appropriate, consented protocol;
- claims about all Nevada edibles based on one product;
- regulatory compliance conclusions;
- superiority to another state's symbol without a separately designed comparative study.

## Prerequisites

Do not begin participant testing until all of the following are complete:

1. One current Nevada edible has been reconciled across purchase location, package, serving count, producer/license, item identity and available COA/approval information.
2. RN's ten-frame capture is complete, including full package, opened package, complete serving set, strongest and weakest visible marks, actual-size reference and macro detail.
3. A nonconsumable test kit has been created from high-resolution photographs and, if physical handling is necessary, inert replicas that cannot be mistaken for edible cannabis.
4. All item identifiers needed for research are preserved; purchaser information and unrelated traceability data are redacted.
5. The participant invitation, consent text, compensation, privacy plan and accessibility accommodations are approved by RN.
6. The test administrator has a stop procedure for discomfort, accidental disclosure of sensitive information or participant confusion about whether any test object is consumable.

No participant may be asked to ingest, taste or smell cannabis. No loose cannabis serving may be handed to participants. Testing occurs with images, packaging photographs and clearly marked inert replicas only.

## Test corpus

Use the same underlying product across these conditions:

| Condition | Material | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|
| A | Official clean symbol, neutrally displayed | Baseline recognition of the state-designed mark |
| B | Front and back of the unopened package | Package-level identification and competing information |
| C | All servings at approximately actual size | Whether the mark is detectable in the real set |
| D | Strongest and weakest visible serving imprints, randomized | Production variability and selection bias |
| E | Macro serving image | Whether enlargement changes interpretation |
| F | Actual-size image under bright, ordinary and dim lighting | Contrast and lighting sensitivity |
| G | Serving image beside dose text exactly as sold | Symbol-versus-dose inference |
| H | Broken or partially obscured serving, only if naturally present | Robustness under real handling; never manufacture damage and call it typical |
| I | Inert relief replica, optional | Tactile discoverability and dexterity, not blind accessibility certification |

The strongest mark may never be the only serving shown. The complete set and weakest legible imprint are mandatory to prevent beauty-shot bias.

## Participants

### Pilot

Run a **6-person procedural pilot** first. Its purpose is to repair the instrument, timing, materials and moderator behavior—not to generate publishable recognition percentages.

Include, where voluntarily disclosed:

- at least one adult with low vision or corrected-vision difficulty;
- at least one adult with color-vision difference;
- at least one adult with limited hand use, dexterity or grip;
- at least one adult unfamiliar with legal cannabis products;
- at least one Nevada cannabis consumer or caregiver;
- at least one nonconsuming adult who may encounter cannabis in a household or social setting.

One person may satisfy more than one category. Do not infer disability, cannabis use or caregiver status.

### Bounded field panel

If the pilot works, recruit **18–24 adults** with purposeful diversity across:

- Nevada resident / visitor;
- prior legal-cannabis exposure / little or none;
- consumer / nonconsumer / caregiver or household member;
- ages 21–34 / 35–54 / 55+;
- self-described visual, color-vision, reading, cognitive or dexterity access needs;
- cannabis-industry worker / general public.

Industry participants must remain separately coded so familiarity does not inflate public recognition. The panel is qualitative and purposive, not representative.

Do not recruit minors. Parents and caregivers may discuss household interpretation and storage, but may not speculate on behalf of a child as if it were observed evidence.

## Exact session sequence

Sessions should last approximately 20–30 minutes and use the following order.

### 1. Unprompted package view

Show Condition B.

Ask:

1. “Tell me everything you notice.”
2. “What do you think this product is?”
3. “What, if anything, tells you that?”
4. “Is there anything you would want to know before handling or sharing it?”

Do not mention cannabis, THC, warning or symbol.

### 2. Unprompted serving set

Show Condition C at actual size.

Ask:

1. “What do you notice now?”
2. “Do the individual pieces communicate anything to you?”
3. “Is any mark or feature meaningful, decorative or unclear?”

Record whether the participant independently locates the symbol and the elapsed time to first mention. Timing is descriptive only.

### 3. Strong/weak randomized comparison

Show Condition D without labeling which is strongest.

Ask:

1. “Are these communicating the same thing?”
2. “What differences matter?”
3. “Would you interpret either one differently?”

### 4. Interpretation

Point to the symbol only after unprompted responses are complete.

Ask:

1. “What do you think this mark means?”
2. “What does it definitely tell you?”
3. “What does it not tell you?”
4. “Does it tell you how much THC is present?”
5. “Could someone reasonably mistake it for something else?”

### 5. Clean-symbol recognition

Show Condition A only now.

Ask:

1. “Have you seen this before?”
2. “Where?”
3. “What would you call it?”
4. “Does seeing the clean version change how you interpret the mark on the food?”

### 6. Scale, contrast and lighting

Show Conditions E and F.

Ask:

1. “At which size and lighting condition can you first detect it?”
2. “At which can you identify it?”
3. “What disappears first?”
4. “Would color alone help you?”
5. “What would make the mark clearer without requiring you to know it already?”

### 7. Dose adjacency

Show Condition G.

Ask:

1. “What relationship, if any, do you think exists between the mark and the number?”
2. “Does the symbol itself communicate dose?”
3. “What might someone incorrectly assume?”

### 8. Handling and action

Use photographs unless an approved inert replica exists.

Ask:

1. “Imagine you found this piece outside its package. What would you do?”
2. “Would the mark change whether you ate, stored, discarded or showed it to someone?”
3. “Could you separate and inspect this item comfortably?”
4. “What would be difficult with limited vision, grip or hand movement?”
5. “What information would still be missing?”

### 9. Closing correction

Explain that the Nevada mark indicates cannabis/THC content but does not by itself establish dose, safety, quality, freshness or legality. Confirm that all test items were images or nonconsumable replicas. Invite corrections and withdrawal of any quotation.

## Observation sheet

For each participant, preserve:

- participant code, not name;
- cohort attributes voluntarily provided;
- condition order and randomized pair order;
- unprompted symbol detection: yes / no / uncertain;
- first interpretation, verbatim;
- cannabis/THC recognition: correct / partial / incorrect / no inference;
- dose inference: accurate / unsupported / unclear;
- strongest and weakest mark comparison;
- scale and lighting threshold observations;
- access or handling barriers, in the participant's words;
- stated action after out-of-package discovery;
- moderator prompts or deviations;
- quotation permission: named / anonymous / paraphrase only / no publication;
- withdrawal or correction status.

Do not reduce the record to a single success rate.

## Coding framework

### Detection

- D0: no mark noticed
- D1: mark noticed only after prompting
- D2: mark noticed unprompted but treated as decorative/ambiguous
- D3: mark noticed unprompted as information-bearing

### Recognition

- R0: no meaning
- R1: incorrect meaning
- R2: general warning or regulated-product meaning
- R3: cannabis/THC meaning

### Dose inference

- M0: makes no dose inference
- M1: knows symbol alone does not state dose
- M2: uncertain relationship
- M3: incorrectly treats symbol as a dose quantity

### Action

- A0: no reported effect
- A1: seek package/context
- A2: avoid ingestion or sharing
- A3: change storage/disposal/notification behavior

These codes organize the qualitative record; they are not validated psychometric scales.

## Accessibility audit

The test must explicitly examine:

1. **Color independence:** Can the mark be detected without color? Molded food may be monochromatic.
2. **Relief and edge fidelity:** Does the manufactured imprint retain enough shape to distinguish it?
3. **Scale:** What is the mark's real width and height on the serving—not merely in the official PDF?
4. **Lighting and glare:** Does relief disappear under low light, high glare or translucent material?
5. **Texture and deformation:** Do sugar coating, bloom, breakage, melting or surface texture obscure it?
6. **Dexterity:** Can a person with limited hand use expose, orient and inspect the serving without unreasonable effort?
7. **Low vision:** Does magnification help, and is a package-level text alternative available?
8. **Blind access:** Is equivalent nonvisual information available on the package or through an accessible digital route? A shallow molded mark must not be described as Braille or tactile labeling without proof.
9. **Cognitive load:** Must a person already know the symbol to interpret it?
10. **Language:** Does the mark operate independently of English, and what essential information still depends on text?

RN's color-vision difference and limited hand use are design requirements, not optional edge cases. RN may participate in a private accessibility walkthrough, but her observations cannot stand in for every disabled user.

## Pass, revise and stop rules

### Procedural pilot passes only if

- participants understand the task without being led;
- actual-size conditions display consistently;
- the moderator can preserve an unprompted phase;
- no material is mistaken for something consumable;
- access accommodations do not reveal the correct answer;
- notes and recordings, if separately authorized, can be securely linked to consent.

### Revise before the field panel if

- the package itself reveals the answer before the serving test in a way that defeats the research question;
- image scaling varies by device;
- the strongest imprint is inadvertently privileged;
- the question sequence teaches the symbol too early;
- the inert replica does not faithfully reproduce the mark;
- participants confuse the test object with actual cannabis.

### Stop and do not publish conclusions if

- the actual product cannot be reconciled;
- fewer than three nonindustry public perspectives remain after exclusions;
- disability observations are secondhand rather than directly consented;
- recruitment is dominated by cannabis professionals;
- quotation permissions are missing;
- the evidence would require identifying sensitive health or cannabis-use information;
- results are being framed as statewide prevalence or causal effectiveness.
