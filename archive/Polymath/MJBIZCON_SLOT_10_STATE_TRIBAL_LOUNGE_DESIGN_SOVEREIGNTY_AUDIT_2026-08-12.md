# MJBizCon 2026 Canonical 14 — Slot 10 State/Tribal Lounge Design-Sovereignty Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026 HST  
**Proposition entering audit:** *One Las Vegas Lounge Needs Negative Pressure. Another Uses the Desert Sky.*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY ONLY AS A COMMUNITY-GATED COMPARATIVE DESIGN-SOVEREIGNTY TERRITORY; THE BINARY HEADLINE FAILS; DOSSIER AND DRAFTING REMAIN BLOCKED.**

## Controlling correction

The comparison is real and potentially one of the slate's most distinctive stories, but the inherited headline overstates both sides.

- Nevada's state lounge framework uses separated smoking and nonsmoking spaces, negative pressure, exhaust, filtration and monitoring as a baseline architecture, but approved alternative devices and professional-engineer waivers exist. No individual state lounge can be said to “need negative pressure” without its approved facility plan.
- The Las Vegas Paiute Tribe's current NuWu materials describe more than an uncovered courtyard. Sky High is marketed as a 16,000-square-foot indoor/outdoor lounge, while the NuWu Courtyard is a three-acre outdoor space with a Dab Bar, events and weather-dependent hours. “Uses the desert sky” is evocative but incomplete.
- Open air is not the absence of technology or governance. Shade, heat, wind, weather closure, lighting, power, service equipment, security, access, crowd movement, fire/emergency response, sanitation and worker judgment all remain designed systems.
- State sources cannot define the Tribe's law, authority, design purpose or cultural meaning. Tribal/operator public materials establish present facts, but they do not substitute for Las Vegas Paiute governmental authority and appropriate community review.

## Revised proposition

**Preferred working headline:** *Two Las Vegas Cannabis Lounges Put Smoke Under Different Systems.*

**Preferred deck:** Nevada's state framework begins with pressure zones, exhaust and particulate monitoring; the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe opened indoor/outdoor and courtyard consumption under its own authority. The contrast is not regulation versus freedom—it is two governments designing public consumption differently.

**Alternate after facility evidence:** *One Las Vegas Lounge Controls the Room. Another Opens the Courtyard.*

Do not use “design sovereignty” as decorative language. It must be grounded in the Tribe's own description of authority, decisions, goals, technical standards and lived operation.

## Four-lens discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The two places are genuinely specific:

- a currently operating Nevada state-licensed lounge selected from DAZED! at 2548 West Desert Inn Road or Society Elevated at 4640 Paradise Road, Suites 8–9, after its approved design is recovered; and
- NuWu/Sky High/Courtyard at 1235 Paiute Circle on Las Vegas Paiute land, described by its operator as an indoor/outdoor lounge and three-acre open-air Courtyard.

The state-side physical system may include sealed/self-closing boundaries, separated air zones, negative pressure or an approved alternative, direct exhaust, air changes, filtration, CO2/PM2.5 monitoring and alarms, coupled with dosing, service and exit-risk controls.

The tribal-side system must be documented rather than inferred. Current operator materials establish an outdoor Courtyard, outdoor Dab Bar, food trucks, events, open seating/standing, age/receipt rules, designated consumption areas and weather-dependent operation. They do not yet establish the Tribe's air-quality, worker-exposure, heat, wind, fire, accessibility, incident, security or transportation standards.

### 2. Institution + inventions

The state system derives from AB 341, CCB regulations, local approvals, professional engineering, inspection and state licensing. The tribal system exists through the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe's inherent sovereign authority, its institutions—including the Las Vegas Paiute Cannabis Authority as named in current NuWu materials—and any applicable tribal law, federal framework and intergovernmental agreement.

Nevada did not invent negative pressure or outdoor hospitality. The Las Vegas Paiute Tribe did not invent courtyards, dab devices or event venues. The technological and cultural story is institutional choice: two governments assemble different building, environmental, product, labor and behavioral controls around the same activity in the same metropolitan climate.

The state compact is relevant only to the extent its actual terms govern cross-jurisdictional cannabis administration. It must not be treated as the source of tribal sovereignty or automatically as the legal basis for the lounge.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- 2017: NuWu Cannabis Marketplace opens on Las Vegas Paiute land; exact enterprise chronology requires tribal confirmation.
- November 2019: NuWu's own current materials state that the Las Vegas Paiute Cannabis Authority opened Nevada's first legal cannabis tasting/consumption venue.
- Early 2020: NuWu says the venue closed during the pandemic.
- 2021: NuWu reports reopening; Nevada separately enacts AB 341 authorizing state-licensed lounges.
- 2022–2023: Nevada builds and modifies its technical lounge framework.
- 2023 onward: current Sky High materials describe a later lounge iteration with indoor and outdoor space; confirm exact opening and continuity.
- 2024: the first state final lounge license/opening follows, years after the tribal venue.
- 2026: NuWu advertises current Sky High and Courtyard operation and dated outdoor events; Nevada lists three active state lounge licenses, with two current public operations evidenced in the Slot 9 audit.

The comparison must follow parallel operating cycles: entry/age authentication → product acquisition and information → service/device preparation → designated consumption place → environmental/worker controls → observation/intervention → disposal → departure. Differences may only be reported from each authority's and venue's own evidence.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

Both systems involve government officials, designers, engineers, builders, HVAC or outdoor-environment specialists, product staff, hosts, dabtenders/servers, security, cleaners, event workers, artists/performers, food vendors, emergency responders, transportation workers and patrons.

The NuWu Courtyard also makes climate part of the operating system. Extreme heat, cold, wind, dust, rain and smoke from regional fires may affect hours, comfort, exposure and accessibility. Outdoor dilution may reduce accumulation relative to an enclosed room but does not establish zero secondhand exposure or worker protection. Shade and cooling may create their own energy and equipment systems. These are reporting questions, not conclusions.

The present consequence is that visitors encounter different legal and designed environments within Las Vegas. That is not evidence that one is universally safer, freer, more authentic or more effective.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology/system:** comparative governance-through-design—how two sovereign governments and their operators organize air, climate, product, service, labor, safety and movement around public cannabis consumption.
2. **Tangible technology/object/process:** state lounge approved airflow/pressure plan, doors/exhaust/filters/monitors/alarms; tribal lounge/courtyard site plan, shade/weather systems, Dab Bar equipment, designated-area boundaries, age/receipt access, service devices, sanitation, event and emergency infrastructure. Each object must be facility-specific and authority-validated.
3. **People and labor:** Las Vegas Paiute officials and cannabis-authority personnel; state CCB/local officials; architects/engineers; builders/technicians; hosts, servers/dabtenders, security, cleaners, performers/event/food workers, emergency/transportation workers and patrons.
4. **Place-specific history:** the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe opened a legal tasting/consumption venue in 2019, before Nevada authorized state lounges in 2021 and before the first state final license in 2024; both systems now operate in metropolitan Las Vegas.
5. **Present-day consequence/continuation:** indoor/outdoor tribal and state-licensed lounge models coexist, giving public consumption different physical and jurisdictional forms. Their outcomes and governing correspondence remain to be documented.
6. **Concurrent/noncausal temptations:** tribal operation did not necessarily cause AB 341 or state engineering rules; state delay does not prove hostility or borrowing; open air does not equal no controls or automatic safety; negative pressure does not equal protection; “Paiute design” cannot be inferred from a commercial courtyard; Las Vegas climate did not necessarily cause the Courtyard; chronological priority is not technical lineage.

## Final gate

**PASS PROVISIONALLY ONLY AS A COMMUNITY-GATED TERRITORY.** The story has exceptional place, culture, visual and technical potential. Its core is not that the state engineered a room while the Tribe merely used weather. It is that two sovereign governments and their operators designed public cannabis consumption through different physical, institutional and labor systems.

Drafting remains blocked until Las Vegas Paiute authority and terminology, tribal rules/standards, community review opportunity, permissioned site evidence, a matched state facility, comparable worker/environment/accessibility evidence and culturally lawful visual/audio routes are secured.
