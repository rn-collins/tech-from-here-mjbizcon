# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Slot 13 Tonopah Mass-Balance Public-Record Closure

**Closure date:** 13 August 2026  
**Candidate:** *The Extract Output Was Larger Than the Input*  
**Decision:** **FAIL AS A STANDALONE CANONICAL ARTICLE; VACATE SLOT 13**

## Outcome

The bounded public-record closure cycle recovered the official 17 April 2025 Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board agenda, approved minutes and meeting video, plus current-license and ownership-transition evidence. Those records close the suspension-to-correction chronology but do **not** close the proposed article's decisive mass-balance evidence.

The 2023 CCB notice remains strong evidence that the Board said agents found extract output exceeding recorded utilized cannabis while investigating suspicious Metrc activity. The 2025 proceeding establishes, however, that no complaint was drafted or served and that the negotiated respondent admissions were limited to two other violations: failure to follow an approved security plan and improper storage of cannabis/cannabis products. The public settlement presentation described production-run thresholds, seed-to-sale tracking, tagging, inventory control, harvest/waste information and inventory/sales reporting as **allegations**, not admissions or adjudicated findings.

The proposed title therefore overstates the evidentiary closure available for a standalone feature. Without the underlying Metrc packages, scale records, extraction-run inputs and outputs, unit conversions, yield assumptions, adjustments, waste records or settlement document, the workflow cannot explain the apparent impossibility, test competing explanations or show what the discrepancy ultimately proved. Generic extraction science would not repair that case-specific gap.

## Recovered official chronology

1. **1 November 2023, 6:14 p.m.:** CCB summarily suspended The Hempire Company LLC's cultivation and production licenses, citing a present threat to public health and safety.
2. **2 November 2023:** CCB publicly stated that agents investigating suspicious Metrc activity and insufficient camera coverage found extract output exceeding cannabis utilized, missing packages, camera failures, uncontrolled drying and other deficiencies. It said failed or untested material **may** have been combined with passing lots. The notice required an approved correction plan before operations could resume.
3. **March 2024:** According to Mystic Holdings' later SEC filing, Qualcan LLC entered a management-services arrangement for the facility. This is issuer disclosure, not the CCB's adjudication of the 2023 allegations.
4. **27 December 2024:** The CCB approved transfer of the licenses previously held by The Hempire Company in receivership to Tonopah Farm LLC, according to Mystic's SEC filing. The filing says closing occurred 6 January 2025.
5. **17 April 2025:** CCB considered Case No. 2023-031. The official presentation said no complaint had been drafted or served. It described the wider case as allegations and presented two admissions, a $30,000 civil penalty, an approved correction plan and lifted suspensions. The Board unanimously approved the settlement.
6. **July 2026:** CCB's active-license workbook lists C184 and P120 active under Tonopah Farm LLC. Current operators must not be associated with the alleged 2023 conduct.

## What the official meeting record establishes

Official video, approximately **27:54–35:43**:

- the investigation concerned the facility under prior ownership;
- Tonopah Farm was not operating the licenses when the alleged violations occurred;
- the late-2023 audit and investigation resulted in summary suspension;
- no complaint was drafted or served;
- the case included allegations concerning security, production-run thresholds, seed-to-sale tracking, tagging, harvest and waste information, inventory control and inventory/sales reporting;
- the respondent admitted one Category 3 violation for C184—failure to follow an approved security plan—and one Category 4 violation for P120—improper storage;
- the parties agreed to $30,000 in civil penalties, divided equally between the two licenses;
- CCB staff approved a correction plan as a precursor to lifting the suspensions; and
- counsel stated that Tonopah Farm had invested close to $6 million after taking over operations and had brought the licenses out of suspension. Treat the investment amount as counsel's statement at the meeting, not an independently audited CCB finding.

The approved minutes confirm the settlement, correction-plan approval, lifted suspensions and Board vote, but do not reproduce the mass-balance evidence or settlement paragraphs.
