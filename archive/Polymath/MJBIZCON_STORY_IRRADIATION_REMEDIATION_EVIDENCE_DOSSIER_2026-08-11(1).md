## Editorial decision at this checkpoint

This candidate survives the technology-centrality test. Its subject is a coupled biological, physical, analytical, and data system:

1. a Nevada cannabis lot fails a specified quality-assurance test, including microbial screening;
2. a licensee may seek Board-agent approval to remediate the lot or send it to extraction;
3. a remediation device applies a physical or chemical process intended to reduce or inactivate microorganisms;
4. the processed lot must pass all required quality-assurance tests;
5. its identity and status must remain connected across physical tags, equipment logs, Metrc, Accela, laboratory results, and packaging; and
6. if ionizing radiation was used, current NAC 678B.626 requires an irradiation notice and Radura symbol on every package.

Nevada did not invent gamma irradiation, X-ray treatment, radio-frequency heating, ozone treatment, or microbial testing. Its distinctive story is the documented control chain around a machine-altered post-harvest product—and the state’s continuing argument over what consumers should be told about that alteration.

The candidate is stronger than the earlier comparison suggested because a May 2026 CCB complaint describes whole cannabis flower physically inside a RadSource machine and connects it to a missing Metrc tag, a missing RadSource-log entry, and an Accela remediation-status entry. That is a rare Nevada-specific object/process/data case. It is a regulator’s allegation, not an adjudicated finding, and must be reported that way.

The candidate is **retained for comparison but not locked**. Visual feasibility improves from `V?` to **amber-plus**: ten evidence-bearing slide assignments can now be identified, but zero slides are produced and several essential items remain rights-uncleared or require permission.

## 1. Revelation and falsifiable version

### One-sentence revelation

In Nevada, a cannabis lot can fail a microbial test, enter a machine that changes its microbial load, and later pass—but the state treats that transformation as a chain of approvals, retesting, tags, logs, software states, and, for irradiation, consumer-facing labeling.

### Falsifiable version

The story fails or must be narrowed if current Nevada law no longer permits the described remediation route; if NAC 678B.626 has been repealed or superseded before publication; if the May 2026 complaint does not contain usable visual evidence or is later dismissed on the relevant facts; if specialist review shows that the draft collapses fundamentally different technologies into one mechanism; or if reporting cannot establish how a real Nevada operator moves a lot through approval, treatment, retesting, and release.

### Recommended provisional headline

**The Machine Between a Failed Microbial Test and a Passing Cannabis Lot**

This headline states the technological transformation without claiming that treatment guarantees safety, that all remediation is irradiation, or that every treated lot originally contained visible mold.

### Other qualified headlines

3. **A Passing Cannabis Test Can Come After the Product Has Changed**

### Unsafe variants

- “The Machine That Makes Moldy Cannabis Safe.” A microbial reduction is not a universal safety guarantee; toxins, dead organisms, post-treatment contamination, sampling, and process limits require specialist treatment.
- “Nevada Lets Growers Hide Irradiation.” Current NAC requires irradiation labeling; a 2026 proposal to repeal it is not current law.
- “All Remediated Cannabis Is Irradiated.” False. Nevada records discuss radio frequency, ozone, and radiation-based devices among possible approaches.
- “Irradiation Makes Cannabis Radioactive.” False and scientifically misleading.
- “Nevada Invented Cannabis Remediation.” Unsupported.
- “A Passing Test Proves the Whole Lot Is Clean.” Sampling supports a bounded inference; it does not directly test every gram.
- “Remediation Erases Mold.” Different processes may inactivate or reduce viable organisms without removing every organism, residue, toxin, or underlying cultivation problem.

## 3. Current Nevada mechanism

### Operative law located on 11 August 2026

- [NAC 678B.569](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-678B.html) permits, with approval of the appropriate Board agent, a lot or production run that fails residual-solvent, pH, water-activity, homogeneity, or microbial-screening testing to be remediated or used to make an extract. After processing, the remediated lot or extract must pass all required quality-assurance tests.
- The same section bars a retest request if the lot or run has undergone remediation since the initial samples were taken. Treatment therefore is not merely a second measurement of unchanged material.
- [NAC 678B.626](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-678B.html) requires packaging of cannabis treated with radiation at any time to bear a bold irradiation notice and the Radura symbol.
- The CCB’s [laws-and-regulations page](https://ccb.nv.gov/laws-regulations/) now describes the NCCR PDFs as historical and points to the NAC as the current codified source.

### Pending change that must not be called current

The [28 May 2026 revised proposed regulation R152-24](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Register/2024Register/R152-24RP1.pdf) would repeal NCCR 12.065/NAC 678B.626’s irradiation-label requirement. The located document is expressly titled **Revised Proposed Regulation**. A proposal is evidence of a live policy dispute, not proof of repeal. Final status must be recaptured immediately before drafting and publication.

## 5. Technical mechanism

### The necessary distinctions

- **Microbial screening** measures specified organisms or microbial indicators under a laboratory method and action limit.
- **Remediation** is the regulatory disposition/process category for changing a failed lot so it may be tested again or used differently after approval.
- **Decontamination** describes reducing or inactivating contamination; it is not necessarily identical to Nevada’s legal use of “remediation.”
- **Ionizing irradiation** includes gamma rays, X-rays, and electron beams. These carry enough energy to ionize matter and can damage microbial DNA or cellular machinery.
- **Radio frequency** is non-ionizing electromagnetic energy. It heats material volumetrically under controlled conditions and can reduce viable microbial populations through thermal effects.
- **Ozone and other processes** act through different chemical or physical mechanisms and cannot be explained as if they were radiation.
- **Retesting after remediation** measures the treated material. A later passing result does not retroactively mean the untreated lot passed.

### Physical–digital chain

| Stage | Physical object/process | Data/document object | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial lot | harvested and processed flower or product | lot/package identity; Metrc tag | identity mismatch or untagged material |
| Initial sampling | laboratory collects representative sample | chain of custody; sample ID | nonrepresentative sample or handling error |
| Initial test | microbial culture/PCR or other approved method | CoA; pass/fail state | method uncertainty, contamination, transcription error |
| Approval | failed lot held for authorized disposition | Board request/approval; Accela entry | treatment before approval or unclear status |
| Treatment | RF, X-ray/gamma/e-beam, ozone, or another approved process | equipment cycle/log; operator record | wrong dose, uneven exposure, missing log, recontamination |
| Re-sampling/testing | treated lot sampled again | new CoA and linked history | treating the result as if material were unchanged |
| Release/label | product packaged for transfer or sale | Metrc state; irradiation label where applicable | missing disclosure, broken tag lineage, discordant records |

### What the research does and does not show

- Hazekamp’s 2016 study of four medicinal-cannabis varieties found gamma irradiation did not change THC/CBD or moisture in that sample set but reduced some terpenes. This is evidence about those varieties and conditions, not a universal no-effect guarantee.
- Jerushalmi et al. (2020) found gamma, electron-beam, and cold-plasma treatments significantly reduced fungal colony-forming units under tested conditions. Method, dose, organism, moisture, density, packaging, and starting load matter.
- A process that reduces viable counts is not automatically a substitute for good cultivation/manufacturing practices. The canonical article needs an independent microbiologist or post-harvest specialist to address dead organisms, mycotoxins, regrowth, recontamination, and what a standard microbial assay can and cannot reveal.

## 6. Nevada’s strongest located case object

The [May 2026 CCB complaint](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Complaint-Vegas-Valley-Growers-C006-Final.pdf) alleges that inspectors observed whole cannabis flower inside a RadSource machine without an identifying Metrc tag and absent from the RadSource log. The complaint says staff described it as a failed lot being prepared for remediation through a Remediation Status Entry in Accela.

This record is editorially valuable because it makes the system visible:

> flower → machine → physical tag → equipment log → Metrc identity → Accela status

But it may be used only with these constraints:

- call it an allegation in a filed complaint, not a proven violation;
- obtain and review the respondent’s answer, any amended complaint, settlement, dismissal, order, or hearing record;
- do not imply the machine itself caused the alleged tagging/logging failure;
- do not imply the lot was sold, passed, unsafe, or concealed unless the final record proves it;
- redact or avoid any security-sensitive or personally identifying material; and
- conduct rights/legal review before reproducing complaint images.

## 8. Competing interpretations

1. **Safety-tool interpretation:** controlled treatment can reduce viable microbial load and salvage product that can subsequently meet required tests.
2. **Process-control interpretation:** remediation is one control in a larger system, not a substitute for cultivation sanitation, drying, storage, or GMPs.
3. **Consumer-choice interpretation:** disclosure allows a buyer to distinguish irradiated from nonirradiated product.
4. **Stigma interpretation:** warning-like language may cause consumers to mistake irradiated product for radioactive product or infer danger not established by the science.
5. **Transparency critique:** a final passing CoA can obscure the temporal fact that the tested product was physically changed after an earlier failure unless the history remains visible.
6. **Waste/capital interpretation:** remediation can preserve inventory, labor, and revenue that destruction would erase; that incentive heightens the importance of validation and audit trails.
7. **Equity interpretation:** expensive equipment or outsourced treatment may advantage larger operators while disclosure rules may distribute stigma unevenly.

The article must not begin from “remediation good” or “remediation bad.” It should show what the machine changes, what the test measures, what the records preserve, and what the consumer can actually know.

## 9. Contradictory evidence and unresolved questions

- The current NAC requires irradiation labeling, while R152-24 proposes repeal. The proposal cannot be treated as operative.
- Nevada’s legal “remediation” category is broader than irradiation. Packaging disclosure is clearest for radiation, not necessarily every non-ionizing or chemical treatment.
- Vendor claims describe high pass rates and minimal product effects, but independent peer-reviewed studies are method- and condition-specific.
- A viable-count reduction does not by itself answer whether toxins, allergens, chemical changes, sensory changes, or later contamination remain.
- No public Nevada dataset was located showing how many lots fail microbial screening, how many are remediated, which technologies are used, how many later pass, or how often labels disclose irradiation.
- No complete public named-lot chain was located linking initial CoA, remediation approval, equipment log, Metrc/Accela entries, retest CoA, final package, and sale.
- The May 2026 complaint supplies a named enforcement case but not yet its defense or final disposition.
- Current CCB operational guidance for remediation-status entry and equipment logs has not been located in a public manual.
