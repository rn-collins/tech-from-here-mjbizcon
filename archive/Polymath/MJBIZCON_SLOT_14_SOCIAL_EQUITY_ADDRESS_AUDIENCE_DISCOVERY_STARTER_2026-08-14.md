## Editorial correction before any further work

“Address” is a useful headline shorthand, not the whole eligibility rule. The story must explain the complete decision structure. Nevada required the applicant entity to meet ownership/control conditions and a qualifying person to satisfy a conviction/family-history route or a residence route. The residence route connected evidence of five years’ residence to a census tract and then to specified socioeconomic and incarceration datasets. The 2024 problem publicly reported by the Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board concerned a miscalculated incarceration-rate component. Do not imply that a street address alone conferred eligibility, that every social-equity applicant used the same route, or that geography by itself measured individual harm.

## 2. Pain points

Frequency ranking is **withheld** because there is no qualifying audience corpus. The following are research hypotheses derived from the official sequence, not findings about what the audience says:

1. **Eligibility can change after selection.** A person or entity may commit time and money before a later audit changes the result.
2. **A proxy can be hard to inspect.** Applicants need to know which address, tract, dataset vintage, threshold and calculation were used.
3. **Proof burden can be substantial.** Five years of residence evidence can require bills, statements or other records that not everyone retains consistently.
4. **Correction creates two different harms.** Six selected applicants lost their path while eight previously undrawn applicants learned they should have been included.
5. **A corrected pool still uses chance.** Technical eligibility only determines access to a random drawing; it does not guarantee a license.
6. **Privacy and transparency can conflict.** Reproducing a decision may require sensitive residence, ownership or conviction/family information.
7. **Geographic proxies can misclassify lived experience.** That is a question requiring applicant, community and expert evidence—not a conclusion established by the rerun alone.

## 3. Solutions and workarounds

No audience recommendation can be labeled widely recommended, contested or ineffective from the recovered corpus. Candidate administrative remedies to investigate are:

- publish the exact formula, thresholds, source datasets, vintages and change log;
- give applicants a privacy-safe preview of their tract classification before submission;
- preserve an appeal/correction path with reasons and deadlines;
- conduct independent pre-drawing validation and publish an audit protocol;
- retain versioned calculation artifacts so the original and corrected outcomes can be reproduced;
- offer document-assistance support without making informal assistance a hidden eligibility advantage.

These are inferred options, not evidence of what Nevada applicants requested or what Nevada law requires.
