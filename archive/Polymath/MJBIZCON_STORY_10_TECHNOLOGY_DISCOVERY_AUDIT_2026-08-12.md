# MJBizCon Story 10 — Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Prior working title:** *The Regulatory Perimeter Extends Beyond the Plant*  
**Decision:** **FAIL AS TECH FROM HERE; FORMALLY REMOVE FROM THE CANONICAL 14 AND PRESERVE FOR A SEPARATE LOCAL-LAW/REGULATION LANE.**

## Controlling correction

Clark County publishes a twenty-two-page application packet for a “Cannabis Support Business.” Its opening instruction says that a person operating a business whose purpose is to provide ancillary support to cannabis establishments or the cannabis industry at large must apply to operate within unincorporated Clark County. The packet expressly gives cannabis media, cannabis business consulting and medical-cannabis patient consulting as examples. It requires business/location information, an organizational flowchart, ownership disclosure, relevant agreements, financial records and a code conference; it says the department may request FBI background checks and requires public-safety signoff before the conference.

That is a striking local regulatory perimeter. It is not a central technology.

The required broad pass tested whether the real subject could instead be beneficial-ownership mapping, source-of-funds tracing, digital licensing, searchable public records, background-check infrastructure, supply-chain/vendor classification or a Nevada-specific compliance technology. None survives as an independent proposition. The county's online portal and license searches are generic administrative interfaces. The core mechanism remains legal classification followed by forms, document production, human investigation, inspections and discretionary review.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The place is specifically unincorporated Clark County and other communities processed by its Department of Business License—not the City of Las Vegas, all Clark County or all Nevada. The relevant physical sites include the Clark County Government Center at 500 South Grand Central Parkway, the applicant's business location, the Department of Business License, inspection agencies and the code-conference setting.

Candidate technologies include the county's jurisdiction lookup, zoning/address determination, online application/payment portal, license-status database and public searches by business name, owner, address, parcel or license number. These tools help people navigate the ordinary business-license system. The surviving evidence does not show that they were invented for cannabis support businesses, that this category produced a distinct technical architecture, or that the interface determines the substantive support-business classification.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Clark County's institutional process assembles several real information practices:

- jurisdiction and zoning determination;
- entity registration and business-name records;
- lease/deed and physical-location proof;
- description of services;
- organizational flowcharts and ownership percentages totaling 100 percent;
- management, employment, purchase and transfer agreements;
- source-of-funds, tax-return and bank-statement review;
- possible FBI background checks or use of a CCB agent card;
- public-safety inspections and agency signoff;
- a code conference and review of the business plan;
- licensing, renewal, audit, payment and public status records.

These practices can reveal hidden ownership and financing, but the packet does not document a novel beneficial-ownership database, graph-analysis system, automated risk score, cannabis-specific background-check platform or technical interoperability with state systems. An organization chart is a tangible documentary object, not evidence of a technological invention.

### 3. Period + products/processes

The available packet reflects Clark County's decision to create a local license category for businesses defined by their cannabis-support purpose. Its process blends older privileged/regulated-business financial questionnaires and investigation practices with cannabis-specific classification language. The financial questionnaire itself cites broad county licensing and investigative authority and asks applicants to trace ownership, capital, deposits, expenditures, liens, bankruptcy and foreign assets.

The historically meaningful development is regulatory extension: local government applied intensive disclosure and investigation practices beyond plant-touching establishments to at least some ancillary businesses. The current record does not establish the category's enactment history, changes, code-section trigger, advisory interpretations, application numbers, denials, enforcement or whether the packet remains uniformly applied in practice. More importantly for this series, it does not establish a technical development distinct from the law.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The ecosystem includes cannabis establishments; consultants, publications and patient consultants; landlords; investors and lenders; lawyers and accountants; the county licensing, zoning, audit and public-safety staff; the CCB; the Secretary of State; Department of Taxation; background-check authorities; inspectors; banks; applicants and workers.

The public consequence is that some businesses outside cannabis cultivation, production, testing, distribution or retail may face cannabis-specific local licensing and financial disclosure. The likely failure modes are category ambiguity, jurisdiction confusion, incomplete forms, inconsistent advice, privacy exposure, burdensome documentation, delayed investigations, mismatch between public-facing instructions and code, and uncertain treatment of ordinary vendors. Those are important administrative-law and governance questions. They do not become a technology story simply because records move through databases or portals.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** none. The central system is a local legal classification and licensing/investigation process.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** tested objects include the online portal, jurisdiction/zoning lookup, public license searches, ownership flowcharts, source-of-funds schedules, background checks and inspection records. None is shown to be a Nevada cannabis-specific technical breakthrough or a coherent technological system driving the proposition.
3. **People and labor:** support-business owners and workers; consultants and publishers; applicants and document preparers; county licensing, audit, zoning, public-safety and inspection staff; CCB agents; background investigators; lawyers, accountants, landlords, lenders and technology vendors. Their labor sustains the process but does not cure the missing technical subject.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** unincorporated Clark County created or administers a local cannabis-support-business category whose packet reaches specified non-plant-touching activities. That is place-specific regulatory history.
5. **Present-day technological consequence or continuation:** ordinary e-government tools support application, payment, status checking and public search. The evidence does not demonstrate that the support-business category caused those tools or materially changed their technology.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** the growth of cannabis compliance software, beneficial-ownership analytics, fintech, background-check services, e-signatures and online licensing occurred during the regulated market's growth, but the packet does not show that Clark County's category caused, adopted or transformed those technologies. A software vendor serving cannabis is not necessarily governed by the packet; a portal is not the regulatory perimeter; data collection is not automatically data science.

## Alternatives tested

### A. Keep the current “regulatory perimeter” article

**Reject for Tech From Here.** The central object is classification and local licensing law. This remains true even if the article explains the paperwork unusually well.

### B. Rebuild around beneficial-ownership technology

**Reject on current evidence.** The county requires ownership flowcharts and detailed financial disclosure, but no cannabis-specific graph system, automated analytics, interoperable registry or technical lineage is established. The strongest story would still be why the county demands the information.

### C. Rebuild around the online licensing and public-search portal

**Reject.** Clark County's portal supports ordinary licensing functions and explicitly has limited mobile support. Nothing located shows that cannabis support licensing produced a novel portal or public-data system. A portal audit could be useful public-service reporting but is too generic and thin for this slot.

### D. Rebuild around background-check technology

**Reject.** The packet says the department may request an FBI background check and may accept a CCB agent card instead. It does not document the identity-matching workflow, data exchange, error/correction mechanics or Nevada-specific technical development. Story 2 already owns a stronger identity/credentialing territory.

### E. Rebuild around support-business compliance software

**Reject as concurrent.** Cannabis compliance vendors may use specialized technology, but their products are not demonstrated descendants or components of Clark County's support-business licensing system. Selecting a vendor merely because it falls near the category would repeat the prohibited “place as scenery for generic technology” problem.
