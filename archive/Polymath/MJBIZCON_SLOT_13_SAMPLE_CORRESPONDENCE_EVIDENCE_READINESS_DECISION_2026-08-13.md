# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Slot 13 Evidence-Readiness Decision and Technology Audit

**Decision date:** 13 August 2026

**Decision:** **PROMOTE PROVISIONALLY: “THE SAMPLE PASSED. THE PRODUCT FAILED.”**

## Controlling proposition

**The Sample Passed. The Product Failed.**

Expanded proposition: **Nevada's laboratory result can govern a whole cannabis lot only if the material carried into the laboratory truthfully corresponds to the product sold from that lot. In September 2025, the CCB said products had been misrepresented during laboratory sampling: initial samples passed, secondary samples failed for yeast, mold and/or Aspergillus, and some lots were no longer available to retest. The technology story is the fragile chain that makes a small sample legally stand for a much larger product.**

This is the strongest evidence-ready replacement for Slot 13. It has an official seven-page bulletin naming products, batch/lot identifiers, Metrc package identifiers, sale dates and licensed retailers; current Nevada sampling/testing rules and logs; the historical Laboratory Library; and potentially recoverable initial/secondary records. It is promoted only provisionally because the public bulletin does not explain who misrepresented what, how agents detected it, which exact samples failed, or the status of any investigation or adjudication.

## Four-lens technology discovery

### Place + technology

The case is geographically Nevada-wide but concretely local. Bulletin 2025-01 states that affected uninfused pre-rolls and whole-flower packages were sold between 1 April and 29 August 2025 through identified licensed sales facilities, including multiple Las Vegas-area addresses as well as northern and rural Nevada locations. A small sample moved from a licensed lot into Nevada's laboratory system; the resulting pass traveled digitally and legally with products that reached retail shelves.

The place-specific system is not “a laboratory somewhere.” It is the chain linking a cultivation lot, the location and conditions of independent sampling, sealed custody and Metrc identity, a Nevada laboratory, secondary confirmation, specific licensed retailers and consumers searching a package label after the warning.

### Institution + inventions

The relevant technical system includes:

- lot definition and physical segregation;
- statistically/administratively representative sample selection;
- independent sampling labor and sampling plan;
- field log, sample identifiers, seals and chain of custody;
- Metrc batch, lot and package identifiers;
- microbial preparation and assays for yeast/mold and Aspergillus;
- laboratory information and certificate/reporting systems;
- release decision attached to the initial result;
- regulator detection of a correspondence problem;
- secondary confirmation sampling and comparison;
- inventory availability or disappearance before retesting;
- affected-package query, retailer tracing and public bulletin; and
- consumer label/package matching.

The central invention is representative sampling as a legal-technical substitution: a small amount becomes evidence about a larger lot. The September 2025 event demonstrates that instrument accuracy cannot rescue a sample that does not truthfully represent the product.

### Period + products/processes

The chronology presently proved is bounded:

1. named batches/lots were produced from late 2024 into spring 2025;
2. samples passed initial laboratory testing;
3. products entered packages and retail distribution;
4. CCB agents discovered what the Board described as product misrepresentation during lab sampling;
5. the CCB required secondary confirmation testing;
6. secondary results showed microbial failures involving yeast/mold and/or Aspergillus;
7. some lots could not undergo secondary testing and were listed as a precaution;
8. Bulletin 2025-01 was issued 12 September 2025, naming products, identifiers and sales facilities.

Do not add an actor, method, motive, date of discovery, illness, enforcement outcome or causal pathway unless a later primary record proves it.

### Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The system joins cultivation and packaging workers; independent samplers; couriers; laboratory accessioning, preparation, microbiology and QA staff; Metrc operators; CCB agents and analysts; retailer inventory/compliance workers; consumers and patients; and clinicians or public-health personnel concerned with microbial exposure.

Its present consequence is larger than one warning. Every Nevada test result rests on correspondence among physical lot, selected material, custody identity, analytical result and the packages released under that result. The story makes visible the people and records that keep those identities aligned.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology/system:** Nevada's sample-to-lot correspondence and microbial-release system, from lot definition and independent selection through custody, analysis, Metrc/COA representation, secondary confirmation and consumer warning.
2. **Tangible object/process:** one precisely selected affected batch/lot traced through its initial sample, initial result, retail package identifiers, secondary sample/result and Bulletin 2025-01 entry. Use sealed surrogate sampling materials for demonstrations; do not purchase affected cannabis.
3. **People and labor:** growers, samplers, couriers, laboratory accessioning/preparation/analysis/QA workers, CCB agents, retailer staff and consumers who must match a long identifier to a package.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada's regulated cannabis system uses independent sampling, Metrc identifiers and laboratory release decisions; the 2025 bulletin documents a real state-discovered breakdown spanning licensed production, testing and retail locations.
5. **Present-day consequence/continuation:** the named retail packages had already circulated; secondary testing reversed the evidentiary meaning of initial passes for some lots, while unavailable lots could not be confirmed.
6. **Concurrent/noncausal temptations:** an initial pass does not prove the laboratory instrument erred; a later fail does not by itself identify intentional fraud; “misrepresented during sampling” does not identify the actor or technique; unavailability does not prove destruction or concealment; a microbial result does not prove illness; a bulletin does not prove every unit was recovered or every consumer warned; identifiers enable tracing but do not guarantee correspondence.

## Nonduplication boundaries

- **Slot 4, measurement chain:** owns how a valid sample becomes an analytical classification in ordinary operation. Slot 13 owns the 2025 failure of sample-to-product correspondence and secondary contradiction.
- **Slot 5, laboratory manipulation:** owns adjudicated laboratory conduct involving retesting/reporting and official-looking certificates. Slot 13 must not describe the 2025 matter as laboratory fraud without an adjudicated record.
- **Slot 6, recall tracing:** owns the general reverse-traceability/incident-response system. Slot 13 may show where named packages went only to complete this incident; it cannot become another generic recall explainer.
- **Slot 3, Item Catalog:** owns public-interface coverage and upstream omissions. Slot 13 may use catalog/lab-library records as evidence, not retell the interface audit.

If these boundaries cannot survive outlining, merge the incident into Slot 4 and reopen Slot 13.
