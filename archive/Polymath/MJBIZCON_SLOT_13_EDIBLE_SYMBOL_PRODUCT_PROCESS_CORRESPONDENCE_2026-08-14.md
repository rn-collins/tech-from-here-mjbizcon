# Slot 13 — Product-to-Process Correspondence Audit

**Date:** 14 August 2026 HST  
**Proposition:** **Nevada Put the Warning on the Edible Itself**  
**Decision:** partial closure only; retain provisionally; drafting remains blocked  
**Publication state:** unpublished, unscheduled and unsent

## Bottom line

The public record now proves the architecture around a real Nevada edible:

- current Nevada law requires the serving-level mark for practical solid edible servings;
- the CCB publishes the official symbol and a form-specific exemption process;
- a licensed establishment must submit packaging for pre-approval;
- the exemption request requires a named product, explanation and photographs;
- approval must be kept at the production site and available for inspection;
- Nevada-specific commercial molds make the required mark through repeated cavities and production-scale filling/demolding;
- current Nevada dispensary menus identify specific 10-piece, 100-milligram gummy products; and
- historical reporting identifies a Nevada Wana licensee using molds and dispensary purchasing staff checking incoming products for the stamp.

The record does **not** yet close one item-level chain. No recovered public source simultaneously proves that a current named Nevada product, the pictured edible and mark, its package and compliance label, its producer/license, its packaging approval or stamp/mold exemption, its Metrc catalog item and its COA all correspond to one another. Brand and menu photographs frequently show only the outer package or use state-variable imagery. A supplier mold demonstrates a possible production technology, not the mold used by a named Nevada licensee.

Accordingly, the story survives, but the central correspondence gate remains red.

## Governing object-and-approval chain

| Stage | Current evidence | What is established | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Official mark | [Nevada universal-symbol document](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Nevada-Universal-Cannabis-Symbol.pdf) | The symbol must be stamped, molded or printed with color contrast on each individual single serving; milligrams may appear next to, but not within, the symbol | Does not identify a product or prove recognition/effectiveness |
| Serving rule | [NAC 678B.599](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-678B.html) | Practical single servings must bear the Nevada symbol; impractical products use individually marked wrappers; qualifying liquids are excepted | Rule text is not proof of actual compliance |
| Package system | [NAC 678B.596](https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nac/NAC-678B.html) | Package warnings, food-grade materials, serving demarcation/separability and child-resistant system | Does not tie a package to a specific approval record |
| Package approval | [CCB FAQ](https://ccb.nv.gov/faq/) and [customer-portal notice](https://ccb.nv.gov/cannabis-customer-portal-operational/) | Packaging is pre-approved; licensed establishments/designees submit through Accela | Approval files are not exposed in current public search |
| Impracticability decision | [CCB exemption form](https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Request-Exemption-from-Stamping-or-Molding-Oct-2023.pdf) | Request identifies establishment and product, explains impracticability, includes photographs; approval is retained for inspection | No completed product-specific request recovered |
| Equipment/menu changes | [CCB industry forms](https://ccb.nv.gov/industry/) | Facility/equipment/menu modifications may require CCB inspection request | Does not show a named mold or depositor approval |
| Public product identity | [CCB item catalog page](https://ccb.nv.gov/cannabis-item-catalog/) | Current catalog connects items to ingredients, allergens, potency, holds and downloadable COAs through Metrc | Public search did not yield an indexable item record containing the serving photograph or packaging approval |

## Tangible production technology recovered

Two Nevada-specific mold suppliers make the abstract rule physically legible:

1. [PJ Bold 192-cavity Nevada mold](https://www.pjbold.com/products/thc-candy-mold-nv): 192 four-milliliter square cavities; food-grade silicone; stated CAD and CNC micro-etching; compatible with gummies, chocolate, caramel and hard candy.
2. [Dark City Molds Nevada triangle mold](https://darkcitymolds.com/products/3ml-triangle-gummy-mold-nevada-thc-symbol-10mg): 182 three-milliliter cavities; hand-fill, flood-and-scrape and depositor compatibility; supplier-stated rapid demolding and long service life.

These listings support the existence of a real tooling class and reveal likely production operations: tool selection, cavity geometry, dosing/fill consistency, demolding, cleaning, wear and mark legibility. They do not prove that a Nevada licensee uses either product, that every supplier representation matches Nevada requirements, or that the mold alone controls cannabinoid dose.

### Important compliance discrepancy to investigate

The CCB's official symbol document says milligram text may appear **next to** the symbol but may not appear **within** it because the symbol cannot be modified. Supplier listings describe Nevada cavities using “10mg” alongside or as part of their geometry, and search images can make that spatial relationship ambiguous. Do not declare a supplier mold compliant from marketing text or a web image. Obtain CCB approval, scaled drawings and an actual imprint before publication.

## Labor chain now visible—but not yet reported

The evidence supports these roles as reporting targets, not as established facts about one current plant:

1. Product developer or compliance lead interprets the rule and submits packaging/marking materials.
2. Tool designer converts the official symbol into a cavity, stamp or food-safe transfer at edible scale.
3. Production staff prepare the formulation and operate filling/depositing and setting/cooling steps.
4. Demolding/finishing staff preserve mark legibility and segregate defects.
5. Quality/compliance staff inspect serving count, separation, symbol, package and label against approved records.
6. CCB staff review packaging or impracticability requests and may require inspection.
7. Distributor/retail receiving or purchasing staff check incoming products before sale.

The historical Wana/Euphoria report supports roles 2 and 7 in 2019. Current named-worker evidence is still required for every step used in the article.

## Required exact capture protocol

RN should not be asked to research, label or organize the evidence. The production team prepares the shot list and reconciles identifiers afterward. RN's field burden is limited to lawful purchase and a short controlled photograph sequence if she approves.

### Preferred purchase/capture target

**Wana Nevada Sunrise, 10-pack / 100 mg THC**, purchased from a currently licensed Las Vegas-area retailer after confirming the exact item appears in the Nevada Metrc catalog. If unavailable or if its servings do not visibly carry the mark, use a currently cataloged incredibles 10-pack only after the same pre-check.

### Required capture sequence

1. Retail receipt with price/payment information and unrelated personal data covered; preserve date, retailer and item name.
2. Sealed front, back, top, bottom and closure of package before opening.
3. Full compliance label in readable, glare-free light.
4. Package identifier(s), producer/license information, lot/package/batch references and QR/barcode without posting scannable personal data.
5. Open package with all servings visible and countable.
6. Each serving arranged in one plane, mark facing camera.
7. Macro image of one representative clear mark and one weak/ambiguous mark if present—never select only the best imprint.
8. Ruler or neutral scale beside, not covering, a serving.
9. Package closure after reopening to document the separate child-resistant component.
10. Any mismatch, missing mark, illegible mark, wrapper route or form-specific exception photographed without accusing the producer of violation.

Do not consume or alter the product before correspondence is complete. Follow lawful possession, transport, storage and disposal rules. Do not bring cannabis onto federal property or across state lines.
