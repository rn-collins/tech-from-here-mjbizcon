# MJBizCon Challenger — Dispensary ID-Scanner Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Candidate title:** *The ID Scanner at the Dispensary Is Not Merely Reading Your Birthday*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS AN EXCEPTIONALLY RELATABLE, TANGIBLE TECHNOLOGY, LABOR, ACCESS AND PRIVACY STORY. IT BECOMES A SERIOUS SLOT 13 CHALLENGER, BUT PROMOTION IS BLOCKED UNTIL AN ACTUAL NEVADA DEVICE-TO-DECISION-TO-DATA WORKFLOW IS RECOVERED.**

## Controlling finding

Nevada does not merely require a worker to glance at a date of birth. Current NRS 678B.545 requires a cannabis establishment agent, before a sale, to check a government-issued photo identification using a Board-approved identification scanner to determine the identification's validity. AB 342 codified that scanner requirement effective 1 January 2024 after it already existed in regulation.

The CCB's March 2024 scanner guidance specified that approved scanners must support:

- government-issued identification reading;
- 400-DPI high-resolution imaging;
- a scanning window large enough for passports;
- visible and infrared multi-wavelength illumination;
- **data and image capture**;
- a subscription service;
- near-infrared reading of ICAO-compliant documents;
- 1D and 2D barcodes, including 2D codes from paper and most mobile devices;
- magnetic stripes;
- authentication of foreign-national identification; and
- age verification.

The CCB's February 2026 superseding age-verification guidance adds a revealing fallback. If an ID will not scan or the scanner fails, workers must inspect security features such as ultraviolet markings, holograms, microprinting, tactile elements, laser engraving and infrared features; seek a matching secondary photo ID when authenticity remains uncertain; and manually log issuing authority, year of birth, age eligibility, secondary ID and employee/supervisor verification.

This proves a richer capture-and-authentication machine plus skilled human fallback. It does **not** prove that every scanner stores every image, that dispensaries transmit scans to the CCB or police, that a scan automatically enters a marketing/customer profile, or that all vendors implement the required functions identically. Those questions must be answered facility by facility.

## Strong proposition

**Nevada’s Dispensary ID Scanner Reads the Document, Not Just the Birthday.**

Alternate: **Before Nevada Sells You Cannabis, a Machine Tests the Story Your ID Tells.**

The inherited title remains usable only if the article immediately names the additional functions and avoids implying unproved retention or surveillance.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

Las Vegas makes document authentication unusually concrete. A visitor economy sends domestic licenses, passports, passport cards, tribal identification, military and immigration documents, Canadian provincial licenses and other credentials to the same cannabis counter. Nevada's accepted-ID list includes seventeen primary categories while excluding other foreign driver licenses and voter IDs. The front door therefore converts a global stream of documents into a local access decision.

The relevant place is the actual threshold of a licensed Las Vegas dispensary or consumption lounge, plus delivery, curbside and drive-through contexts requiring approved mobile scanners. A scanner at reception may occur before a later statutory scan/check at sale; actual facility practice must establish when, how often and for what separate purposes identification is scanned.

### 2. Institution + inventions

The technical system can combine:

- document imager/camera and 400-DPI optical capture;
- visible, ultraviolet and infrared inspection;
- 1D/2D barcode and magnetic-stripe readers;
- optical character recognition and machine-readable-zone parsing;
- document-template/security-feature comparison;
- expiration and age calculation;
- foreign/ICAO-document support;
- subscription-based reference data or software updates;
- mobile hardware for delivery/curbside/drive-through;
- operator interface, alert and override/fallback workflow;
- supervisor confirmation and manual exception log;
- possible connections to point-of-sale, queue, loyalty or customer systems—**only where facility-specific evidence proves them**.

The central invention is document authentication as a human-machine decision system. The device captures signals a worker cannot consistently inspect unaided, but the worker still compares face to photograph, handles ambiguous documents, interprets failure and bears responsibility for admission/sale.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- Nevada's 2017 adult-use regulations already required scanner-based age verification.
- Lounge rules later required scanner authentication before access and before sale, coupled with visual inspection of the birth date.
- AB 342 in 2023 codified the pre-sale scanner requirement in NRS 678B.545, effective 1 January 2024.
- March 2024 guidance specified demanding scanner features and required equipment requests through Accela.
- February 2026 guidance superseded prior accepted-ID guidance and formalized expired-ID and unscannable/scanner-malfunction procedures.
- Medical dispensaries also sit beside a distinct electronic verification-and-authentication system with statutory privacy protections. That system must not be conflated with every adult-use scan or vendor database.

The canonical workflow is: present document → optical/electronic capture → extract fields/signals → compare against document template/security features → calculate age/expiration → worker compares person/photo and reviews result → accept, seek secondary ID, escalate or refuse → complete sale/access or record a manual exception → apply retention/deletion/access rules.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The system includes customers and patients; reception, security, delivery and sales workers; supervisors and compliance managers; scanner manufacturers and subscription/reference-data providers; point-of-sale and loyalty vendors; CCB equipment approvers, inspectors and auditors; document-issuing governments; privacy/security personnel; and people whose credentials do not fit the system cleanly.

Its consequences reach age control, fraud detection, tourism, accessibility, immigration/privacy anxiety, worker discretion and exclusion. People with expired or damaged IDs, changed names or appearances, nonstandard documents, limited mobility, language barriers or privacy concerns may experience the boundary differently. Those are reporting questions, not established disparate-impact findings.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** a Board-approved document-authentication and age-decision system coupling multispectral capture, machine-readable data extraction, reference/template comparison, worker face/document judgment, exception handling and possible downstream data governance.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** one approved Nevada scanner model and software version, paired with a permissioned set of synthetic/test credentials showing normal approval, suspected-invalid warning, expired-ID exception and unscannable/manual fallback. Never scan or reproduce a real person's credential for publication.
3. **People and labor:** front-door agents, security staff, budtenders, delivery workers, supervisors, compliance teams, CCB approvers/inspectors, hardware/software/vendor staff and the consumers/patients whose identity and access are decided.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada moved scanner authentication from early adult-use regulation into statute and technical guidance while Las Vegas's international visitor traffic makes document variety central to ordinary cannabis access.
5. **Present-day consequence or continuation:** every regulated sale now requires Board-approved scanner verification; lounges and mobile sale/delivery contexts add access and operational variations; the state provides a human fallback when hardware or documents fail.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** scanning does not itself prove storage, state/police transmission, loyalty enrollment, facial recognition or identity theft; a failed scan does not prove a fake ID; equipment approval does not prove accuracy across demographic/document groups; more wavelengths do not eliminate human error; an accepted document does not prove lawful sale in every other respect; cannabis stigma does not prove actual misuse of scanner data.

## Nonduplication boundaries

- Slot 1 owns historical medical-patient credentialing and verification before retail supply. This challenger owns present-day document authentication at the commercial threshold.
- Slot 2 owns plant/package identity in Metrc; a customer's identification scan is not part of that data double unless actual integration is proved.
- Slot 3 owns public product/COA visibility, not customer identity.
- Slot 12 owns law-enforcement-accessible facility cameras. The scanner story cannot imply a combined surveillance system without proof.
- Slot 14 owns address/geospatial eligibility and allocation, not retail access authentication.
