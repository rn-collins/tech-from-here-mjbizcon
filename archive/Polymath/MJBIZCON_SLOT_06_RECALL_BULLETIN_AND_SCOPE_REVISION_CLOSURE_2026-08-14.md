# MJBizCon 2026 Canonical 14 — Slot 06 Recall Bulletin and Scope-Revision Closure

**Story:** *When a Recall Has to Find the Product*  
**Date:** 14 August 2026 HST  
**Decision:** **PARTIAL CLOSURE — retain; drafting remains blocked**

## What this pass closes

This pass tests whether Nevada's public record demonstrates a recall/advisory scope that can be translated from a source condition into consumer-checkable product identifiers and then revised as the investigation changes.

It uses only first-party Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board records for the factual chain. The 2023 workbook comparison was performed row-for-row after trimming blank-space differences; no fuzzy matching or inferred equivalence was used.

## 2023-01: exact public-list expansion

The CCB issued Bulletin 2023-01 on 19 January 2023 after identifying cannabis treated with unapproved ethephon at Clark Natural Medicinal Solutions. For usable cannabis, the public instruction was source-and-harvest-date based. For downstream products, consumers were told to match both **item** and **production run** on the label. The bulletin also separated hazard discovery from laboratory detection: it said ethephon was not on the required pesticide list and laboratory methods were not configured to detect it.

The CCB's 9 February update said additional products had been identified and supplied a revised workbook. Exact comparison of the two official workbooks shows:

| Public-list component | Initial | Updated | Net change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Infused edible/tincture item–production-run rows | 117 | 145 | +28 |
| Infused flower / infused pre-roll rows | 41 | 43 | +2 |
| Concentrate rows | 221 | 268 | +47 |
| **All item–production-run rows** | **379** | **456** | **+77** |
| Cannabis sales-facility rows | 104 | 105 | +1 |

No initial row was removed in the updated workbook under exact normalized comparison. The update therefore expanded the public scope by **77 listed item–production-run rows** and **one sales-facility row**. These are public-list rows, not a unit count, consumer count, sales count, recovered quantity or proof that every affected derivative was found.

The added rows span multiple downstream forms and brands. That supports the story's central mechanism: a cultivation-level condition can require reverse tracing into production runs, product forms and retail endpoints, and the public warning must translate internal lineage into identifiers a person can find on a package.

## 2025-02: exact public scope contraction

The original official PDF was recovered through the CCB WordPress media index under the filename `Public-Health-Bulletin-2025-02.pdf`; the amended PDF remains the document linked from the public guidance page. The original is dated 15 December 2025. The amended copy restates the underlying date as 12 December and says that on 19 December the CCB removed **eight packages** determined to be unaffected and permitted them to be sold.

Literal comparison resolves what changed while exposing an important terminology problem. The amendment removed **eight product-item entries**, but those entries collectively contained **50 listed 24-character identifiers**. No identifier was added. The removed entries were:

| Removed product-item entry | Listed identifiers removed |
|---|---:|
| Cultivate — Strawberry Milkshake Live Resin Cart 0.5g | 2 |
| Cultivate — Strawberry Milkshake Live Resin Cart 0.5g — TESTER | 2 |
| Cultivate — Velvet Skunk Live Resin Cart 0.5g | 2 |
| Pressure — Greyhound Live Resin Disposable 1g | 9 |
| Pressure — Greyhound Live Resin Disposable 1g — TESTER | 1 |
| Pressure — Strawberry Jack Live Resin Disposable 1g | 15 |
| Pressure — Strawberry Milkshake Live Resin Disposable 1g | 8 |
| Pressure — Velvet Skunk Live Resin Disposable 1g | 11 |
| **Total** | **50** |

The public sentence “remove 8 packages” therefore cannot safely be paraphrased as “remove eight package identifiers.” In the rendered list, eight item-level entries disappear and 50 identifiers disappear with them. The article should state both the agency's wording and the observable document diff, without assigning a technical meaning to “package” that the bulletin does not define.

## Claims now supported

- Nevada has publicly expanded an advisory list after identifying additional affected products.
- The 2023 expansion added 77 exact item–production-run rows and one retail-facility row, with no exact-row removals between the two official workbooks.
- Nevada's public matching instructions can shift with product type: source and harvest date for usable cannabis; item plus production run for processed products.
- A public advisory can be revised downward after further investigation; the 2025 amendment removes eight product-item entries containing 50 listed identifiers, while its prose describes the change as removal of eight packages.
- Traceability and detection are different systems. The 2023 record says the pesticide was outside the required laboratory screen and methods were not set up to detect it.

## Claims still prohibited

- “Nevada recalled every affected unit.”
- “The system found every derivative or consumer.”
- “All listed product was recovered, destroyed or returned.”
- “The 2025 amendment proves the original inclusion was negligent or erroneous.”
- “Only eight identifiers were removed” or any unqualified equation of the bulletin's “eight packages” with the 50 listed identifiers removed from the table.
- Any quantified exposure, illness, recovery, disposition or consumer-notification rate not supported by a separate record.
- “Recall” as a completed physical recovery when the recovered document is a public health and safety bulletin/advisory.

## Remaining evidence gates

1. Obtain an official definition or incident record explaining whether the bulletin's “eight packages” refers to eight item entries, eight source groupings or another internal scope object; do not infer it from the public table.
2. Reconstruct one deidentified source → harvest/package → downstream production run → retailer chain from official or permissioned records.
3. Establish the exact administrative-hold workflow and status changes used during the incident.
4. Recover quantities placed on hold, sold, returned, destroyed, released and unresolved, with definitions that prevent double counting.
5. Establish how retailers received and implemented the notice and how consumers encountered it; the requested 30-day posting is not proof of display or reach.
6. Obtain appropriate analytical-chemistry, regulatory, operator and public-health review before any causal or effectiveness language.
7. Complete rights-cleared visual, label-legibility, mobile, accessibility, LinkedIn, audio and final-package QA.

## Editorial decision

**Retain Slot 06.** The official record now proves a strong, tangible update mechanism: the public scope expanded by exact product/run rows in 2023 and contracted by eight product entries/50 listed identifiers in 2025. That is enough to preserve the proposition and build a claim ledger, but not enough to draft the canonical feature. The article still lacks one complete operational chain, hold/disposition accounting, a definition for the agency's “eight packages” wording and human implementation evidence.
