# MJBizCon Canonical 14 — Story 1 Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026 HST  
**Story examined:** `Nevada Put Medical Cannabis in Its Constitution`  
**Governing method:** Canonical 14 v2.1 four-lens technology-discovery and causal-classification gate  
**Evidence examined:** current Story 1 canonical package; pre-event structured outline; production tranche; corrected technology-centrality slate; Nevada constitutional, legislative, DPBH, and CCB public records  
**Publication authority:** None. Keep unpublished, unscheduled, unsent, and unuploaded to public destinations.

## Decision

**Remove Story 1 from the Tech From Here Canonical 14. Preserve the complete article as a strong Nevada cannabis law/history feature for another Polymath lane. Do not cosmetically relabel the constitution, ballot, or amendment process as technology.**

The broad pass located one genuine technological descendant—the confidential patient registry and card/verification system commanded by the amendment—but that mechanism belongs to the Story 2 territory and cannot rescue Story 1 without collapsing the distinction between the two articles. Later seed-to-sale tracking, Metrc, laboratory systems, public product records, and the 2026 Item Catalog are part of Nevada's later cannabis regulatory ecosystem. They are not shown by the current evidence to be technical descendants of the 1998–2000 ballot mechanism itself.

Story 1's strongest proposition remains legal and institutional: Nevada voters placed a legislatively conditioned medical-cannabis command in Article 4 of the state constitution after two statewide approvals. That proposition is accurate, consequential, place-specific, visually supportable, and worth publishing—but it is not a technology story.

## Four-lens discovery record

### 1. Place + technology

The pass tested these Nevada-specific technology possibilities:

- the official ballot booklet and statewide voting process;
- the constitutional amendment procedure requiring two approvals;
- the confidential patient/attendant registry and law-enforcement verification function required by Article 4, section 38;
- the registry identification card and later online/paper application workflows;
- later medical-establishment licensing, testing, seed-to-sale tracking, patient-sale identification, and public product/COA infrastructure.

**Finding:** the booklet is a real documentary object, and election administration uses technologies and labor, but the surviving article does not investigate ballot production, tabulation, voting machines, election information systems, or another distinctive Nevada technical mechanism. The two-election amendment procedure is constitutional procedure, not by itself technology.

The confidential registry is a real information system. DPBH states that Nevada's Medical Marijuana Cardholders/Caregivers Program was created in 2001 and processes applications for registry cards. The current system includes an online portal, paper/email routes, identity-document submission, card issuance, replacement, and record updates. That is the strongest technological story revealed by this pass.

### 2. Institution + inventions

Institutions examined conceptually and through surviving records:

- Nevada voters and election administration;
- the Legislative Counsel Bureau and official ballot materials;
- the Legislature and AB 453 implementation;
- DPBH and the Medical Marijuana Patient Cardholder Registry;
- the Department of Taxation and Cannabis Compliance Board as later program administrators;
- Metrc as the later seed-to-sale reporting platform.

**Finding:** no invention, instrument, technical standard, or distinctive operating technology is central to the current constitutionalization article. The Legislature's translation of the amendment into eligibility, forms, quantities, confidentiality, verification, and cards created administrative machinery, but the technical center is the registry/card system. Rewriting Story 1 around that machinery would absorb Story 2's most promising technological proposition.

### 3. Period + products/processes

The 1998–2001 territory contains tangible documents and processes:

- initiative petition and constitutional-amendment procedure;
- statewide ballot booklet and Question 9 text;
- vote administration and certification;
- physician advice and patient eligibility concepts;
- the mandated confidential registry;
- the 2001 statutory implementation process.

**Finding:** the ballot booklet is meaningful visual evidence of the legal decision, not proof of a central technology. The existing public evidence does not establish a Nevada-specific cannabis device, medical product, cultivation method, laboratory technique, supply technology, or election technology that drove the amendment story. Adding period-concurrent cannabis technologies would decorate the legal history rather than explain it.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

Present-day Nevada maintains:

- a Medical Marijuana Patient Cardholder Registry with online and paper/email routes;
- a separate cannabis-establishment regulatory system;
- Metrc reporting across licensed medical and adult-use establishments;
- patient-sale identification within inventory tracking;
- a public Item Catalog and laboratory-data infrastructure.

**Finding:** the patient registry is a documented continuation of the constitutional command to establish a confidential registry. The wider establishment, Metrc, laboratory, and catalog infrastructure is a later regulatory ecosystem. The amendment supplied legal authorization and obligation, but current evidence does not support presenting every later technical system as a causal descendant of Question 9.

## Causal-classification ledger

| Connection | Classification | Allowed treatment |

| Two statewide approvals → constitutional text | **Causal lineage** | The votes caused adoption through Nevada's constitutional process, subject to exact legal wording. |
| Constitutional registry command → 2001 patient-registry implementation | **Causal lineage / documented continuation** | AB 453 and DPBH program history support a direct implementation bridge. |
| 2001 registry → current cardholder registry/portal | **Documented continuation** | Treat as an evolving administrative information system; verify detailed version history before narrating technical evolution. |
| Constitutional amendment → licensed dispensaries and establishment regulation | **Contextual/legal lineage; technical causation unresolved** | The amendment required appropriate supply, but do not attribute the complete later commercial architecture to Question 9 alone. |
| Constitutional amendment → Metrc seed-to-sale tracking | **Contextual relationship; no demonstrated technical lineage** | Metrc later serves Nevada's medical and adult-use system. Do not claim the ballot designed or caused the platform. |
| Constitutional amendment → laboratories, product categories, COAs, public Item Catalog, or lounges | **Concurrent/later ecosystem only unless separately evidenced** | May appear as contrast showing historical change; never as implied technical descendants. |
| 1998–2000 medical-device or cannabis-product developments outside the verified Nevada record | **Concurrent only / rejected** | Exclude from the proposition unless specific transmission into Nevada's amendment or implementation is documented. |

## Alternatives considered

1. **Keep the current constitutionalization story in Tech From Here.** Rejected: the legal decision remains the subject.
2. **Treat constitutional language as “infrastructure” or “governance technology.”** Rejected: this would use systems vocabulary to evade the central-technology requirement.
3. **Rebuild around Nevada's voting or ballot technology.** Rejected on the current record: no distinctive technical mechanism, operators, failure modes, or consequence has been established, and it would displace the cannabis story.
4. **Rebuild around the confidential registry as protection, proof, and surveillance.** Technically promising, but it overlaps Story 2's registry/card/access territory and should be tested there rather than duplicated.
5. **Treat modern Metrc, laboratories, and catalog systems as the amendment's technological descendants.** Rejected as causal overreach on current evidence.

## Package consequences
