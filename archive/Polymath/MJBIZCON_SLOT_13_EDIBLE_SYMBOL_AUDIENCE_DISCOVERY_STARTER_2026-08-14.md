## 2. Pain points

Frequency ranking is **withheld** because no qualifying corpus exists. The following are research hypotheses only:

1. **The mark may be unnoticed.** A symbol molded in a small, textured or sugar-coated surface may not be salient.
2. **Recognition may not equal comprehension.** Seeing the mark may not tell a novice what it means or how much THC the unit contains.
3. **Serving geometry may be mistaken for dosing precision.** Physical separation does not prove uniform formulation or predictable individual response.
4. **Accessibility may be weak.** Low vision, colorblindness, tactile ambiguity, deformation or packaging glare may affect recognition.
5. **The evidence disappears during use.** Once servings are removed from the labeled package, the mark must carry more of the identification burden.
6. **Child resistance and adult usability can conflict.** A closure may burden people with limited hand strength or dexterity while still requiring secure storage after opening.
7. **Workers carry hidden inspection labor.** Mold fill, demolding, surface coatings, wrapper alignment and quality checks may determine whether the mark is legible.
8. **Visitors may not know Nevada’s symbol.** State-specific marks can create a recognition problem across jurisdictions.

None may be stated as frequent, common or experienced in Nevada until real evidence supports it.

## 3. Solutions and workarounds

No audience-derived solution can be labeled widely recommended, contested or ineffective. Candidate practices for investigation include:

- keeping every remaining serving in its original labeled child-resistant package;
- using high-contrast, sufficiently deep mold impressions rather than relying on a shallow surface mark;
- pairing the symbol with plain-language dose information on each individual wrapper where practical;
- testing recognition and opening/reclosing with older adults, low-vision users and people with limited hand strength;
- documenting production-line reject criteria for incomplete or obscured marks;
- teaching tourists and first-time purchasers what the Nevada symbol means at the point of sale.

These are hypotheses, not established Nevada practice or legal advice.
