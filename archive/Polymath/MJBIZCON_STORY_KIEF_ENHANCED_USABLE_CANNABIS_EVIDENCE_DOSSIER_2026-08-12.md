## 1. Selection finding

This candidate passes the technology-centrality and non-duplication tests. Nevada's May 14, 2026 guidance creates a specific physical-to-digital control chain for flower and pre-rolls enhanced only with kief:

> incidental resin separation → deliberate separation/manufacturing → tested kief lot → addition to flower → homogeneous distribution → new Metrc item category → Kief-Enhanced processing job → input/final weights and source-lot linkage → representative sampling → laboratory result

The surprising proposition survives, but the earlier title needs a legal-technical qualification. Nevada does not declare every cannabis concentrate to be flower. It classifies kief as usable cannabis and states that adding kief to another usable-cannabis item does not, by itself, create an infused cannabis product. At the same time, deliberately separating kief is treated as licensed production/manufacturing and the recombination must become separately visible in Metrc.

### Corrected story promise

**Nevada Lets Kief-Enhanced Flower Stay Flower—But Makes the Enhancement Legible.**

### Strong headline options

1. **Nevada Added Kief to Flower—and Made the Database Notice**
2. **When Is Stronger Flower Still Flower? Nevada Drew the Line in Data**
3. **The Cannabis Changed. Its Legal Category Did Not. Its Digital Identity Did.**

### Unsafe or rejected framings

- **“Nevada Added Concentrate to Flower—and Kept Calling It Flower.”** Too broad: it invites readers to generalize from kief to every concentrate.
- **“Nevada Calls Infused Pre-Rolls Flower.”** Wrong under the guidance: kief-only enhancement is expressly not treated as an infused product; products containing oil, distillate, resin, or another concentrate may follow a different category.
- **“Nevada Hid Stronger Cannabis in the Flower Category.”** Unsupported intent and overstates consumer visibility findings.
- **“Kief-Enhanced Flower Is Homogeneous.”** The guidance requires homogeneous addition; it does not prove every lot achieves it.
- **“The New Rule.”** These are Board guidance documents, not a newly enacted statute or regulation. Cite the underlying authority and describe the guidance accurately.

## 2. Falsifiable revelation

The story fails if current Nevada authority does not support all of the following:

1. kief is treated as usable cannabis;
2. adding only kief to usable cannabis does not by itself create an infused cannabis product;
3. deliberately separating kief is a licensed manufacturing/production activity;
4. kief-enhanced flower and pre-rolls must use specifically named Metrc categories;
5. the enhancement must be recorded as a processing job rather than a packaging act or inventory adjustment;
6. source materials, weights, finished output, testing, and representative sampling remain traceable; and
7. these requirements were effective June 1, 2026.

Nevada CCB's two May 14 guidance documents support all seven as the intended compliance architecture. They do not yet prove consistent implementation across operators and laboratories.

## 3. Primary evidence spine

### A. Cultivation/production guidance — controlling mechanism source

**Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, “Kief-Enhanced Usable Cannabis — Classification, Processing, and Testing Requirements,” May 14, 2026; effective June 1, 2026.**  
Source: https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Cultivation-Production-Retail-Guidance-Kief-Enhanced-Usable-Cannabis.pdf

It establishes:

- incidental trichome separation is distinguished from deliberate isolation or concentration;
- deliberate dry sifting, mechanical tumbling, ice-water separation, and similar collection is production/manufacturing;
- kief-enhanced products remain usable cannabis and are not considered infused products solely because of the kief;
- four new item categories: bulk flower, packaged flower, count-based pre-roll, and weight-based pre-roll;
- a required **Kief-Enhanced** processing job, production batch number, and recorded kief weight, cannabis/product weight, and combined final weight;
- lot-specific records linking source flower, source kief, and finished product;
- SOP updates, pre-use testing, homogeneous addition, entire-lot presentation, and random representative sampling;
- 15-pound kief-lot and five-pound enhanced-flower-lot ceilings.

### B. Laboratory guidance — observation and sampling source

**Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board, “Kief-Enhanced Usable Cannabis — Testing and Identification Guidance,” May 14, 2026; effective June 1, 2026.**  
Source: https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Lab-Guidance-Kief-Enhanced-Usable-Cannabis-1.pdf

- laboratories should verify product identification, document visible discrepancies, and may notify the Board;

## 4. The technical mechanism

### Expert-level account

The guidance creates a classification boundary based on process rather than appearance alone. Trichomes dislodged incidentally during harvesting, curing, trimming, packaging, transport, or routine handling do not independently constitute extraction. Intentional isolation or concentration through screening, tumbling, ice-water processing, or similar means is a manufacturing event requiring the appropriate production license and records.

When tested kief is then added to flower or a pre-roll, Nevada preserves the finished material's usable-cannabis classification but requires a separate item identity and a processing job. The record captures the kief input, flower/product input, combined output, production-batch identity, source-lot relationships, and test status. Physical distribution matters because a sample only represents a lot if the added material is reasonably dispersed and the laboratory can randomly sample the entire lot.

### General-reader account

Nevada drew two lines around the same golden dust. If resin glands fall off flower during ordinary handling, that is incidental. If a facility deliberately screens or tumbles the plant to collect them, it has manufactured kief. If that kief is mixed back into flower, the finished item may still be sold in Nevada's flower family—but the state now expects the database to remember that it was enhanced, how much material went in, which lots supplied it, and what the laboratory actually sampled.

## 5. The saveable framework — The Matter-to-Record Test

1. **Separate:** Did the trichomes fall incidentally or result from a deliberate isolation process?
2. **Authorize:** Did an appropriately licensed production facility perform deliberate separation?
3. **Qualify:** Was each kief lot identified, tested, and within the applicable lot limit?
4. **Combine:** Which flower/product lot received which kief lot, and in what weights?
5. **Distribute:** Was the kief added across the complete lot rather than concentrated in a favored portion?
6. **Re-identify:** Was the correct Kief-Enhanced item category and processing job used?
7. **Sample:** Did the laboratory receive the complete lot and select randomly representative material?
8. **Reconcile:** Do the production batch, source packages, output weights, COA, and sale item agree?

This framework adds a raw-plant classification and sampling lens to the series. It does not duplicate the edible correction loop.

## 6. Human, labor, and accountability map

The public guidance names roles, not people. The canonical story therefore requires real reporting from:

- a Nevada production worker or production manager who separates and/or distributes kief;
- an inventory/compliance worker who creates the processing job and reconciles weights;
- a Nevada independent testing-laboratory sampler or director who can explain complete-lot access and visible discrepancy handling;
- a retailer or item-catalog specialist who can show how the new category and COA reach the consumer; and
- a CCB or Metrc representative who can confirm implementation, category launch, correction protocol, and audit visibility.

Do not erase the workers who physically sift, weigh, mix, fill, clean, label, and reconcile the product. “Metrc tracks it” is shorthand for human-entered records and cannot be treated as autonomous proof.

## 7. What the public record does not prove

1. How many Nevada products or facilities use the new categories.
2. Whether all relevant licensees implemented the June 1 change correctly.
3. The exact licensed-user interface for the Kief-Enhanced processing job.
4. Whether the public item catalog visibly distinguishes these categories in every search result.
5. How operators demonstrate “homogeneous” distribution in practice.
6. What mixing equipment, sampling plan, variance measure, or acceptance threshold operators use.
7. Whether a laboratory can reliably infer added kief from appearance alone.
8. Whether pre-guidance ambiguity was accidental, negligent, or strategic.
9. Whether consumer-facing labels use “kief-enhanced,” “infused,” or another retail term consistently.
10. Whether the five-pound lot ceiling resolves representative-sampling risk.

### Retail-language conflict: confirmed as a reporting issue, not resolved

Current Nevada retail/educational pages still commonly describe kief-bearing pre-rolls under the broader consumer term **infused**. That vocabulary cannot be used to infer the product's legal or Metrc category. A retail item described as “kief infused” may include oil, distillate, resin, diamonds, or another concentrate in addition to kief and therefore may not be a kief-only item governed by the new usable-cannabis pathway.

Example of the broad retail vocabulary: https://greennv.com/learn/what-is-an-infused-pre-roll

**Consequence:** The canonical article needs a real package/ingredient record and COA, not a menu label alone.

A June 2026 discussion in r/vegastrees responded directly to the CCB guidance and questioned whether the new system can prevent collected kief from being selectively added back to flower lots to influence potency results. Commenters made conflicting claims about ordinary facility practices.
