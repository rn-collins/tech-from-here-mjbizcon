## Story 1 closure — constitutional chronology

### Cleared proposition set

1. The current Nevada Constitution, article 4, section 38, directs the Legislature to provide by law for specified medical use of cannabis and a registry system.
2. The 2000 official statewide ballot pamphlet identifies Question 9 as an amendment to the Nevada Constitution.
3. That official pamphlet states the proposal was previously approved at the 1998 General Election and required approval again in 2000 to become effective.
4. The constitutional text did not itself create a dispensary network or prove practical access.

### Draft boundary

The article may explain the two-election constitutional mechanism. It may not call the provision an unrestricted personal right, claim that constitutionalization supplied cannabis, or collapse Story 1 into Story 2's later access chronology.

### Primary authorities

- Nevada Legislative Counsel Bureau, *Statewide Ballot Questions 2000*, Question 9: https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Research/VoteNV/BallotQuestions/2000.pdf

## Story 7 closure — gaming lineage

### Direct design evidence

The evidence no longer depends on vocabulary resemblance:

- The enacted AB 533 digest states that section 54 created a five-member Cannabis Compliance Board “generally modeled after” the Nevada Gaming Control Board.
- CCB testimony on SB 49 described the new centralized regulator as intended to mimic how the Gaming Control Board regulates gaming.
- 2019 Senate Judiciary testimony described the framework as modeled after the Gaming Commission and Gaming Control Board.
- Later legislative testimony identifies a concrete shared institutional feature: exemption from the ordinary Nevada Administrative Procedure Act framework under NRS chapter 233B.

### Required crosswalk in the article

| Dimension | Cannabis evidence to show | Comparison limit |
|---|---|---|
| Board structure | Membership, appointment, expertise, terms | Do not imply identical qualifications or powers. |
| Suitability | Cannabis licensing and ownership review provisions | Similar terminology does not make standards or outcomes identical. |
| Rulemaking/procedure | Statutory treatment, including NRS 233B relationship | Explain due-process and transparency consequences; do not celebrate exemption without scrutiny. |
| Enforcement | Investigation, discipline, conditional/final licensing | Compare actual authorities and appeal routes provision by provision. |
| Institutional purpose | Public-health/visitor protection and industry integrity statements | Cannabis is a biological consumer product and federally illegal; gaming is not. |

## Story 15 promotion ledger — When a Recall Has to Find the Product

**Bounded thesis:** Nevada's recall and public-warning record shows reverse traceability in action. When a problem is discovered after cultivation, testing, transfer, manufacture, or sale, regulators must translate linked regulatory records into holds, affected-package lists, retailer notices, label-based consumer instructions, and later amendments. The system can narrow a warning, but an amended list also demonstrates that traceability is investigative and revisable—not omniscient.

| ID | Material proposition | Primary authority / locator | Status | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15.1 | Nevada licensees must report establishment data through Metrc, the state's seed-to-sale compliance system. | CCB Guidance, Metrc overview. | V | This proves required reporting, not completeness or accuracy of every record. |
| 15.2 | CCB uses Metrc administrative holds to prevent identified product from being sold or transferred. | CCB Solicitation of Input Minutes, 31 Jan. 2024. | V | Document the trigger, scope, release/correction process, and human notifications. |
| 15.3 | A 2023 bulletin identified nearly 8,000 pounds treated with unapproved ethephon and later expanded the affected-product list. | CCB Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2023-01 and update. | V | Ethephon was not among analytes Nevada labs were required or configured to detect, according to the bulletin. Avoid implying the original COAs tested for it. |
| 15.4 | The regulator connected affected material to downstream product types and sales facilities and directed consumers to use label information. | Same bulletin and linked product list. | V | Audit the spreadsheet fields and distinguish harvests, packages, manufactured derivatives, sales facilities, and sale dates. |
| 15.5 | A December 2025 amended bulletin removed eight packages after further investigation. | CCB Public Health and Safety Bulletin 2025-02 Amended. | V | The correction illustrates revision and narrowing; determine the underlying evidentiary reason before characterizing error. |
| 15.6 | Traceability guarantees that every affected consumer is found or every affected unit recovered. | No authority supports this. | O | Investigate sales after transfer, consumer reach, opened/consumed product, record errors, destroyed inventory, and notice effectiveness. |

### Why this story displaces Story 13

| Criterion | Desert cultivation | Recall/traceability |
|---|---:|---:|
| Nevada-specific primary evidence | Weak | Strong |
| Distinct mechanism | Moderate | Strong |
| Technology visible in operation | Mostly inferred | Directly documented |
| Human stakes | General resource burden | Product exposure and consumer warning |
| Visual evidence | Facility access dependent | Bulletins, package lists, labels, holds, timelines |
| Reproducibility | Poor without private data | Strong from dated public records |

### Promotion gate still open

Before drafting, build one end-to-end case map showing discovery, affected source material, descendant packages/products, administrative holds, retailer notification, consumer identification, destruction/release, amendments, and enforcement outcome. Review is required from a traceability operator, laboratory/analytical specialist, and recall or public-health expert.
