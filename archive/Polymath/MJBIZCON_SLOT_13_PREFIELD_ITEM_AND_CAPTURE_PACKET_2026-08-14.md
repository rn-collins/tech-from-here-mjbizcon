## Ten-frame capture sequence

1. **Receipt:** item, retailer and date visible; cover payment details and unrelated purchases.
2. **Sealed front:** package upright, entire edge visible.
3. **Sealed back:** all warnings and printed copy readable.
4. **Compliance label:** tight, glare-free photograph of producer, license and batch/package identifiers.
5. **Closure:** how the unopened package closes; do not demonstrate with force if painful.
6. **All servings:** opened package with every serving visible and countable.
7. **Marks up:** all servings on a plain surface, mark facing camera.
8. **Typical mark:** macro photograph of a representative serving beside a small ruler.
9. **Weakest mark:** macro photograph of the least legible, incomplete or ambiguous serving, even if all appear acceptable.
10. **Reclosed package:** closure after one ordinary reopening, without making a child-resistance claim.

If the product uses individual marked wrappers instead of marks on the food, photograph every wrapper front/back and stop. That is evidence of the exception route, not a failed assignment.

## Photography settings and access controls

- Use a matte white, gray or black background—whichever creates the strongest contrast with the product.
- Use diffuse daylight or one soft lamp; no colored hotel lighting.
- Photograph in the phone's standard color mode; do not apply filters or portrait blur.
- Include at least one high-resolution horizontal frame suitable for Beehiiv and one vertical frame suitable for LinkedIn.
- Use a phone stand or stable surface; RN should not have to hold the phone and manipulate the package simultaneously.
- Ask a companion to open the child-resistant package if hand strength or pain makes this difficult; document who opened it only in the private production log.
- Do not photograph faces, customer information, employee badges or other patrons without permission.
- RN owns her photographs; record creation date, device and any edits. Preserve originals.

## Questions prepared for producer—do not send without RN approval

1. Who is the current Nevada licensee and facility producing this exact SKU?
2. Is the symbol molded, stamped, printed or otherwise applied, and at what production step?
3. What equipment or tool creates it? Is the tool Nevada-specific?
4. How do cavity geometry, fill volume and symbol relief interact? What does the mold not control about dose?
5. Who checks symbol presence and legibility, and at what sampling frequency?
6. What defects cause rework, rejection or destruction?
7. What changed when Nevada adopted or redesigned the symbol?
8. Does the same recipe use different molds or wrappers in other states?
9. Has this SKU received a stamp/mold exemption or alternative-demarcation approval?
10. May RN observe or photograph the mold, depositor, demolding and QA steps, and under what rights terms?

## Questions prepared for CCB—do not send without RN approval

1. Does the Board maintain item-level packaging approvals separately from Metrc catalog records?
2. For the captured product, what final approval establishes the symbol and packaging route?
3. Has the product or producer requested an exemption from stamping or molding?
4. How does the Board assess mark legibility and the boundary between permitted adjacent milligram text and impermissible modification of the symbol?
5. Does equipment/menu-change review reach the mold or marking equipment itself?
6. What inspection records document symbol presence, legibility or corrective action?
7. What non-confidential portions can be released with product-submitted photographs?
8. What changed in the 2021 amendment that moved impractical products to individually marked wrappers and a prescribed exemption request?
