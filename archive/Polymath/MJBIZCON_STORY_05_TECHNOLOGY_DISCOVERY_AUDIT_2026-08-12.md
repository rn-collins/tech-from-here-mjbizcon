# MJBizCon Story 5 — Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Prior working title:** *Twelve Governments, Twelve Agreements*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY ONLY AS A FUNDAMENTAL TECHNOLOGY-CENTERED REDESIGN TERRITORY. THE CURRENT COUNT-BASED PROPOSITION AND TITLE FAIL. DRAFTING REMAINS BLOCKED.**

## Why the existing story cannot proceed unchanged

The Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board's live Industry page now links **thirteen**, not twelve, tribal-state cannabis compacts: the twelve previously inventoried documents plus a Yerington Paiute Tribe compact. A permanent title based on a document count is therefore already obsolete. The new link is not a clerical footnote: the 62-page Yerington file contains compact terms and an attached tribal code, and it must be added to the page-image, operative-status, amendment, and nation-review matrix before any cross-compact conclusion is canonical.

The agreements are also not, by themselves, the technology. They are legal and governance protocols that prescribe interfaces between separately sovereign systems. The viable technological subject is the cross-jurisdictional interoperability work required when cannabis, its identifiers, its test evidence, its delivery record, and information about it cross a sovereignty boundary.

## Strong proposition to test

**When Cannabis Crosses a Sovereignty Boundary, Its Data Has to Cross Too.**

Alternate: **Cannabis Can Cross a Sovereignty Boundary Only Through an Interface Between Governments.**

Both remain hypotheses until implementation evidence establishes what systems actually exchange, who enters and reconciles the data, which records remain within tribal control, what fails, and how discrepancies are resolved. “Interoperability is not the same as surrendering sovereignty” remains a useful bounded line, not a substitute for nation-specific evidence.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The relevant place is not generic Nevada or generic “tribal land.” It is each named nation's jurisdiction and the precise boundary at which a product or record moves between a tribal enterprise or government and a Nevada-licensed establishment. The physical and informational crossing may involve delivery verification, an invoice or manifest, a tracking-system entry, testing evidence, packaging and labeling, and inspection or audit access. Each nation must be treated separately; compact language cannot establish community experience, benefit, harm, consensus, or present operations.

### 2. Institution + inventions

The institutional field includes each tribal government and its designated regulators or enterprises, the Governor/authorized state officials, the Cannabis Compliance Board and its predecessors, testing laboratories, transport and retail workers, auditors, and system vendors. The “invention” is not a single device. It is a negotiated interface architecture: rules for traceability, delivery confirmation, testing equivalence, packaging/labeling, notice, fiscal review, inspection/cooperation, dispute resolution, termination, and limits on jurisdiction and data disclosure.

### 3. Period + products/processes

The statutory authority began in 2017 and the documents must be dated, amended, superseded, and verified individually. The Yerington compact, executed in 2017, provides tangible examples: delivery verification and invoice execution; entry of delivered purchases into the state tracking system within 24 hours; recording certain intertribal receipts or deliveries in either the tribal or state tracking system; state-compliant packaging, testing, and labeling for sales to state licensees; and use of the state traceability system for transactions with state licensees. OCR located these provisions, but quotation and final interpretation require page-image verification.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The present system includes distinct tribal regulatory regimes, Nevada's state licensing and traceability environment, laboratories, packaging/labeling standards, transport records, audits and inspections, and statutory rules governing information. NRS 223.250 authorizes cross-jurisdictional agreements and now protects specified tribal-government information from public-record disclosure when the cannabis is not sold or transferred to a state cannabis establishment. That is a material data-sovereignty and confidentiality dimension. The CCB separately states that tribal establishments are not CCB-licensed and do not appear on its state license list. These facts support a story about interfaces between systems—not absorption into one state system.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** cross-jurisdictional cannabis interoperability across a sovereignty boundary: product identity, traceability, transaction and delivery records, test evidence, packaging/labeling, transport, notice, audit/inspection, and protected information.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process, or breakthrough:** a tracked package and its identifier; executed delivery invoice or manifest; 24-hour system entry; laboratory result; compliant package and label; audit record; and the state/tribal traceability entries used at a transaction boundary. The final article needs one fully verified end-to-end example, not a merely illustrative list.
3. **People and labor:** tribal lawmakers and regulators; tribal enterprise cultivation, processing, transport, inventory, compliance, retail, and IT workers; state regulators; laboratory staff; drivers; auditors; attorneys and negotiators; and the workers who enter, verify, reconcile, correct, and disclose records. Nation-specific consent and review govern representation.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada's 2017 compacting authority and each agreement's relationship to a named tribal government and its jurisdiction. “Nevada tribes” is never an adequate substitute for naming the relevant nation and verifying its own account.
5. **Present-day consequence or continuation:** the current CCB page links thirteen compacts; state law continues to authorize coordination while recognizing information ownership/confidentiality; cross-boundary commerce, if currently operative, still requires legal and technical handoffs. Current operations, systems, amendments, and results remain unverified.
6. **Tempting but merely concurrent or unsupported connections:** statewide Metrc use does not prove every tribal system is technically derived from or integrated with Metrc; compact clauses do not prove successful implementation; shared clauses do not make nations, systems, or outcomes equivalent; state-facing traceability does not equal surrender of sovereignty; a CCB web link does not alone prove operative status; legalization, tourism, gaming, or general economic development occurring in the same period is not a demonstrated cause or consequence of the interfaces.

## Distinction from other Canonical 14 stories

Story 4 concerns the state-regulated item's human-maintained data double inside Nevada's traceability regime. Story 5 concerns the interface and governance boundary between distinct sovereign regimes. Story 6 concerns what the public Item Catalog exposes or withholds. Story 12 concerns production of a laboratory result. Story 15 concerns reverse traceability during a recall. Story 5 may refer to those systems only to show a verified boundary handoff; it must not consume their mechanisms.

## Package consequences

### Video pathway

Investigate official material from each relevant nation first, then state hearings, compact signings, regulator explanations, and authorized enterprise or technical demonstrations. Every candidate must be watched and logged by creator, source, date, exact timecodes, caption/transcript quality, depicted nation/place/system, rights status, and lawful in-feed route. Reject generic cannabis B-roll, pan-Indigenous framing, unverified promotional claims, and any video that treats sovereignty as a loophole. If no exceptional, rights-usable, nation-specific video survives, document every candidate and reject video as the primary format.

### Real-image carousel pathway

No decorative text-card carousel. Build only after nation-specific review and item-level rights clearance. Candidate evidence pages are: (1) a verified compact signature/title page; (2) the exact page prescribing a traceability or delivery handoff; (3) an exact testing/packaging/labeling page; (4) an exact sovereignty/jurisdiction page; (5) the current official CCB inventory showing the added Yerington link; (6) the exact NRS 223.250 confidentiality language; and (7) a permissioned present-day photograph of the physical operation or handoff. Each page requires the actual selected image, provenance, reuse basis, caption, credit, and alt text. Compact pages are legal evidence, not cultural authority to narrate a nation's history.

### RN location-photography pathway

**Primary assignment, permission-dependent:** a prearranged exterior/site-context photograph at the Las Vegas Paiute Tribe's cannabis enterprise, chosen only after the Tribe identifies the appropriate location, viewpoint, boundaries, terminology, and whether photography is welcome. Photograph a genuine operational or jurisdictional interface—such as a permitted exterior receiving/retail context—without customers, workers, license plates, security arrangements, restricted areas, cultural material, or tribal seals unless expressly authorized. Best light: early morning or late afternoon; allow extra time for check-in and written conditions.

**Backup:** a permissioned, non-sensitive photograph of a Nevada Cannabis Compliance Board location or a public state record showing the compacting interface. Do not substitute an unrelated Las Vegas landmark. No field capture proceeds without access confirmation; public-right-of-way legality is not a substitute for cultural and relationship-based permission.

### Best single-image decision

Provisional best archival image: the page-image-verified clause from one nation-specific compact that most clearly shows the tracked handoff, paired with a caption that identifies only that agreement and date. It cannot stand for all nations. Preferred contemporary lead, if authorized and materially stronger, is the nation-approved RN photograph of the actual place where the interface operates. Rights and nation review control.

### Audio edition

Default to RN's verified AI voice after the final article is approved, using: **“Listen to an AI-narrated edition in RN’s voice.”** Prepare a listening-adapted script, spoken citations, pronunciation sheet for every nation, person, place, Indigenous word, statute, abbreviation, and technical term, filename/title/runtime/version date, near-opening Beehiiv Audio-block instruction, written-article transcript route, and playback/pronunciation/pacing/factual/mobile/accessibility QA. Flag all nation names and sovereignty-sensitive passages for RN and appropriate nation-specific review. Real or hybrid narration remains RN's choice. No generic voice, invented wording, music, misleading AI representation, or publication without authorization.
