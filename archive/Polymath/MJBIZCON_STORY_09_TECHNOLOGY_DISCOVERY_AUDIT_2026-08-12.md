# MJBizCon Story 9 — Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Prior working title:** *Social-Equity Lounge Scarcity Is Produced in Stages*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY ONLY AS A FUNDAMENTAL DATA-TO-DECISION REDESIGN. RETIRE THE CURRENT SCARCITY-PIPELINE PROPOSITION.**

## Controlling correction

The current story treats application, selection, prospective licensing, suitability, conditional licensing, final inspection, local approval and opening as stages that can produce scarcity. That is a useful institutional map, but it makes licensing outcomes—not technology—the subject.

The broad discovery pass reveals a stronger Nevada-specific technical story. Nevada converted residential history into a bright-line social-equity classification by linking an applicant's address to two external neighborhood datasets: the Neighborhood Atlas Area Deprivation Index and the Opportunity Atlas incarceration measure. A computerized random-number selector then chose among qualifying applications. In 2024, an internal audit found that Opportunity Atlas results had been miscalculated in six of the ten selected social-equity applications. Investigators rechecked the addresses relied upon by all thirty social-equity applicants, distinguished the data applicable during the 2022 licensing round from later ADI updates, removed six selections and reran computerized selection among eight eligible undrawn applicants.

That is not merely a licensing story. It is a documented classification–selection–audit–correction system in which an address becomes a proxy for historical disadvantage and a data error changes who advances.

## Provisional redesign proposition

**Nevada Turned an Address Into a Social-Equity Eligibility Test—and Had to Rerun the Result.**

Alternate: **When a Neighborhood Data Error Changed Who Advanced for a Nevada Cannabis License.**

The article must remain about how a public decision system translates people, addresses and historical conditions into eligibility—not a generalized verdict on whether social equity “worked.”

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The relevant place is not merely a lounge site. It is the chain from a person's former residential address to a census tract, from that tract to two national neighborhood datasets, and from those values to Nevada's cannabis-licensing process. Las Vegas is the administrative and market center: the CCB ran the selection at 700 East Warm Springs Road, many prospective entities identified Las Vegas or unincorporated Clark County locations, and the eventual operating lounge geography is highly concentrated. Yet eligibility addresses could be nationwide, so the article must not imply that the classification measured only Nevada neighborhoods.

The tangible geographic technology includes address records, census-tract boundaries, tract lookup/matching, the Neighborhood Atlas interface and ADI version, the Opportunity Atlas interface and incarceration measure, worksheets, screenshots or exports, eligibility calculations and the audit table. The field photograph cannot prove the data method; it must be paired with the actual data artifacts.

### 2. Institution + inventions

AB 341 required Nevada to define social-equity applicants and reserve at least ten of the first twenty independent-lounge licenses. The CCB and Cannabis Advisory Commission Social Equity Subcommittee operationalized “adversely affected” through ownership, residential history, neighborhood deprivation/incarceration measures and specified cannabis-conviction relationships.

The system combines several technologies and institutional processes:

- Accela application submission and document intake.
- Human collection and verification of residential histories and conviction-related evidence.
- Address-to-census-tract correspondence.
- Neighborhood Atlas ADI state deciles.
- Opportunity Atlas incarceration percentiles/values tied to people who grew up in the tract and were incarcerated at the 2010 Census.
- Bright-line threshold logic.
- Completeness/interest review before selection.
- Smartplay computerized random-number selection, overseen in 2022 by Henry and Horne.
- CCB investigative audit, re-performance and correction.
- Suitability investigation, conditional licensing, local approval, final inspection and opening after selection.

No single component is “the algorithm.” The article should call it a data-to-decision system unless the exact software, code and automation boundaries are recovered.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- **2021:** AB 341 creates the social-equity designation and reserved-license structure but leaves operational criteria to rulemaking.
- **2022 rulemaking:** Nevada adopts criteria including at least 51 percent ownership; five years' residence in a qualifying tract; ADI state-only decile of at least seven; Opportunity Atlas incarceration measure at or above the 90th percentile, described by CCB as roughly 3.3 percent; and applicable conviction/family criteria.
- **October 2022:** Applicants submit through Accela. The CCB initially reports 100 complete applications, then reports 99 after review: twenty retail, forty-nine independent non-social-equity and thirty independent social-equity.
- **30 November 2022:** Two digital drawings select prospective independent-lounge applicants; ten are drawn from the social-equity pool. Selection is not a license or an opening.
- **January–July 2024:** The CCB conducts an internal audit before any reserved social-equity license has been issued. It finds Opportunity Atlas miscalculations in six selected applications, requests the relied-upon addresses from all thirty applicants and identifies at least eight eligible undrawn applicants.
- **22 August 2024:** All eight opt into a new computerized selection; six receive prospective licenses. The CCB says it will refund the $2,500 fee to social-equity applicants not proceeding.
- **July 2026 endpoint:** the CCB reports three active lounge facilities statewide. That total includes lounge types and does not establish how many are social-equity, independent, selected, conditional or merely delayed.

The period therefore documents a system that generated a decision, discovered a correspondence/calculation failure and produced a corrected selection. It does not by itself explain every later non-opening.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The present ecosystem includes applicants and their families; addresses and residence records; census geography; researchers who built the Neighborhood Atlas and Opportunity Atlas; CCB application, licensing, investigative and technical staff; the Cannabis Advisory Commission; Accela; Smartplay; external auditors; lawyers and consultants; local land-use/licensing offices; capital providers; architects/contractors; and prospective lounge workers.

The system's public consequence is distributive: classification determines access to a lower application fee and a reserved selection pool; computerized selection determines who advances; later human and institutional stages determine whether advancement becomes an operating business. Its failure modes include wrong tract correspondence, wrong metric interpretation, transcription/calculation error, dataset-version drift, missing residence evidence, nonresponse, withdrawal, confidentiality limits, opaque software operation and public conflation of selection with licensure.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** Nevada's social-equity data-to-decision system: residential-address evidence → census geography → two neighborhood proxies → bright-line eligibility classification → computerized random selection → investigative audit and corrected selection.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** a real applicant address redacted/protected; census-tract lookup; the applicable Neighborhood Atlas ADI record; the applicable Opportunity Atlas incarceration value; the CCB social-equity worksheet; the 2022 selection record; the 2024 audit table; and the replacement selection output. The article must reconstruct at least one privacy-safe correspondence chain without exposing confidential applicant information.
3. **People and labor:** applicants gathering five years of residence and conviction evidence; family members whose histories may be relevant; CCB intake, licensing and investigative staff; CAC members; census/data researchers; geospatial/data workers; Smartplay operators; audit personnel; attorneys/consultants; local licensing and construction workers; and people trying to finance and open the businesses after selection.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada's 2021 lounge law created a reserved social-equity licensing route, and its regulator chose a particular combination of national neighborhood metrics to implement it. The 2022 Las Vegas selection and 2024 Nevada audit provide a documented local episode in public-sector data classification and correction.
5. **Present-day consequence or continuation:** six initially selected applications were removed after the audit and six replacement prospective licenses were issued. The same criteria remain presented on the CCB's current social-equity page. Current license/operating outcomes must be rebuilt entity by entity before publication.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** few open lounges do not prove that the eligibility metrics or random selector caused scarcity. Capital conditions, site acquisition, zoning, local licensing, suitability, HVAC/buildout, ownership disputes and market conditions occur later. Geographic deprivation and incarceration proxies do not prove an individual person's degree of harm. An eligible address does not cause a cannabis conviction, and a cannabis conviction does not make every address eligible. A later ADI update does not automatically prove the 2022 classification was wrong; the CCB expressly says it accounted for version change.

## Current story disposition

Retire *Social-Equity Lounge Scarcity Is Produced in Stages* as the canonical proposition. Preserve its stage map as a limitations section and outcome ledger. The redesigned article begins with the 2024 audit discovery, moves backward through address classification and 2022 selection, and then follows the correction forward. Later scarcity is the question the technical story refuses to overclaim, not its headline conclusion.

## Final gate

**THE CURRENT SCARCITY STORY FAILS. THE SLOT PASSES PROVISIONALLY AS A FUNDAMENTAL REDESIGN AROUND AN AUDITABLE DATA-TO-DECISION SYSTEM.**

The defensible story is not “an algorithm caused lounge scarcity.” It is that Nevada translated addresses and neighborhood proxies into a bright-line eligibility decision, discovered material calculation errors after computerized selection, audited the full pool and reran the decision. The article survives only if the team reconstructs that workflow, versions and correction without turning neighborhood proxies into individual truth or prospective selection into an operating license.
