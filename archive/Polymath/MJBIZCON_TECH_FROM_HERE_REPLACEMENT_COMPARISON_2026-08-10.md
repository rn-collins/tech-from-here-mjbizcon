## Hard gates applied

A contender advances only if it can support:

- a named technical object, process, infrastructure, instrument, or standard;
- inputs, transformations, outputs, operators, and failure modes;
- a Nevada-specific history, adaptation, constraint, or consequence;
- a complete canonical story whose technological mechanism drives the headline, opening, middle, and ending;
- a complete LinkedIn-native technological revelation;
- a saveable payload;
- a meaningful real image on every carousel slide, except for a justified supplemental explanatory diagram;
- an item-level rights, caption, credit, alt-text, and accessibility route.

## Seven replacement-capable finalists

The first six fill the guaranteed vacancies. The seventh is held because Story 5 has not proved a concrete interoperability technology.

| Priority | Provisional canonical promise | Standalone LinkedIn payoff | Saveable payload | Image-bearing carousel evidence sequence |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Edible homogeneity:** A legal dose begins as a manufacturing problem: the same batch must be mixed, sampled, measured, and corrected before “10 mg” can mean anything bite to bite. | A gummy does not become a 10-mg gummy because a label says so; Nevada requires a chain of production and measurement decisions. | “From recipe to reported dose” failure-and-correction map | real ingredients/mixing equipment; production-run record; sampling tool; lab instrument; COA; failed result; relabel/reformulate form; corrected package; final evidence-route slide |
| 2 | **Irradiation/remediation:** A product can be biologically changed after harvest so it passes a microbial threshold; Nevada's record makes the process, disclosure, and retesting boundary investigable. | “Passed testing” can describe the product after a machine changed its microbial load—not the untouched harvest. | Remediation disclosure questions for consumers and operators | real contaminated/affected material only if appropriate; remediation equipment; process/control panel; Nevada radiation-label language; package label; microbial result; remediation record; retest boundary; final checklist |
| 3 | **ID scanners:** An age-verification device translates an identity document into a yes/no gate, but the technical and ethical story is what it reads, computes, displays, stores, and shares. | The scanner at the door is not merely checking age; its design determines whether proof of age becomes a retained identity record. | Five-question scanner data-flow test | real acceptable IDs with data masked; scanner hardware; barcode/magnetic stripe; feature guidance; age-result screen; retention setting; privacy notice; failure/false-reject state; data-flow diagram; final test |

## Story 5 interoperability decision

Story 5 remains **borderline and uncounted**. Its twelve agreements prove coordinated legal interfaces, not necessarily a technical interoperability system.

It may stay only if the dossier documents, for at least a responsibly selected and reviewed subset:

- shared or translated identifiers;
- actual inventory/track-and-trace exchange;
- manifest or transport-data exchange across the jurisdictional boundary;
- laboratory-result or product-status exchange;
- access controls, retention, correction, outage, or dispute mechanisms;
- who operates each interface and what happens when records disagree;
- nation-specific review and Indigenous-law review;
- culturally appropriate real-image evidence beyond agreement pages.

If those mechanisms are not established, Story 5 leaves Tech From Here and the shelf-life finalist fills the slot. The agreements may remain in a sovereignty/law reporting lane.
