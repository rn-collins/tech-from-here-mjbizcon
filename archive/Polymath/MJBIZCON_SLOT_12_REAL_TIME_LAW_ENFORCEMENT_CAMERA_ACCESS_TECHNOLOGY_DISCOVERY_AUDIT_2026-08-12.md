# MJBizCon Slot 12 — Real-Time Law-Enforcement Camera Access Technology-Discovery Audit

**Audit date:** 12 August 2026  
**Provisional inherited title:** *A Nevada Cannabis Camera Must Be Available to Police in Real Time*  
**Decision:** **PASS PROVISIONALLY AS A DISTINCT, HIGH-INTEREST TECHNOLOGY, LABOR, PRIVACY AND GOVERNANCE STORY. REVISE THE TITLE AND BLOCK DRAFTING PENDING OPERATIONAL, HISTORICAL, SECURITY AND RIGHTS EVIDENCE.**

## Controlling correction

Nevada law does not establish that police continuously watch cannabis establishments. Current NRS 678B.510(6) requires every cannabis establishment to install a video-monitoring system that displays its interior and exterior and is capable of remote access by **a law-enforcement agency in real time upon request**. Current NAC 678B.220 adds a technical and operational stack: specified camera resolutions and frame rates, coverage zones, continuous recording, remote-access credentials in the establishment's Accela/electronic-licensing portal, low-light identification, at least 30 days of storage, on-demand copies, failure notification, battery backup, malfunction logs and repair/notification duties. Consumption lounges separately require security personnel to monitor real-time footage while open.

The phrase **upon request** is indispensable. Capability is not access; access is not continuous observation; a legal requirement is not proof of how often, why, by whom or under what local procedure the capability is used. “Police” is also narrower than the statutory phrase “law-enforcement agency.”

## Revised proposition

**Nevada Requires Every Cannabis Establishment to Build a Live Video Door for Law Enforcement.**

More legally literal alternate: **A Nevada Cannabis Camera Must Be Remotely Reachable by Law Enforcement—in Real Time, Upon Request.**

The first is more magnetic but may be used only if “door” is clearly identified as RN's bounded metaphor and the article immediately states that the record proves required capability, not continuous viewing. The second is safer for the dossier. Retire “available to police” unless the copy supplies the statutory actor and condition.

## Required broad discovery pass

### 1. Place + technology

The place is not generic CCTV culture. Nevada places a remotely accessible video layer around every licensed cannabis establishment—cultivation, production, testing, retail and lounge—while the Las Vegas concentration of establishments makes the system locally visible. The rule covers entrances, exits, vault areas, points of sale, limited-access areas, activity in or adjacent to the establishment, and, in lounges, the entire consumer area.

The story's physical geography therefore spans a licensed Las Vegas facility, the CCB's electronic-licensing and inspection system, and the relevant local law-enforcement agency. A building exterior alone cannot prove the live-access workflow. Any reporting inside a facility must avoid publishing camera positions, blind spots, network design, credentials, storage location, alarm routing, employee patterns, customer faces or other exploitable security information.

### 2. Institution + inventions

The regulated system is assembled from familiar inventions whose mandatory combination is unusual and consequential:

- IP or digital/analog video cameras and encoders;
- network video recorder, storage service or other on-demand recording system;
- 1920 × 1080 and 720 × 480 minimum recording classes at 15 frames per second;
- call-up monitor of at least 55 inches and still-image printer;
- low-light imaging, date/time generator and tamper-resistant mounting;
- remote-access endpoint and credentials recorded in Accela/the Board's licensing portal;
- at least 30 calendar days of recordings plus immediately available USB 3.0-or-better media sized for a prescribed seven-camera/seven-day export;
- audible and visual failure notification, five-minute battery backup, alternative coverage, malfunction log and repair workflow;
- intrusion detection, automatic breach notification and communication with law enforcement;
- human security monitoring in an open consumption lounge.

No single camera is the story. The central technology is a legally mandated **remote evidentiary-access system** joining image capture, network reachability, credential custody, retention/export, failure handling and human response.

### 3. Period + products/processes

- Nevada's medical-marijuana regime already contained remote real-time law-enforcement access language before the CCB existed; the recovered 2017 statute states the requirement for medical establishments.
- The 2019 cannabis recodification carried the requirement into what became NRS 678B.510. This chronology defeats any claim that the gaming-modeled CCB invented or imported the system in 2019.
- Later regulations increased technical specificity and added portal credentialing, larger call-up monitors, storage/export detail, failure controls and lounge-specific monitoring.
- As revised through April 2026, the statute still says “in real-time upon request,” while the regulations specify different actor/condition wording across camera classes. The statute controls the minimum claim; any apparent regulatory variation requires Nevada legal review rather than editorial harmonization.

The canonical process to reconstruct is: facility risk/coverage plan → installation and configuration → credential entry and custody → 24-hour capture/storage → request and authentication → live connection or recorded export → evidentiary use → access/failure logging, correction and oversight.

### 4. Infrastructure + present-day ecosystem

The current ecosystem includes licensees, owners, security directors, guards, budtenders and lounge personnel; camera/network installers; managed-video and alarm vendors; IT and cybersecurity workers; Accela/CCB administrators; Board agents and inspectors; local and other law-enforcement agencies; attorneys, insurers and auditors; and workers, customers, visitors and neighbors whose images can enter the record.

The system's present consequences are both operational and civic. It makes regulated premises technically reachable during an incident, produces retained evidence and gives regulators measurable specifications to inspect. It also creates credential, cybersecurity, privacy, misuse, overcollection, access-governance and failure risks. The public record does not yet establish request frequency, connection success, audit logging, permitted purposes, warrant/consent doctrine, agency retention after access, third-party disclosure, breach history, effectiveness or disparate impact.

## Six-field technology spine

1. **Central technology or technological system:** a mandatory remote evidentiary-access system connecting continuous image capture, real-time law-enforcement reachability upon request, portal-held access information, retention/export, alarms, failure controls and human response.
2. **Tangible technology, object, process or breakthrough:** one security-safe, permissioned reconstruction of a request path using a decommissioned/demo camera, recorder, access endpoint, credential handoff record, timestamped footage/export and malfunction/access log. Never publish live credentials or operational topology.
3. **People and labor:** facility security and compliance staff; lounge monitors; camera/network installers; IT/cybersecurity and storage vendors; CCB portal administrators, inspectors and agents; law-enforcement requesters and evidence personnel; attorneys/auditors; and the workers, customers and visitors who are recorded.
4. **Place-specific historical connection:** Nevada imposed remote real-time law-enforcement access in its pre-CCB medical-cannabis regime and retained it in the statewide cannabis system; Las Vegas supplies the dense, regulated facility and agency setting in which the interface operates.
5. **Present-day consequence or continuation:** every current Nevada cannabis establishment must maintain the capability, while the current regulation makes it an inspectable stack of coverage, resolution, credentials, storage, export, uptime and correction requirements. Actual use and outcome remain unproved.
6. **Tempting concurrent or noncausal connections:** casino surveillance did not necessarily cause cannabis-camera design; cameras do not prove safety; remote capability does not prove continuous police viewing; “upon request” does not by itself define request procedure or legal authority; portal credentials do not prove successful access; recording does not prove review; real-time access is not facial recognition, automated analytics or direct database integration; more pixels do not prove better evidence.

## Comparative-selection judgment

Slot 12 survives. It is more immediate and culturally legible than the earlier gaming-versus-cannabis institutional comparison because it has a surprising rule, a tangible technical stack, people on both sides of the lens, a precise Nevada lineage and unresolved questions with real public stakes. Its appeal is the contradiction between a seemingly simple camera mandate and the distributed labor/governance necessary to make remote evidence trustworthy.

The story is not yet draftable. It must recover one security-safe real workflow and the actual access-governance practice. If no facility, agency or CCB evidence can establish practice beyond the published rule, publishable scope must contract to **how Nevada designed the capability**, with the absence of use data stated as a limitation.
