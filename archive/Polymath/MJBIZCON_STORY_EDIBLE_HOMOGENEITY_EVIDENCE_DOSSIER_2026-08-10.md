## Editorial decision at this checkpoint

This candidate survives technology centrality and Nevada specificity. Its real subject is not “cannabis rules” or a gummy with a label. It is a coupled manufacturing-and-measurement system:

1. a production facility chooses a recipe and process intended to distribute THC across units or servings;
2. a laboratory samples and measures multiple units from a production run;
3. Nevada compares the results with an intended concentration and distribution rule;
4. a failure enters Metrc and the certificate of analysis (CoA); and
5. the run may be relabeled at tested potency, repurposed/reformulated, retested through an approved route, or destroyed.

Nevada is not shown by the present record to have invented edible homogeneity testing, HPLC, or dose-uniformity standards. Its specific contribution is the public administrative correction loop that turns unequal measured units into a regulated data state and then permits bounded changes to the run, its label, its CoA, and its Metrc status.

The dossier does **not** yet lock the story into the corrected fourteen. The evidence route is strong, but the carousel lacks a rights-cleared Nevada-specific lead photograph and enough operator/process images to satisfy RN's real-image standard without generic decoration.

## 1. Revelation and falsifiable version

### One-sentence revelation

In Nevada, “10 mg” is not merely label copy: it is the output of a manufacturing, sampling, laboratory, database, and correction system that can formally change what a failed production run is allowed to become.

### Falsifiable version

The story fails or must be materially narrowed if current operative Nevada law no longer requires the described homogeneity verification; if the 2021 relabel/remediation guidance has been withdrawn or superseded; if a failed run cannot presently follow the stated correction routes; or if specialist review shows that the article conflates label accuracy, within-run homogeneity, analytical accuracy, and per-serving legal limits.

## 5. People, operators, institutions, owners, maintainers, and affected groups

### Documented system roles

- **Cannabis production facility:** chooses and documents recipe/process; produces the run; initiates a study or post-failure request; relabels, reformulates/repurposes, retests, or destroys only through an allowed route.
- **Cannabis independent testing laboratory:** collects/tests required samples; operates analytical methods and controls; reports results in Metrc and on the CoA; retains records; may amend the CoA and Metrc status after approval.
- **Cannabis Compliance Board and appropriate Board agents:** preapprove product production; prescribe forms/processes; approve post-failure requests; inspect and audit records.

- **Consumers and patients:** rely on a printed concentration/serving claim but cannot see the full within-run distribution from the label alone.

## 6. Technical mechanism

### Expert-level explanation

Homogeneity is distinct from mean potency and label accuracy. A production run can have an average concentration close to its intended value while individual units or servings vary materially. Nevada's current codified rule directs an independent laboratory to test multiple samples from one production run, subject to a reduced verification route for a product whose homogeneity was previously verified and whose recipe has not changed. The presently displayed pass conditions compare each sample's THC concentration and weight with intended values using a ±15% band and include a distribution constraint preventing a small fraction of product from containing a disproportionate share of total THC.

The analytical result depends on more than the instrument: sampling design, unit definition, sample preparation, extraction recovery, matrix effects, calibration, quality controls, calculation, and reporting all matter. HPLC is a plausible and common cannabinoid-quantification platform, but the Nevada documents reviewed here do not establish that every laboratory uses the same instrument or method for every edible matrix. The article must not turn “laboratory test” into “an HPLC machine decides.”

After a failed homogeneity verification, the 2021 guidance requires the laboratory to record a failing state in Metrc and on the CoA. The production facility then selects an authorized request path. For relabeling, the facility seeks permission to sell the run at tested potency; after approval, the original laboratory amends Metrc and the CoA to passing, inserts prescribed language, and issues the amended report. Packaging that conflicts with tested potency must be corrected. Other pathways can physically change or eliminate the run and require their own permissions and subsequent records.

### General-reader explanation

A batch can be “right on average” and still be wrong piece by piece. Nevada does not ask only how much THC the whole run seems to contain. It samples more than one unit and asks whether the concentration is spread closely enough to the intended value. If the run fails, the failure becomes a data event. The producer cannot simply print a new number: it must enter one of Nevada's correction routes, and the laboratory records must change with the approved outcome.

### Inputs → transformations → outputs

| Stage | Inputs | Transformation | Output | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Formulation | cannabinoid ingredient, food matrix, recipe, target concentration | weighing, heating/cooling, mixing/emulsification, depositing, portioning | production run | segregation, poor mixing, ingredient variability, timing/temperature deviation |
| Sampling | run identity, unit/serving definition, sample plan | selection of multiple units/servings | laboratory samples + chain of custody | nonrepresentative selection, wrong unit identity, handling error |
| Analysis | prepared extracts, standards, method, instrument controls | separation/detection/quantification and calculation | per-sample concentrations/weights | matrix effect, extraction loss, calibration/QC failure, transcription error |
| Regulatory comparison | intended values, results, current criteria | tolerance and distribution checks | verified or failed homogeneity | wrong rule version, formula error, incomplete result set |
| Data/reporting | result, run identifiers, CoA fields | entry into Metrc and CoA | regulated pass/fail state | mismatched identifiers, delayed/incorrect entry, version ambiguity |
| Correction | failed state + producer request | approval, physical or label change, retest or destruction | amended status, changed run use, or disposal | unauthorized change, consumer-confusing amendment, records disagree |

## 8. Competing interpretations

1. **Consumer-protection interpretation:** multiple-sample verification and a correction trail make a printed potency claim more meaningful.
2. **Manufacturing-control interpretation:** homogeneity failure is process feedback, not merely a regulatory violation.
3. **Administrative-salvage interpretation:** approved relabeling/reformulation can prevent destruction of a safe but differently potent run, preserving value while updating the claim.
4. **Transparency critique:** an amended “passing” status can compress a more complicated history; the consumer-facing visibility of the original failure and amendment requires reporting.
5. **Sampling critique:** passing sampled units does not prove every unit in the run. The strength of the inference depends on sampling design and process stability.
6. **Measurement critique:** method and matrix uncertainty can be confused with production nonuniformity if the story treats the assay as infallible.

The canonical article must hold these interpretations together rather than selecting a triumphalist “Nevada solved dosing” frame.

## 10. What would make this wrong? — red team

- The special failed-homogeneity guidance has been withdrawn or superseded.
- Current CCB practice differs materially from its posted 2021 document.
- The article uses 10 mg as a universal target instead of the adult-use single-serving ceiling.
- It implies that every bite is tested or guaranteed.
- It treats an average-potency problem as a distribution problem, or vice versa.
- It assumes HPLC without facility-specific method evidence.
- It says a failed run “becomes passing” without explaining the approval, amendment, physical/label consequence, and consumer-facing record.
- It implies relabeling cures physical nonuniformity. Relabeling changes the asserted potency of the run; whether it makes each unit acceptably uniform depends on the verified criteria and approval basis.
- It presents a CCB form or status field as proof that practice is consistent across facilities.
- It erases medical consumers, workers, analytical uncertainty, or financial incentives.
- It uses proposed R152-24 language as current law.
