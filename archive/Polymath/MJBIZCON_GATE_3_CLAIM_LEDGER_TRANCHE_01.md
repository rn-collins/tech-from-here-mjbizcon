## Evidentiary rules

- `V` = verified by the located primary/official source.
- `Q` = verified only with the stated qualification.
- `O` = open; do not draft as fact.
- `R` = review gate remains.
- A regulator's statement proves what the regulator said or did, not that the system worked as intended.
- Every legal proposition must be rechecked against the current constitution, statutes, regulations, and later authority immediately before publication.

## Story 1 — Nevada Put Medical Cannabis in Its Constitution

**Bounded thesis:** Nevada voters approved a constitutional amendment requiring the Legislature to provide by law for specified medical use of cannabis. The measure passed in two successive general elections before becoming Article 4, section 38.

| ID | Material proposition | Exact authority / locator | Status | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | The current Nevada Constitution contains a medical-cannabis provision. | Nevada Constitution, art. 4, § 38(1), official online constitution. | V | Say “requires the Legislature to provide by law”; do not describe the text as an unrestricted individual right. |
| 1.2 | The provision concerns use on physician advice for enumerated or law-approved conditions. | Nev. Const. art. 4, § 38(1)(a). | V | Preserve the text’s medical and legislative conditions. |
| 1.3 | Question 9 appeared in the 2000 statewide ballot materials as a constitutional amendment. | Nevada Legislative Counsel Bureau, *2000 Ballot Questions*, Question 9, full text of measure. | V | Historical election mechanics and effective date must be described from official records. |
| 1.4 | Nevada required voter approval twice because the initiative amended the constitution. | 1998 and 2000 official ballot/election records; legislative materials discussing Question 9. | Q | Retrieve the 1998 pamphlet/result and quote the governing amendment procedure before drafting the chronology. |
| 1.5 | Constitutionalization guaranteed practical access to lawful retail supply. | No authority supports this. | O | The story must distinguish legal/constitutional command from supply infrastructure and patient experience. |

**Primary links:** https://www.leg.state.nv.us/const/nvconst.html ; https://www.leg.state.nv.us/Division/Research/VoteNV/BallotQuestions/2000.pdf

**Draft gate:** Conditional. Retrieve the official 1998 ballot record and implementing-law chronology before article drafting.

**Bounded thesis:** Nevada law authorizes government-to-government cannabis agreements, and executed compacts coordinate defined state–tribal interfaces. The story is a clause-level comparison, not a generalized narrative about “tribal cannabis.”

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Nevada authorizes the Governor or designee to enter cannabis agreements with tribal governments. | NRS 223.250, as identified by the CCB Tribal Partnerships page. | V | Quote/check current statutory text before publication. |
| 5.2 | Executed compacts exist with multiple distinct tribal governments. | CCB-hosted executed compacts: Ely Shoshone Tribe; Pyramid Lake Paiute Tribe; Fallon Paiute-Shoshone Tribe; Fort McDermitt Paiute and Shoshone Tribe; Moapa Band of Paiute Indians; Winnemucca Indian Colony. | V | Each nation must be named exactly; do not collapse nations into one category. |

## Story 6 — The Public Item Catalog Is a Dated Window, Not Total Transparency

**Bounded thesis:** Nevada exposes regulated cannabis item/testing information through a public catalog, but the interface must be audited as a time-bounded access tool rather than treated as a complete historical database or a substitute for scientific interpretation.

| ID | Material proposition | Exact authority / locator | Status | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | The CCB publicly links a Nevada Cannabis Metrc Item Catalog. | CCB homepage and guidance/consumer navigation. | V | Record access date and capture the interface state. |
| 6.2 | The catalog can expose product-level laboratory documentation/COAs. | CCB Item Catalog interface and downloadable records, tested item by item. | Q | Verify a reproducible sample; do not infer universal coverage from interface design. |
| 6.3 | Display is limited to items sold within the preceding six months. | CCB catalog notice/usage language previously identified at Gate 2. | Q | Re-capture the exact notice and date because the interface is dynamic. |
| 6.4 | The catalog is a complete historical record of every lawful Nevada product and test. | Unsupported and contradicted by the six-month limit. | O | Never use “complete market transparency.” |
| 6.5 | A consumer can correctly interpret potency, uncertainty, limits of quantitation, pass/fail status, and contaminant results merely because a COA is downloadable. | Unsupported. | O | Scientific-literacy and usability testing are part of the story. |

**Primary link:** https://ccb.nv.gov/cannabis-item-catalog/

**Draft gate:** Conditional. Complete a dated, reproducible sample audit across product types, laboratories, mobile/desktop access, missing records, and COA terminology.

## Story 7 — Borrowed Architecture, Different Machine

| ID | Material proposition | Exact authority / locator | Status | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Story 9 — When Random Selection Met Post-Selection Suitability

**Bounded thesis:** Nevada used a computerized random-number selector to choose ten social-equity lounge applications to proceed, then a later internal audit found six selected applicants ineligible. The mechanism is a collision among scarce licenses, eligibility rules, random selection, and subsequent verification—not a clean “license algorithm.”

| ID | Material proposition | Exact authority / locator | Status | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9.1 | Thirty social-equity applications were in the pool and a computerized random-number selector drew ten on Nov. 30, 2022. | CCB, July 2, 2024 internal-audit release, first page. | V | Describe selection as permission to proceed, not final licensure. |
| 9.2 | An internal audit begun in January 2024 found six selected applicants ineligible. | Same CCB release. | V | Attribute the finding to the CCB internal audit; retrieve full supporting record if publicly available. |
| 9.3 | None of the ten reserved licenses had been issued when the audit began. | Same CCB release. | V | This is a point-in-time fact. |
| 9.4 | The Board conditionally approved its first social-equity lounge applicant in May 2025. | CCB May 15, 2025 meeting minutes, agenda item A. | V | Conditional approval still required local approvals and final CCB inspection before opening. |
| 9.5 | Random selection caused the eligibility failures. | Not established. | O | Separate upstream verification, applicant representations, randomization, audit, cure/appeal, and final suitability. |
| 9.6 | The process achieved or failed social equity in lived outcomes. | Not established by licensing records alone. | O | Requires ownership/control, financing, opening, survival, employment/community, and affected-applicant evidence. |

**Primary links:** https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/July-2-CCB-Completes-Internal-Audit-of-Licenses-Reserved-for-Social-Equity-FOR-RELEASE-1.pdf ; https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/05.15.25-Meeting-Minutes.pdf ; https://ccb.nv.gov/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/AB341_EN.pdf

**Draft gate:** Conditional. Retrieve selector protocol/code description, eligibility criteria in force, audit methodology, applicant disposition/cure/appeal outcomes, and current license/opening status.
