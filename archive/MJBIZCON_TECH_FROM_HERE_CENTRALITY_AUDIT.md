## Controlling decision

Every canonical article and its independently authored LinkedIn post must be unmistakably about technology. Law, regulation, cannabis, tourism, equity, institutions, or culture may explain what technology does and who it affects, but none can substitute for a technological subject.

A story passes only when a cold reader can identify all of the following without editorial explanation:

1. A named technological object, process, platform, infrastructure, scientific technique, or technical standard.
2. A mechanism: inputs, transformations, outputs, operators, and failure modes.
3. A Nevada-specific history, adaptation, deployment, constraint, or consequence.
4. People and institutions that design, operate, maintain, finance, govern, experience, or resist the technology.
5. A material consequence produced by the interaction of the technology with culture, law, labor, land, power, or ownership.
6. A headline, opening, narrative spine, and ending whose subject is the technology.
7. A LinkedIn post with a standalone technological insight and a saveable technical payload.
8. A carousel and real-image plan that prove, orient, compare, or explain the technology.

Using words such as “system,” “infrastructure,” “architecture,” “data,” “innovation,” or “technology” does not satisfy the test. “Governance technology” is not an automatic rescue category.
